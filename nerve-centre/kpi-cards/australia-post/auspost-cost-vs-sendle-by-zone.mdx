## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> A zone-by-zone price comparison of what you actually paid Australia Post against what the same parcel would have cost on Sendle. Sendle's flat-rate-by-zone model can undercut AusPost on certain metro-to-metro lanes, so this card surfaces every zone where you are over-paying and quantifies the gap. It is a carrier-mix optimisation tool, not a service-quality tool.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it compares** | Per-zone average paid charge from your AusPost shipment manifest against Sendle's published flat rate for an equivalent parcel size and zone pairing. Each of the nine AusPost zones (5 metro, 4 regional) gets a row. |
| **Data source** | AusPost charged cost comes from the Shipping & Tracking API manifest / `POST /shipping/v1/prices/items` (or the per-article charge on your AusPost Business credit-account invoice). Sendle comparison rates come from the Sendle quote API keyed on origin and destination postcode plus cubic weight. The card joins on zone pair and parcel size band. |
| **Zone model** | AusPost prices by zone: 5 metropolitan zones plus 4 regional / remote zones. Sendle prices by a coarser metro vs regional vs remote band with national flat rates. The card normalises both to AusPost's nine-zone grid so the comparison is like-for-like by lane. |
| **What "cost" means** | Net charged price including fuel and per-article surcharges, excluding optional cover / extra insurance and Signature on Delivery, so the comparison reflects the base carriage you could substitute. GST is shown ex-GST to match invoice line items. |
| **Time window** | `30D vsP` (rolling 30 days, compared with the prior 30 days). Picked so the annual October AusPost price review and Sendle's own rate changes show up as a step in the comparison. |
| **Alert trigger** | Any zone where AusPost is more than 15 percent above the Sendle equivalent for the same lane and size band. One zone tripping is enough to fire. |
| **Roles** | owner, operations, finance |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:calc */}
For each AusPost zone the card takes every despatched article in the 30-day window, reads its charged price from the AusPost manifest (or reconciled invoice line), and averages by zone and parcel size band. It then requests the Sendle flat rate for the same origin-to-destination zone pairing at the matching cubic-weight band and computes the delta:

`zone_gap_pct = (auspost_avg_charged - sendle_equivalent_rate) / sendle_equivalent_rate * 100`

A positive `zone_gap_pct` means AusPost is dearer on that lane; negative means AusPost is cheaper. The card renders one row per zone, sorted by gap descending, with the count of parcels behind each row so a 40 percent gap on 3 parcels is not mistaken for a 40 percent gap on 3,000. The `>15%` alert evaluates per row, not on the blended average, because the optimisation opportunity is lane-specific: you do not switch your whole carrier mix, you re-route the zones where AusPost loses.

Cubic weight matters: AusPost and Sendle both charge on the greater of dead weight and cubic weight (length x width x height x the carrier's cubic conversion factor). The card carries the cubic-weight band through so a bulky-but-light parcel is compared at its cubed rate on both sides, not its scale weight.
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:calc */}

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A Melbourne DTC homewares brand, around 5,800 parcels a month, AusPost-only on a Business credit account, considering adding Sendle for the lanes where it makes sense. Reading taken at 09:00 AEST on 14 Apr 26 for the trailing 30 days (15 Mar 26 to 13 Apr 26), parcels in the 0.5kg to 2kg band.

| Zone (origin to destination) | Parcels (30D) | AusPost avg charged | Sendle equivalent | Gap % | Alert |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VIC metro to VIC metro | 1,920 | $9.45 | $7.95 | +18.9% | Tripped |
| VIC metro to NSW metro | 1,340 | $11.20 | $9.80 | +14.3% | Clear |
| VIC metro to QLD metro | 880 | $12.10 | $9.80 | +23.5% | Tripped |
| VIC metro to SA metro | 410 | $10.60 | $9.80 | +8.2% | Clear |
| VIC metro to WA metro | 360 | $14.80 | $13.50 | +9.6% | Clear |
| VIC metro to regional VIC | 540 | $11.90 | $12.40 | -4.0% | Clear |
| VIC metro to regional NSW/QLD | 290 | $15.20 | $16.10 | -5.6% | Clear |
| VIC metro to regional WA/NT | 60 | $19.40 | $22.80 | -14.9% | Clear |

The card flags two zones over the 15 percent line: metro-to-metro within Victoria (+18.9 percent on 1,920 parcels) and Victoria-to-Queensland metro (+23.5 percent on 880 parcels). Five things to notice:

1. **The opportunity is concentrated, not spread.** Two zones carry 2,800 of the month's parcels and an average gap around $1.85 each. Moving just those two lanes to Sendle saves roughly $5,180 a month, about $62k a year, with no change to the regional lanes where AusPost already wins.
2. **AusPost wins on regional and remote.** Every regional row is negative: Sendle's flat national rate over-charges for regional Victoria and dramatically over-charges remote WA/NT (-14.9 percent in AusPost's favour). This is the textbook pattern: aggregators flatten the curve, so the single carrier wins the long, sparse lanes and loses the dense, short ones. Do not move regional volume.
3. **Parcel count is the guardrail.** The remote WA/NT row shows a 14.9 percent AusPost advantage but on only 60 parcels. Even a wrong call there is rounding. Always read the gap and the count together; the alert is per-row precisely so a big percentage on tiny volume does not drive a carrier-switch decision.
4. **This is a cost card, not a service card.** Sendle being cheaper on VIC-to-QLD metro says nothing about whether Sendle delivers on time. Before re-routing, pair with [Express Post Next-Business-Day OTD](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/australia-post/express-post-next-business-day-otd) and [On-Time Delivery Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/australia-post/on-time-delivery-rate) to confirm you are not buying a cost saving with a reliability hit your customers will feel.
5. **The October price review is the recurring trigger.** AusPost revises parcel pricing each October. A zone that sat at +9 percent in September can jump past the 15 percent alert in October without you touching anything. Treat a fresh October alert as "re-price the contract", not "Sendle got cheaper".

## Reconciling against the source

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in the carriers' own tooling:**

For the AusPost side, the authoritative record is your [Australia Post MyPost Business](https://auspost.com.au/mypost-business) account or, for credit-account merchants, the [Australia Post Business](https://auspost.com.au/business) parcel-send invoice. Go to **Reports / Statements → Parcel charges** and filter by the 30-day window. The per-article charge there is the truth the card reconciles AusPost cost against. The price the API quotes (`POST /shipping/v1/prices/items`) is the rack rate; your contracted rate on the invoice is usually lower, so always reconcile the card against the invoice, not the quote API, if your account has negotiated pricing.

For the Sendle side, the comparison is a live quote from the [Sendle dashboard](https://www.sendle.com/) quote tool or its public rate card for your plan tier (Sendle's flat rates differ by plan: Standard, Premium, Pro). The card uses the plan tier configured on your Sendle connector, so if no Sendle account is connected the comparison falls back to Sendle's published list rates, which are higher than negotiated Pro rates and will under-state the true saving.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from the carriers' own figures:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Contracted vs rack rate** | Ours can read high on AusPost | If your AusPost contract rate is loaded but the card is still using the API rack quote for a lane it has not seen on an invoice yet, the gap will over-state. Reconcile against the invoice once a full billing cycle has run. |
| **Sendle plan tier** | Ours can read high on the gap | Published Sendle rates are the worst case. A negotiated Pro plan narrows the gap; load the correct plan on the connector. |
| **Cubic vs dead weight** | Either | Both carriers bill the greater of dead and cubic weight. If your manifest dimensions are missing or rounded, the cubed band can mismatch and shift a zone by one price step. |
| **Surcharge scope** | Ours reads base-only | The card excludes optional cover, Signature on Delivery and remote-area surcharges to keep the carriage comparison clean. AusPost's invoice total includes them, so the invoice line will be higher than the card's "charged" figure for parcels that carried extras. |
| **AEST vs UTC boundary** | Boundary days off | AusPost statements are in carrier-local (AEST / AEDT). The card stores in UTC. Over a 30-day window this averages out to well under 1 percent, but a single-day comparison can shift a few parcels across the boundary. |

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Should I just move everything to whichever carrier is cheaper per zone?**
No. The card is lane-level on purpose. The right move is to split the carrier mix: keep AusPost on the regional and remote zones where it wins, route the flagged dense metro lanes to Sendle. A blanket switch loses the regional saving and usually buys a service-quality problem on the long lanes where aggregators are weakest.

**Why is Sendle cheaper on metro but dearer on regional?**
Aggregator economics. Sendle prices on a coarse national flat-rate band, which over-recovers on short dense metro lanes (cheap to actually move) and under-recovers on long sparse regional lanes (expensive to move), so it has to price the flat rate above AusPost's regional zone rate to stay viable. AusPost prices per zone, so it tracks the real cost curve more closely. The result is the pattern you see: aggregator wins metro, single carrier wins regional.

**Does this account for my negotiated AusPost rates?**
Only once it has reconciled against your invoice. The API quote is rack rate. If your account has contracted pricing, load it on the connector or let one full billing cycle reconcile, otherwise the card over-states the gap against AusPost. The alert is deliberately set at 15 percent, above typical contract-vs-rack noise, but a fresh connector with no invoice history will read high.

**What about Express Post and time-definite services?**
Excluded from the substitution logic. Sendle's standard parcel service is not a like-for-like replacement for AusPost Express Post next-business-day. The card compares only the standard / Parcel Post tier. If a flagged zone is mostly Express volume, the saving is not real because the services are not equivalent. Check [Parcel Post vs Express vs Standard Mix](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/australia-post/parcel-post-vs-express-vs-standard-mix) first.

**Why did a zone suddenly trip the alert in October?**
AusPost runs an annual price review effective in October each year. Zone rates can move several percent overnight, pushing a lane that sat just under 15 percent over the line with no change on the Sendle side. Treat an October trip as a contract re-pricing trigger, not as a Sendle rate change.

**Sendle is cheaper but will my customers notice?**
Possibly. Sendle is a carbon-neutral courier aggregator with a different last-mile network and a different tracking experience to AusPost. Before moving volume, check [On-Time Delivery Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/australia-post/on-time-delivery-rate) and [Shipments with Tracking-Event Gap >24h (domestic) / >5d (intl)](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/australia-post/shipments-with-tracking-event-gap-24h-domestic-5d-intl) on a small test cohort. A cost saving that raises your "where is my order" ticket volume is not a saving.

**The card shows a big gap on a zone with only a handful of parcels. Do I act?**
No. Read the gap percentage and the parcel count together. A 30 percent gap on 40 parcels a month is around $1k a year of opportunity against real switching cost and operational complexity. Prioritise zones that are both over-priced and high-volume; ignore the long tail.
