## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> A 90-day sparkline of the average rated charge per DHL Paket consignment. It smooths out day-to-day noise so finance and despatch can see the direction of unit shipping cost: the slow creep from surcharge changes, the step from an annual rate review, or the seasonal lift from peak handling fees. There is no alert; this is a trend read, not a threshold.
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## What it tracks

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:body */}
The card divides total rated DHL Paket spend by parcel count for each day and plots the rolling line over 90 days. Values come from the DHL Paket Shipment API (`/parcel/de/shipping/v2/orders`) rated charges, reconciled against the monthly Rechnung in the [DHL Geschäftskundenportal](https://www.dhl.de/de/geschaeftskunden.html), and are shown in EUR. The 90-day span is deliberate: it is long enough to catch a DHL annual rate change (the rate card typically resets on 1 January), a Weihnachten peak-surcharge season, and a structural shift in your zone or weight mix, but short enough to stay readable. A rising trend with a stable zone mix points to carrier price increases or surcharge creep, which is a renegotiation cue; a rising trend that tracks a shift towards heavier parcels or the Welt zone is a product-mix story, which you confirm on [Cost by Zone](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/deutsche-post/cost-by-zone). Use the trend to decide whether a cost movement is the carrier or your own catalogue.
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:body */}

## Reconciling against the source

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
Compare the trend against month-on-month average cost per parcel on the **Rechnung** in the DHL Geschäftskundenportal. Differences are usually the rolling daily average here versus calendar-month invoice totals, and invoice-only surcharges (fuel, peak, Sperrgut) that do not appear in the API-rated value. Rating timestamps are in carrier-local German time.
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
