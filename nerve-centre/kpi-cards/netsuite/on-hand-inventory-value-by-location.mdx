> Live dollar value of stock on hand, valued at landed cost, split by NetSuite location so you can see exactly which warehouse is holding the working capital.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> The current total value of inventory you physically hold, valued at landed cost, broken out per NetSuite location or warehouse. It answers "how much cash is sitting on each warehouse floor right now". This is the where-is-my-capital view of the same dollars that the consolidated total inventory value card reports as a single number.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | Sum of on-hand quantity multiplied by unit cost, valued at landed cost where landed cost is configured, aggregated and then split per NetSuite location. |
| **Cost basis** | Landed cost where available (item cost plus freight, duty and handling), otherwise the item's costing method value (average, FIFO or standard). |
| **Currency** | Reporting currency for the consolidated headline; per-location rows can be read in the subsidiary's base currency. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. On OneWorld, locations roll up to their owning subsidiary. |
| **Time window** | `RT` (live snapshot of current on-hand) |
| **Alert trigger** | None. Informational by design. |
| **Roles** | owner, finance, operations |
| **Chart type** | KPI value with per-location breakdown beneath the headline total |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

Calculated automatically from your NetSuite data. The card takes current on-hand quantity for every stocked item, values each at landed cost where landed cost is configured (falling back to the item's costing-method value otherwise), and sums the result. It then splits that total across the NetSuite locations that hold the stock, so the headline is the company-wide figure and each row underneath is one warehouse, store or 3PL location.

Because this is a live snapshot rather than a period view, the number moves continuously as receipts, fulfilments, transfers and adjustments post. There is no alert: the card is a positioning and working-capital view, not a threshold monitor. See the At a glance summary above for the cost basis and the worked example below for how to read the per-location split.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A US outdoor-equipment brand on NetSuite OneWorld. US parent (USD) runs two owned warehouses plus a West Coast 3PL; a Canadian sub (CAD) runs one warehouse feeding Canadian DTC and wholesale. Annual revenue ~$60M. Snapshot taken 12 Jun 26. Reporting currency USD.

| Location | Subsidiary | On-hand value (USD) | Share of total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| East DC (owned) | US parent | $9,420,000 | 41% |
| Central DC (owned) | US parent | $6,180,000 | 27% |
| West Coast 3PL | US parent | $3,640,000 | 16% |
| Toronto DC | CA sub | $3,160,000 | 14% |
| Returns / quarantine bin | US parent | $480,000 | 2% |
| **Total On-Hand Inventory Value** | | **$22,880,000** | **100%** |

Five things to notice:

1. **The headline of $22.88M is the same dollars your CFO sees on the Balance Sheet, but the per-location split is what operations actually needs.** A single consolidated inventory number is a finance figure: it tells you how much capital is locked up, but not where it is or whether it is in the right place. By splitting the same total across five locations, the card turns a Balance Sheet line into an operational map. The East DC holding 41% of all inventory is a fact the CFO never sees on the Income Statement and the warehouse manager lives with every day. Surfacing the split live, rather than at month-end, lets operations rebalance stock before a region runs short while another sits long.

2. **The West Coast 3PL holding $3.64M is the line that should prompt a carrying-cost conversation.** Stock at a 3PL is billed by the pallet or by the cube, so every dollar sitting there is a recurring variable storage cost, unlike owned-warehouse stock where the rent is fixed regardless of how full the building is. $3.64M parked at a 3PL that bills per-cube is a different financial animal from $9.42M in an owned East DC. The card surfaces the location split; pair it with the carrying-cost view to see which locations are the most expensive places to hold a dollar of stock, and you have the case for either consolidating into owned space or accelerating sell-through at the 3PL.

3. **The $480K in the returns and quarantine bin is the line nobody owns and everybody forgets.** Returns-pending and quarantine locations accumulate stock that is neither sellable nor written off: it is in limbo. $480K sounds small against a $22.88M total, but it is 2% of working capital frozen in a location that generates zero revenue and is often invisible to both the buying team (who think it is gone) and finance (who think it is sellable). The per-location split is the only routine view that puts this number in front of someone weekly. The action is a standing process to grade and clear the bin: restock the good units, write down the dead ones, and stop the limbo from growing.

4. **On OneWorld, the Toronto DC line is in CAD underneath but rolls up in USD at the top, and that distinction matters.** The Canadian sub manages its warehouse in CAD, so the operational team in Toronto thinks in CAD, but the consolidated headline translates that stock to USD at the current rate. A weakening Canadian dollar makes the Toronto line shrink in the consolidated view even if not a single unit moved. When you read the per-location split for operational decisions (rebalancing, reorder), read the local-currency row; when you read it for the working-capital headline, read the translated USD. Confusing the two leads to phantom moves where the consolidated number changes and the warehouse swears nothing happened.

5. **The card is deliberately alert-free, and that is the right design for a positioning view.** There is no "good" or "bad" total inventory value in the abstract: $22.88M is healthy for a $60M outdoor brand heading into peak season and bloated for the same brand in a soft quarter. The number only becomes a signal when paired with velocity (turnover), age (aging buckets) or demand (open Sales Orders against each location). So this card stays informational and hands the judgement to its siblings. Use it as the denominator and the map, then let turnover, aging and reorder-point cards supply the verdict on whether the stock in each location is the right stock.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

This is one of the cleaner cards to reconcile, because NetSuite exposes inventory valuation natively and the card is essentially a faithful read of it, re-pivoted by location. The closest native views:

> **Reports → Inventory → Inventory Valuation Summary** (the company-wide base; the headline should tie out closely)
> **Reports → Inventory → Inventory Valuation Detail** (item-by-item value, the granular source)
> **Reports → Inventory → Inventory Valuation by Subsidiary** (for OneWorld; per-sub roll-up)
> **Lists → Accounting → Items** with the on-hand-by-location sublist (per-location quantities, the split this card pivots on)
> **Saved search**: an item saved search summing `Location Quantity On Hand × Cost` grouped by location, which is essentially what this card computes

The headline should reconcile tightly to the Inventory Valuation Summary at the same instant. Because the card is a live snapshot and the report is run on demand, small differences arise purely from transactions posting between the two reads.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from the Inventory Valuation report:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Snapshot timing** | Either | The card is live; the report is run at a moment in time. Receipts, fulfilments and transfers posting between the two reads create small differences. |
| **Landed cost vs costing-method value** | Either | The card prefers landed cost (item plus freight, duty, handling) where configured; an Inventory Valuation report run on base item cost will read lower by the landed-cost component. |
| **Costing method** | Either | Average, FIFO and standard costing value the same units differently. The card uses the item's configured method; a report run with a different valuation basis will diverge. |
| **In-transit inventory** | Either | Stock on an inter-location or inter-company transfer that has shipped but not been received can appear in different locations depending on whether the transfer is in-transit-tracked. |
| **Non-inventory and assembly components** | Either | Whether assemblies are valued as built units or as component stock affects the per-location value of a manufacturing or kitting location. |
| **Returns and quarantine bins** | Either | Some accounts exclude returns-pending or quarantine locations from "sellable" valuation; the card includes all stocked locations unless filtered. |
| **FX translation cadence** | Small | On OneWorld, remote-sub locations translate to reporting currency at the current rate; a report run at period-end FX will differ slightly. |
| **Negative on-hand** | Either | A location showing negative on-hand (oversold before receipt posted) is valued differently across configurations and can pull a location row down. |

**Cross-connector reconciliation:**

The per-location value becomes far more powerful when joined to commerce availability and to demand. NetSuite alone can tell you a location holds $9.42M of stock; it cannot tell you whether that stock is in the SKUs the storefront is actually trying to sell from that location.

| Card | Expected relationship | What the comparison reveals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ERP-vs-Ecom Inventory Variance %](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/erp-vs-ecom-inventory-variance) | Should agree per location | If a location's on-hand value is high but the variance card flags that location as drifting from commerce availability, the value is real but the storefront cannot sell it, which is the worst combination. |
| [Low Stock Alerts](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/low-stock-alerts) | Complementary | High total value in a location alongside low-stock alerts for popular SKUs in that same location means the value is locked in the wrong items. |
| [Inventory Turnover](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/inventory-turnover-ratio) | Inverse signal | A location holding a large share of total value but turning slowly is where working capital goes to sit. |

The killer cross-channel framing is value-in-the-wrong-place. A warehouse can hold the largest share of company inventory value and still be the cause of stockouts, if the value is concentrated in slow SKUs while the fast SKUs run short. NetSuite shows the value; the commerce connectors show the demand; Vortex IQ joins them so you can see not just where your capital is, but whether it is where the orders are. That intersection (high value, high local demand for different SKUs) is invisible to NetSuite alone and is the single most common cause of "we have plenty of inventory but keep stocking out".

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Why does the headline differ from my Balance Sheet inventory line?**
Usually because of cost basis and timing. The card prefers landed cost (item cost plus freight, duty and handling) where you have configured it, while a quick Balance Sheet read might be on base item cost; the landed-cost component pushes the card higher. Timing also matters: the card is a live snapshot and the Balance Sheet is a period-end figure, so any stock movement since close will diverge. For an exact tie-out, run the Inventory Valuation Summary at the same instant on the same costing basis.

**What exactly does landed cost include, and what if I have not set it up?**
Landed cost is the fully-loaded cost of getting a unit onto your shelf: the supplier price plus inbound freight, customs duty, insurance in transit and handling. If you have configured landed cost in NetSuite, the card uses it, which is the most economically honest valuation. If you have not, the card falls back to the item's costing-method value (average, FIFO or standard), which excludes those inbound costs and will read lower. Many merchants under-value inventory by skipping landed cost; the card surfaces the fuller number where the data exists.

**OneWorld and subsidiaries, how do locations roll up?**
Each NetSuite location belongs to a subsidiary, so the per-location rows roll up to their owning subsidiary, and the subsidiaries roll up to the consolidated headline. The dashboard filter lets you read one subsidiary's locations in isolation or the whole group. The common pitfall is a shared 3PL location servicing more than one subsidiary; confirm how that location is assigned in NetSuite before reading its value as belonging to a single entity.

**FX and multi-currency, how should I read the per-location rows?**
Read local-currency rows for operational decisions and the translated reporting-currency headline for working-capital decisions. A remote-subsidiary location is managed in its base currency, but the consolidated total translates it to reporting currency at the current rate. A pure FX move will change the consolidated value of a foreign location without any unit moving, so if the headline shifts on a volatile FX day and operations swears nothing changed, the per-location local-currency rows will confirm the units are unchanged.

**Does in-transit inventory show up, and in which location?**
It depends on how the transfer is set up. Stock on an in-transit-tracked transfer sits in a transit location until received, so it is neither in the origin nor the destination warehouse. Stock on a transfer that posts directly will move from origin to destination instantly. This is the most common reason a specific warehouse row looks lower than the floor staff expect: the units have shipped from another location but not yet arrived and posted.

**Why does a location sometimes show negative on-hand value?**
Negative on-hand happens when a fulfilment or sale posts before the corresponding receipt, leaving the system temporarily showing fewer than zero units. NetSuite allows this in some configurations, and it usually self-corrects when the receipt posts. A persistently negative location is a process problem worth investigating, and it ties directly to the on-hand-went-negative monitoring elsewhere in the Nerve Centre. The value impact is small but the data-quality signal is real.

**Are returns and quarantine locations included?**
By default yes, because they hold real stock with real cost. But that stock is not sellable, so including it inflates the "available inventory value" if you read the headline naively. Some teams prefer to exclude returns-pending and quarantine bins from the headline and watch them as a separate line. The per-location split makes this easy: the bin is just another row, and you can mentally subtract it to get sellable value.

**B2B versus DTC, does the location view read differently?**
The mechanics are identical, but the interpretation differs. A B2B distributor often holds large customer-allocated safety stock in a primary DC, so a single location dominating the value split is normal and healthy. A DTC brand fulfilling from multiple regional locations or 3PLs to cut shipping time will have a more even split, and a sudden concentration in one location can signal a fulfilment-network problem. Read the expected shape of the split against your fulfilment model.

**How does this relate to inventory carrying cost?**
This card is the base; carrying cost applies a holding-cost rate on top. The crucial link is that the rate differs by location type: 3PL stock is billed variably per pallet or cube, while owned-warehouse stock has fixed rent, so a dollar held at a 3PL costs more to carry than a dollar held in an owned building. Reading this card alongside the carrying-cost card tells you not just where your capital is, but which locations are the most expensive places to keep it.

**Does period close change this number?**
Not the live snapshot itself, but close-period adjustments (write-downs, physical-count reconciliations, costing recalculations) post during close and will move the value as they do. If you take a snapshot before count adjustments are entered and another after, the second will be more accurate. For board reporting, take the read after close adjustments are posted so the per-location split reflects the audited count.

**Can I trust this for an oversell-risk decision on its own?**
No, and that is by design. This card tells you the value on hand, not whether the storefront's available-to-sell number agrees with it. For oversell risk you need the ERP-vs-commerce comparison, because a location can show plenty of value while the commerce platform's availability for those same SKUs is wrong. Use this card for the capital and positioning question, and hand the oversell question to the variance and drift cards.

**NetSuite versus other ERPs on per-location valuation?**
NetSuite's location and bin structure makes this split natural, and the card reads it directly. SAP S/4HANA models the same concept through plants and storage locations, and Oracle ERP Cloud through inventory organisations and subinventories; the per-location pivot translates conceptually but the field structure differs. For mid-market commerce on NetSuite, this is one of the most faithful native-to-card reconciliations in the connector, because NetSuite's inventory valuation is robust and the card largely re-pivots rather than re-computes it.
