> Customers carrying 60+ day AR who are still placing or being accepted for new ecom orders. A credit-risk leak where the storefront keeps selling to people who have stopped paying.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> An alert table of every customer who carries accounts-receivable older than 60 days in NetSuite and is also still placing new orders on your commerce platform. This is the credit-risk leak that no single system can see: NetSuite knows the customer owes you and has not paid; the storefront, which does not read the AR ledger, keeps happily accepting their new orders. Each row is a customer you are extending more credit to while they sit on debt they have already failed to clear. Left unchecked it is how a manageable receivable becomes a bad-debt write-off.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | Customers with a NetSuite AR balance aged over 60 days who also have one or more new commerce orders in the period. Each row is one customer, their overdue AR balance, their days-overdue, and the value of the new orders they have just placed. |
| **AR aging basis** | Open invoice balances aged from invoice due date. The 60-day line is the default risk threshold; below it a customer is merely slow, above it they are a genuine credit concern. |
| **Credit hold expectation** | These customers should be flagged with credit hold on (the `onCreditHold` field set to ON or AUTO) via a SuiteScript or workflow so the storefront stops accepting their orders. When this card fires, that automation has not caught them, so manual review is needed. |
| **Currency** | Per-customer AR in transaction currency; the at-risk total in reporting currency. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. On OneWorld a customer can carry AR in one subsidiary and order against another; the card surfaces the combined exposure. |
| **Time window** | `30D vsP` (new ecom orders in the last 30 days, compared against the prior 30) |
| **Alert trigger** | `any customer over 60d AR with new ecom orders`, driven by the credit-risk sentiment key |
| **Roles** | owner, finance |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

Calculated automatically by joining your NetSuite AR ledger to your commerce order stream. The card takes every customer with an open AR balance aged beyond 60 days, then checks whether that same customer has placed a new order on the storefront within the period. Where both are true, the customer appears in the table with their overdue balance, the age of the oldest open invoice, and the value of the new orders, so finance can see at a glance how much fresh credit is being extended on top of an unpaid balance.

The join is the entire point. NetSuite holds the receivable and the aging; the commerce platform holds the new order; neither system on its own can tell you that the customer doing both is the same entity. Matching them requires a shared customer identity (email, account code, or a mapped customer record) maintained across both connectors. The alert fires on any match, because in a healthy credit process a 60-day-overdue customer should already be on credit hold and unable to place new orders. See the At a glance summary above for what the metric tracks and the worked example below for a typical reading.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A US industrial supplies distributor on NetSuite OneWorld running a B2B storefront on Adobe Commerce plus a DTC channel on Shopify. Two subsidiaries: a US parent (USD) and a Mexican sub (MXN). Annual revenue ~$68M, of which roughly 70% is B2B on terms. Period: 30 days ending 22 Jun 26. Reporting currency USD.

**Headline:** 14 customers with 60+ day AR placed new ecom orders this period. Overdue AR on those customers: $612,000. New orders accepted from them: $148,000.

| Customer | Oldest invoice age | Overdue AR | New ecom orders (30d) | Credit hold flag | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Atlas Fabrication Co | 112 days | $186,000 | $42,000 | OFF | Critical |
| Northgate Mechanical | 88 days | $134,000 | $31,000 | OFF | Critical |
| Reyes Industrial (MX sub) | 74 days | $96,000 | $28,000 | OFF | High |
| Summit Tool & Die | 96 days | $71,000 | $18,000 | AUTO (not enforced) | High |
| Coastal Welding Supply | 67 days | $48,000 | $14,000 | OFF | Medium |
| (9 more) | 61-83 days | $77,000 | $15,000 | mixed | Medium |
| **Total (this card)** | | **$612,000** | **$148,000** | | |

Five things to notice:

1. **You are extending $148,000 of new credit to customers who already owe $612,000 they have not paid.** That single sentence is the card. On every separate dashboard these look like good news: the storefront sees $148,000 of healthy new orders, and the AR team sees a manageable-looking aging report. Only the join reveals that the new orders and the old debt belong to the same 14 customers, and that the business is doubling down on its riskiest accounts rather than cutting them off. The longer the loop runs, the larger the exposure grows, because each accepted order adds to the balance the customer has already shown they struggle to clear.

2. **The worst row, Atlas Fabrication, is the textbook failure mode.** They are 112 days overdue on $186,000, which is well past the point where a credit team would normally suspend the account, and yet they placed $42,000 of new orders this period with their credit-hold flag still OFF. This is exactly the case the card spec calls out: the account should have been auto-flagged with `onCreditHold` set to ON or AUTO by a SuiteScript or workflow the moment it crossed 60 days, and it was not. Either the automation does not exist, or it exists and silently failed, which is why the card explicitly routes to manual review when it fires. The fix is immediate: set the hold, which stops the storefront accepting further orders, then start a collections conversation on the $186,000.

3. **Summit Tool & Die shows the subtler and more dangerous failure: a hold that is set but not enforced.** Their flag reads AUTO, so the credit-hold logic did fire, but new orders are still flowing through Adobe Commerce because the storefront is not actually reading and enforcing the hold status at checkout. A flag that is set but not honoured is worse than no flag at all, because it gives the finance team false confidence that the control is working. This is a cross-system integration gap: NetSuite says hold, the storefront does not listen. The card surfaces it by showing new orders against a held account, which should be impossible if enforcement were wired correctly.

4. **The OneWorld dimension hides exposure unless you read the combined view.** Reyes Industrial owes against the Mexican subsidiary in MXN but is placing some of its new orders against the US parent's catalogue. Looked at per-subsidiary, neither sub sees the full picture: the MX sub sees the debt but not all the new orders, and the US parent sees new orders from a customer whose debt sits on another entity's books. The card consolidates the customer's identity across subsidiaries so the true combined exposure ($96,000 owed, $28,000 of fresh orders) is visible as one row. Reading per-sub would understate the risk on exactly the accounts that span entities, which are often the larger, more complex, more dangerous ones.

5. **The economic case for acting is overwhelming and the action is cheap.** Suspending 14 accounts and opening collections conversations is a few hours of a credit controller's time. The exposure being managed is $612,000 of existing debt plus $148,000 of new credit, $760,000 in total, on accounts statistically likely to deteriorate further given they are already 60-plus days late. If even a third of that ages into bad debt, the write-off dwarfs every other line on the working-capital dashboard. The card converts a slow, invisible drift toward write-off into a single prioritised worklist that a controller can clear in an afternoon, which is the highest-leverage credit action available.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

NetSuite holds the AR aging and the credit-hold status; the new orders live on the commerce platform. The NetSuite side of this card reconciles against the receivables reports and the customer record. The closest native views to triangulate against:

> **Reports → Customers/Receivables → A/R Aging Detail** (the per-customer overdue balances and aging buckets, the NetSuite half of the card)
> **Reports → Customers/Receivables → Customer Open Balance** (each customer's total open AR)
> **Lists → Relationships → Customers** (the customer record where the credit-hold status and credit limit live; check whether `onCreditHold` is ON, AUTO, or OFF)
> **Saved search**: a Customer saved search filtered to days-overdue greater than 60 with open balance, which is the NetSuite-only half before the commerce join is applied
> **Reports → Customers/Receivables → Customer Payment History** (to confirm whether a flagged customer has genuinely stopped paying or just has timing noise)

The headline difference between this card and a NetSuite A/R Aging report is that the report cannot tell you which of the overdue customers is still ordering on your storefront, because NetSuite does not see the live commerce order stream until and unless an order becomes a sales order. The whole reason the card exists is that the riskiest signal lives in the gap between the two systems.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a NetSuite A/R Aging report:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Aging-date basis** | Either | The card ages from invoice due date; a report aged from invoice date will show longer ages, pulling more customers over the 60-day line. |
| **Unapplied payments and credits** | Card may overstate | A customer who has paid but whose payment is sitting unapplied still shows an open balance until the cash is applied. The [Cash Application Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/cash-application-rate) card catches this; an unapplied-cash backlog can make this card list customers who have actually paid. |
| **Customer identity match** | Either | The join depends on matching the commerce customer to the NetSuite customer. A customer ordering under a different email or a guest checkout may not match, so a genuinely risky customer can be missed, or a near-duplicate record can double-count. |
| **In-flight new orders** | Either | A new commerce order that has not yet synced to NetSuite is visible to the card via the commerce connector but not yet on any NetSuite report, so the card can list activity the ERP has not recorded. |
| **Subsidiary consolidation** | Either | The card consolidates a customer's AR and orders across subsidiaries; a single-subsidiary A/R report will not show the cross-entity exposure. |
| **Disputed invoices** | Card may overstate | An invoice formally in dispute is technically overdue but may be legitimately withheld. If disputes are not flagged in NetSuite the card cannot exclude them; flag disputes so they do not inflate the at-risk total. |

**Cross-connector reconciliation, the killer cross-channel finding:**

This card is born from the cross-connector join. NetSuite alone sees the debt; the commerce platform alone sees the new order; only the join sees the same customer doing both, and that intersection is the entire signal.

On this distributor, $148,000 of the period's new ecom orders went to customers carrying $612,000 of 60-plus-day debt. NetSuite alone reports the $612,000 as an aging problem and the storefront alone reports the $148,000 as a sales win; only Vortex IQ, joining the AR ledger to the live order stream and the credit-hold status, can say "these are the same 14 customers, and your storefront is not enforcing the holds your ERP has set". That sentence turns a slow drift toward bad debt into a one-afternoon worklist, and it is one no single connector can produce. The card pays for the connector stack on a single avoided write-off.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Why 60 days and not 30 or 90?**
Sixty days is the conventional line between a customer who is merely slow and one who is a genuine credit concern. Most net-30 terms tolerate some slippage, so 30 days overdue is common and rarely alarming. By 60 days the customer has missed by a full cycle and is statistically far more likely to keep aging. The threshold is configurable per workspace; tighten it to 45 for high-risk categories or loosen it to 90 for industries with traditionally slow payment, but 60 is the default for a reason.

**Should these customers already be on credit hold automatically?**
Yes. Best practice is a SuiteScript or workflow that sets `onCreditHold` to ON or AUTO when a customer crosses your overdue threshold, which then stops the storefront from accepting their orders. When this card fires, that automation has either not been built, has not caught a specific customer, or has set the flag without the storefront enforcing it. In every case the card routes to manual review precisely because the automatic control did not hold.

**A customer is on the list but they have actually paid. Why?**
Almost always unapplied cash. The payment arrived but has not been matched to the open invoices, so NetSuite still shows the balance as outstanding. Check the [Cash Application Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/cash-application-rate) card; a backlog of unapplied payments will populate this card with customers who are square. Apply the cash and they drop off. This is the single most common false positive.

**How does the customer identity match work across systems?**
The join matches the commerce customer to the NetSuite customer record, typically on email, account code, or a mapped customer ID. The match is only as good as the identity hygiene. A customer ordering under a personal email on Shopify but billed under a company record in NetSuite may not match, so a real risk can be missed; conversely, duplicate NetSuite records for one customer can split a balance. The [Ecom Customers Absent from NetSuite Customer Record](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ecom-customers-absent-from-netsuite-customer-record) card surfaces the unmatched population that this card cannot evaluate.

**We are on OneWorld. How is cross-subsidiary exposure handled?**
The card consolidates a customer's AR balance and new orders across subsidiaries so a customer who owes against one entity and orders against another shows as a single combined-exposure row. This is deliberate, because cross-entity customers are often the larger accounts where per-subsidiary reporting would understate the risk. The per-subsidiary breakdown is available, but the consolidated row is the one that matters for the credit decision.

**How does FX affect the at-risk total?**
Each customer's AR is held in its transaction currency and translated to reporting currency for the consolidated at-risk total at the dashboard FX cadence. A weakening reporting currency raises the translated value of foreign-currency debt even if nothing changed operationally, so for period-over-period comparison lock the FX cadence. The decision to suspend an account is currency-independent; only the headline dollar exposure moves with FX.

**B2B versus DTC, does this card behave differently?**
Substantially. The risk is far higher on B2B, because B2B customers order on account (terms) rather than paying at checkout, so a B2B storefront can keep accepting on-account orders from an overdue customer indefinitely. DTC customers usually pay at checkout, so an overdue DTC customer placing a new prepaid order is lower risk, the new order is paid even if the old invoice is not. Weight your reading by channel: a B2B row is a credit decision, a prepaid DTC row is more of a relationship flag.

**Does a disputed invoice belong on this card?**
Only if you want it there. A genuinely disputed invoice is technically overdue but may be legitimately withheld pending resolution, so including it overstates true credit risk. Flag disputes in NetSuite so the card can exclude them; otherwise a customer who is withholding payment for a valid quality or delivery reason will appear alongside customers who simply have not paid, and the two require very different handling.

**Does this card touch revenue recognition?**
Indirectly. The new orders these customers place will, if accepted, become Cash Sales or Invoices that recognise revenue under ASC 606 in the normal way. The card's concern is not recognition timing but collectability: revenue recognised on a customer who will not pay becomes a bad-debt expense later. Recognising revenue on a 112-day-overdue customer's new order is technically correct and economically reckless, which is the tension the card highlights.

**Can SuiteScript both cause and cure the problem here?**
Yes. A well-built SuiteScript or workflow is the cure: it sets the credit hold automatically and, ideally, signals the storefront to stop accepting orders. A broken or missing one is the cause: the hold never gets set, or it gets set but is not enforced at checkout. If you suspect the automation has failed silently, check the [SuiteScript Execution Errors (24h)](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/suitescript-execution-errors-24h) card, a failing credit-hold script will both raise errors there and populate customers here.

**NetSuite vs other ERPs on credit-risk-meets-storefront?**
The gap is universal because it sits between the ERP and the storefront, not inside either. SAP S/4HANA has sophisticated credit management (SAP Credit Management) that can block sales documents, and Oracle ERP Cloud has credit checks in order management, but in both cases the ecommerce storefront is a separate system that must be wired to honour the ERP's credit decision in real time, and that wiring is exactly where the leak occurs. Vortex IQ catches the leak regardless of ERP, because it joins the AR side to the live order side rather than relying on either system to police the other.

You can ask "which overdue customers are still ordering and not on enforced hold?" and get the prioritised worklist directly, which is the fastest path from the alert firing to the accounts being suspended.
