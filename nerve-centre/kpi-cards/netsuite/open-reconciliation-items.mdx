> Open Reconciliation Items: a count of unreconciled bank and GL reconciliation items still open past the close date, the most common single blocker to locking a period.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> How many bank or GL reconciliation items are still sitting unmatched after the period should have been reconciled. During the close these are expected to fall to zero; any that remain open past the close date are unexplained differences between your books and the bank, and each one is a reason the period cannot safely lock. The card surfaces the count so finance sees the remaining work before the close slips.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | Unreconciled reconciliation items (bank statement lines or GL entries that have not been matched and cleared) that remain open past the relevant close date. One per open item. |
| **What an item is** | A statement transaction or book entry awaiting a match: a deposit not yet matched to a payment, a charge not yet booked, a timing difference, or an unexplained variance between bank and book. |
| **Tax treatment** | n/a, this is a count of unmatched items, not a financial value, though each item represents a dollar difference that must be explained. |
| **Currency** | Reporting currency for any value context; the count itself is currency-agnostic. Foreign-currency bank accounts each reconcile in their own currency. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects the dashboard filter. On OneWorld, each subsidiary reconciles its own bank and GL accounts, so open items are attributable per sub. |
| **Time window** | `RT` (real-time; the count reflects what is still open right now). |
| **Alert trigger** | Fires when there are `greater than 0 past close-date` items, that is, any unreconciled item remaining after the period should have been reconciled. |
| **Roles** | owner, finance |
| **Chart type** | Single KPI count with zero-tolerance alert past close date. |
| **What it excludes** | Items legitimately reconciled and cleared, and in-period items that are not yet past their close date (those are normal work in progress, not alerts). |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

The card counts reconciliation items, bank statement lines and GL entries awaiting a match, that are still open after the close date for their account or period has passed. During a normal close, the reconciliation process matches book entries to bank statement lines and clears them; the count of open items should fall steadily toward zero as the close progresses. This card focuses on the residue: the items that have not cleared by the time they should have, because those are the ones that block a clean lock and represent genuine unexplained differences between the books and the bank.

The alert is deliberately zero-tolerance once the close date has passed. In-period reconciliation work is normal and is not flagged; an item only becomes a concern when it survives past the point at which it should have been resolved. The card does not classify why each item is open, whether it is a benign timing difference (a cheque in transit), a missing book entry (a bank charge not yet recorded), or a real discrepancy (a payment the bank shows but the books do not). It surfaces the count and lets the controller drill into the bank reconciliation to resolve each one before the period locks.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A DTC and wholesale supplements brand on NetSuite OneWorld. Three subsidiaries: a US parent (USD), a UK sub (GBP), and an EU sub (EUR), each with one operating bank account and a card-processor clearing account. Annual revenue roughly $60M. Snapshot 12 Jun 26, two days after the May close target of 10 Jun 26. Reporting context USD. These figures are illustrative.

| Account | Open items past close date | Typical cause | Aggregate variance (reporting USD) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US operating bank | 3 | Cheques in transit (timing) | $4,200 |
| US card-processor clearing | 9 | Processor fees not yet booked | $1,850 |
| UK operating bank | 2 | Unrecorded bank charges | £640 (~$815) |
| EU operating bank | 0 | Fully reconciled | 0 |
| EU card-processor clearing | 11 | Payouts not matched to orders | EUR 6,300 (~$6,800) |
| **Total** | **25** | | **~$13,665** |

Five things to notice:

1. **Twenty-five open items past the close date is the alert, but the items are not equal, and the card's value is in directing attention to the ones that matter.** The three US operating-bank items are cheques in transit, a benign timing difference that will clear on its own within days and represents no real discrepancy. The eleven EU card-processor items are a different animal: payouts the processor has made that have not been matched to the underlying orders, which can hide fee errors, missing orders, or even a sync failure between the payment platform and NetSuite. A controller who treats all 25 as equal wastes time; one who reads the breakdown clears the timing differences quickly and concentrates on the processor-clearing items, which are where real money and real risk hide.

2. **The card-processor clearing accounts are where the largest and most dangerous open counts cluster, and that is true for almost every commerce business.** Card processors batch many orders into a single payout, net of fees, refunds, and chargebacks, so the clearing account must reconcile many individual order-level book entries against one lumpy bank deposit. When the matching automation lags or breaks, items pile up fast. On this snapshot the US and EU clearing accounts together hold 20 of the 25 open items. This is the structural reason a commerce business's reconciliation pain concentrates in clearing accounts rather than the plain operating bank, and why this card is so often the thing standing between a commerce business and a clean close.

3. **The EU operating bank reading zero is as informative as the non-zero rows, because it proves the process works when it is fed clean data.** One sub fully reconciled its operating bank while two did not, which rules out "the close process is broken everywhere" and points instead at specific accounts and specific causes. A controller can use the zero row as a control: whatever the EU team did to clear its operating bank cleanly is the standard the other subs should meet. The card is not just a list of problems; the absence of a problem on one account is evidence about where the real friction is and is not.

4. **Each open item is a dollar difference that makes the period's cash and bank balances provisional, which is why the alert is zero-tolerance past close.** Until the 25 items clear, the book balance of those bank and clearing accounts does not agree with reality, so cash on the balance sheet, and any cash-position number derived from it, is unverified. An eleven-item backlog on the EU clearing account at roughly EUR 6,300 means the EU sub's recorded cash could be off by that amount in either direction until matched. That is small relative to a $60M business, but the principle is the point: a period with unreconciled bank items has not been independently verified against the bank, and the auditor will treat any such period as not closed. Zero is the only safe number past the close date.

5. **On OneWorld, open reconciliation items are the single most common reason one subsidiary's close lags the others, and this card makes that attributable.** The EU sub here is the laggard purely because of its 11 unmatched clearing items; clear those and the EU close can lock. Because the card respects the subsidiary filter, the controller can see exactly which sub and which account is holding up the consolidation, rather than facing a vague "the close is late" message. Paired with the period-close-status card, this turns "May is not closed" into "May is not closed because the EU card-processor clearing account has 11 unmatched payouts", which is an assignable task with a named owner, not a worry.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

Bank and account reconciliation is native to NetSuite, so this card triangulates directly against the reconciliation tooling and the relevant account registers:

> **Transactions → Bank → Reconcile Account Statement** (the reconciliation workspace where statement lines are matched to book entries; uncleared lines are the open items)
> **Transactions → Bank → Match Bank Data** (if you use the bank-feed matching tool; unmatched feed transactions are open items)
> **Reports → Banking/Budgeting → Reconciliation reports** and the account register for each bank and clearing account (to see uncleared book entries)
> **Saved search**: a Transaction saved search on the relevant accounts filtered to `cleared is false` within or before the period, grouped by account and subsidiary, is essentially the population the card counts
> **SuiteAnalytics workbook**: a reconciliation workbook joined to account and subsidiary, sliced by cleared status and date, reproduces the per-account open-item breakdown

The headline difference is that NetSuite's reconciliation workspace shows uncleared items account by account, but it does not, on its own, count them across all accounts past the close date and alert you when any remain. The card adds the cross-account count and the zero-tolerance-past-close alert on top of NetSuite's native reconciliation state.

**Why our count may legitimately differ from a NetSuite reconciliation view:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In-period versus past-close** | Card may show fewer | The card alerts only on items past the close date; a raw uncleared list includes normal in-period work that is not yet a concern. |
| **Which accounts are in scope** | Either | The card counts the bank and GL clearing accounts configured for reconciliation; a register view of a single account reads lower, an all-accounts view higher. |
| **Cleared versus reconciled** | Either | An item can be marked cleared but not yet formally reconciled in a statement, or vice versa; depending on which flag the comparison uses, counts can differ. |
| **Subsidiary filter** | Either | The card respects the dashboard subsidiary filter; a view over all subs reads higher. |
| **Timing differences** | Card may show more briefly | Cheques in transit and deposits in transit are open items by definition until they clear; a manual reconciliation may set them aside as known timing, while the card still counts them until cleared. |
| **Foreign-currency accounts** | Either | A multi-currency bank account reconciles in its own currency; FX revaluation can create or clear small items that a single-currency view does not show. |
| **Auto-matched items** | Card may show fewer | If a bank-feed rule auto-clears certain items, they drop off; a stricter manual reconciliation might hold them open for review. |

There is a meaningful upstream dependency on the payment connectors even though there is no dollar-for-dollar cross-connector reconciliation here. Card-processor clearing accounts reconcile a single batched payout against many order-level entries, and the order-level entries originate in your commerce and payment platforms. When clearing-account open items spike, the cause is frequently a lag or failure in the payout-to-order matching that depends on payment-connector data landing in NetSuite. So a spike on this card alongside a low [Cash Application Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/cash-application-rate) and unmatched processor payouts is the same problem, the payment reconciliation pipeline, seen from the bank-reconciliation side. That correlation is the fastest way to tell a benign timing backlog from a real processor-sync failure.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**What exactly is an "open reconciliation item"?**
A bank statement line or GL entry that has not yet been matched and cleared in the reconciliation process. It can be a deposit not yet matched to a recorded payment, a bank charge not yet booked, a timing difference like a cheque in transit, or a genuine variance where the bank and the books disagree. Until it is matched and cleared, it is open, and past the close date it is flagged.

**Why is the alert zero-tolerance past the close date?**
Because in-period reconciliation work is normal but residual unmatched items after the close date are not. Any open item past close means the book balance of that account has not been verified against the bank, so the period's cash and bank balances are provisional. The auditor will treat such a period as not properly closed. Zero is the only safe target once the close date has passed; before it, open items are just work in progress and are not alerted.

**Are all open items equally serious?**
No. A cheque in transit is a benign timing difference that will clear on its own; an unmatched card-processor payout can hide fee errors, missing orders, or a sync failure. The card counts both, but the breakdown by account tells you where to focus. Clear timing differences quickly and concentrate effort on clearing-account items, which is where real money and real risk concentrate for commerce businesses.

**Why do card-processor clearing accounts dominate the count?**
Because processors batch many orders into one payout net of fees, refunds, and chargebacks, so the clearing account must match many order-level book entries against a single lumpy deposit. When the matching automation lags or breaks, items pile up fast. For almost every commerce business, the operating bank reconciles cleanly while the clearing accounts are the friction point. Expect the bulk of this card's count to live there.

**Multi-currency and FX, do they affect this card?**
The count itself is currency-agnostic, but foreign-currency bank accounts reconcile in their own currency and FX revaluation can create or clear small items. A multi-currency account may show a residual item that is purely an FX rounding difference; that still counts as open until matched. For value context the card uses the reporting currency, so a small foreign-currency variance is translated for display.

**OneWorld and per-subsidiary reconciliation, how should I read this?**
Each subsidiary reconciles its own bank and GL accounts, so open items are attributable per sub. The card respects the subsidiary filter, which lets you see exactly which sub and which account is holding open items. This is usually how you discover which subsidiary is the laggard in a multi-entity close: the one with the unreconciled clearing account. Slice by sub before acting.

**B2B versus DTC, does the reading differ?**
The mechanism is identical, but the profile differs. DTC businesses live or die by card-processor clearing reconciliation because of high payout volume. B2B businesses have fewer, larger receipts (wire transfers, ACH, cheques) so their open items are fewer but each can be larger and tied to a specific customer payment. A single unmatched B2B wire can be a six-figure item, so for B2B, item value matters as much as item count.

**Does an errored or unposted transaction show up here?**
Indirectly. A transaction that errored and never posted cannot reconcile, so the corresponding bank line stays open with no book entry to match. That is why errored transactions are a sibling concern: clearing the open item may require first fixing and posting the errored transaction. If an open item has no possible match because the book side never posted, look at the pending/error transaction cards.

**How does this relate to period close?**
Very directly. Open reconciliation items are the single most common reason a period cannot lock, because an unreconciled bank account means the period's cash is unverified. This card is a leading indicator for a slipping close; clearing it is usually a prerequisite step on the close checklist. Pair it with the period-close cards to connect the open-item count to the specific period it is blocking.

**Can a SuiteScript or bank-feed rule distort the count?**
Yes, in both directions. An auto-matching bank-feed rule can clear items automatically, reducing the count, sometimes too aggressively, clearing items that should have been reviewed. Conversely, a failed SuiteScript that was meant to post processor fees or apply payments leaves the corresponding items permanently open. If the count behaves oddly, check whether an auto-match rule or a scheduled script changed or failed.

**How does this compare to reconciliation in other ERPs?**
Every serious ERP has bank and account reconciliation, but the tooling differs. NetSuite reconciles per account and per subsidiary with cleared/uncleared status on each transaction. Oracle, SAP, and Microsoft Dynamics have comparable reconciliation modules under different names, some with stronger native bank-feed automation. The card normalises NetSuite's uncleared-item state into a single count with a past-close alert, so the reading is consistent regardless of how the underlying ERP structures its reconciliation, while the place you go to clear an item remains NetSuite-specific.
