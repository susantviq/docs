> Share of the last 12 accounting periods locked on or before their target close date.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> The percentage of your last 12 accounting periods that were locked on or before their target close date. It is a discipline metric: a high rate means the finance team is closing the books predictably and reporting can be trusted to arrive on time, while a falling rate signals process strain, resourcing gaps, or data-quality problems upstream. Unlike most cards this measures the finance function itself rather than the commerce operation.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | Count of the last 12 accounting periods locked on or before their target close date, divided by 12, expressed as a percentage. |
| **Currency** | n/a (a process metric, not a financial value). |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. OneWorld accounts can measure group-level close or per-sub close depending on configuration. |
| **Time window** | `12mo` (rolling last 12 accounting periods) |
| **Alert trigger** | Less than 90% (more than one of the last 12 periods closed late) |
| **Roles** | owner, finance |
| **On-time basis** | Period locked status timestamp versus the configured target close date for that period. |
| **Excludes** | Periods still open within their normal close window; adjustment-only and year-end-only periods may be configured separately. |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

The card looks back over the last 12 accounting periods and, for each one, compares the date the period was locked against the target close date configured for that period. A period that reached locked status on or before its target counts as on-time; one that was locked after its target, or that remains unlocked past its target, counts as late. The on-time count is divided by 12 and expressed as a percentage. The metric is about the cadence of the close process, not about the contents of any period, so it carries no currency or value dimension.

On a OneWorld account the close can be measured at the group level (all subsidiaries locked) or per subsidiary, depending on how the workspace defines "closed", because subsidiaries often close on slightly staggered schedules before the group consolidates. The target close date is a configured expectation rather than a NetSuite-enforced deadline, so the metric reflects your own stated close calendar. The worked example below shows how a single chronically late period drags the rolling rate and why the trend over the 12-period window matters more than any single month.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A consumer-electronics accessories brand on NetSuite OneWorld, selling DTC and through marketplaces, with a wholesale arm into big-box retail. Two subsidiaries: a US parent (USD) and a Canadian sub (CAD). Group revenue roughly $60M. The finance team targets a five-business-day close, meaning each monthly period should be locked by the fifth business day of the following month. The card is read on 22 Jun 26, covering the 12 periods from Jun 25 through May 26.

| Period | Target close | Actual lock | On-time? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jun 25 | 08 Jul 25 | 07 Jul 25 | Yes |
| Jul 25 | 07 Aug 25 | 06 Aug 25 | Yes |
| Aug 25 | 08 Sep 25 | 12 Sep 25 | No |
| Sep 25 | 07 Oct 25 | 07 Oct 25 | Yes |
| Oct 25 | 07 Nov 25 | 06 Nov 25 | Yes |
| Nov 25 | 05 Dec 25 | 05 Dec 25 | Yes |
| Dec 25 | 08 Jan 26 | 19 Jan 26 | No |
| Jan 26 | 06 Feb 26 | 05 Feb 26 | Yes |
| Feb 26 | 06 Mar 26 | 06 Mar 26 | Yes |
| Mar 26 | 08 Apr 26 | 08 Apr 26 | Yes |
| Apr 26 | 07 May 26 | 13 May 26 | No |
| May 26 | 05 Jun 26 | 05 Jun 26 | Yes |
| **On-time** | | **9 of 12** | **75%** |

Five things to notice:

1. **At 75% the card is well under the 90% alert, and three late closes in twelve months is a process problem, not bad luck.** A single late close in a year is forgivable: a system outage, a key person off sick, a one-off acquisition that complicated consolidation. Three in twelve, especially when two of them (Dec and Apr) cluster around quarter and year boundaries, points to a structural cause rather than chance. The card does not diagnose the cause, but the pattern in the table is diagnostic in itself: the late periods are the heavy ones, which suggests the close process bends under load and the team is operating without enough slack for the quarters that carry extra work.

2. **The December close ran 11 days late, and a slipping year-end close has consequences far beyond finance.** December is the year-end period for a calendar-year business, carrying accruals, reversals, inventory counts, and audit-prep work on top of the normal monthly load. An 11-day slip there delays the entire annual reporting chain: audited statements, tax provisioning, board reporting, and bank-covenant certifications all wait on a locked year-end. The card surfaces the slip as a single data point, but a Controller reading it knows the December miss is the one that actually hurts, because everything downstream of year-end is now compressed or late.

3. **The rolling 12-period window means each new on-time close slowly heals the rate while each late one drops it by 8.3 points.** Because the denominator is fixed at 12, every period is worth exactly 8.3% of the score, and the metric has a memory of one year. The Aug 25 miss will roll off the window after the Jul 26 close, lifting the rate if Jul 26 lands on time. This is the right design for a discipline metric: it rewards sustained improvement and does not let a single good month erase a year of slippage, nor a single bad month define the team forever. Watching the rate trend upward over several months is the signal that a process fix has actually taken hold.

4. **A falling close rate is an early warning that the numbers feeding every other card may be provisional.** This is the subtle reason the card matters to people who never touch the close. If periods are closing late, it usually means the underlying data was not clean or complete on time: unreconciled bank accounts, unposted invoices, [open reconciliation items](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/open-reconciliation-items), [transactions failing to save with SuiteScript errors](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/transactions-failing-to-save-suitescript-errors), or [transaction imbalances](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/transaction-imbalances) that block the lock. So a low close rate is a quiet flag that the revenue, AR, and inventory numbers on other cards may still be moving. The most useful operational pattern is to treat a falling close rate as a prompt to check the upstream data-quality cards rather than as a standalone finance-team scolding.

5. **On a OneWorld account, group close depends on every sub closing, so one slow sub caps the whole group.** If the US parent closes reliably on day four but the Canadian sub routinely needs day seven because it shares a part-time controller, the group cannot consolidate until the slow sub is done, and the group-level close rate inherits the worst performer. The card can be configured to measure per-sub close so the bottleneck is visible, which turns "the group is late again" into "the CA sub is the constraint", a far more actionable statement. The fix for a OneWorld close-rate problem is almost always found by decomposing to the slowest sub and resourcing or automating that specific bottleneck.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

NetSuite tracks accounting periods and their lock status natively, so the raw inputs to this card are auditable, but NetSuite does not ship an out-of-the-box "on-time rate" report, which is one reason the card exists. The card compares each period's lock timestamp to its configured target. The closest native views to triangulate against:

> **Setup → Accounting → Manage Accounting Periods** (the period list with lock and close checkboxes; the source of truth for which periods are locked)
> **Setup → Accounting → Manage G/L Audit Numbering** (period locking sequence for accounts that use audit numbering)
> **System Notes / saved search on Accounting Period** (the lock-status change timestamps that establish when each period was actually closed)
> **Reports → Audit Trail / System Notes** (who locked the period and when, for the on-time comparison)
> **Saved search**: an Accounting Period saved search exporting period name, locked status, and the system-note timestamp of the lock event, compared against your stated close calendar

The crucial input that does not live in NetSuite is the target close date. NetSuite knows when a period was locked, but it does not natively store your team's commitment to close by, say, business day five. That target is a workspace configuration in Vortex IQ. If the card and your own assessment disagree on whether a period was on-time, the difference is almost always the target date definition rather than the lock timestamp.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from your own close tracking:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Target close date definition** | Either | NetSuite does not store your target; the card uses the workspace-configured calendar. If your spreadsheet uses a different target (calendar day vs business day, different cut), the on-time judgement differs. |
| **Lock vs close distinction** | Either | NetSuite has separate "close" and "lock" states per transaction type. Whether the card counts the AP/AR sub-close, the GL lock, or full lock changes the timestamp used. |
| **Business-day vs calendar-day target** | Either | A "day five" target lands on different calendar dates depending on weekends and holidays; the card and a manual tracker must use the same convention. |
| **Per-sub vs group close** | Either | On OneWorld, measuring group close (all subs locked) versus per-sub close gives different on-time counts. |
| **Re-opened periods** | Card may read lower | A period re-opened for an adjustment and re-locked may be timestamped at the later lock, counting as late even if the original close was on-time. |
| **Adjustment and year-end periods** | Either | Special periods (adjustment, year-end) may be configured in or out of the 12-period count, shifting the denominator. |
| **Time zone of the timestamp** | Small | A lock at 11pm local on the target day can read as the next day in UTC; the card normalises to the workspace time zone. |
| **Holiday calendar** | Small | The target date depends on the workspace holiday calendar; a mismatch shifts the on-time boundary by a day. |

The reconciliation is usually exact once the target close calendar and the lock-versus-close definition are agreed. A persistent disagreement nearly always traces to one of those two definitional choices, not to the underlying period data.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Where does the target close date come from?**
From your workspace configuration, not from NetSuite. NetSuite records when a period was locked but has no native concept of "the date we promised to close by". Vortex IQ stores your stated close calendar (for example, business day five of the following month) and compares the lock timestamp against it. If you have never configured a target, the card cannot judge on-time, so this is the first setup step. Most teams set it to match the close commitment they already give the board.

**What counts as "closed" on a OneWorld account?**
Configurable, and the choice matters. You can measure group close, where the period counts as on-time only when every subsidiary is locked and the group is consolidated, or per-sub close, where each subsidiary is judged against its own target. Group close is the right board-level metric; per-sub close is the right operational metric because it isolates the slow sub. Many workspaces track both: the group rate for reporting and the per-sub rate for process management.

**Why is a non-finance owner shown this card?**
Because a late close is an early warning that the numbers feeding every other card may still be provisional. If the books are not locked on time, it often means the underlying data was not clean (unreconciled accounts, unposted invoices, failed automations), which means revenue, AR, and inventory figures may still move. An owner watching this card knows when to trust the rest of the dashboard as final versus when to treat it as a work-in-progress estimate.

**Does re-opening a period to post an adjustment count against me?**
It can, depending on configuration. If a closed period is re-opened for a late adjustment and re-locked, the card may pick up the later lock timestamp and count the period as late. Most teams configure the card to honour the original on-time lock when a re-open is a planned audit adjustment rather than a process failure. Decide the policy up front, because a strict "latest lock wins" rule will penalise normal audit activity.

**Single-subsidiary versus OneWorld behaviour?**
On a single-sub account the metric is simply that entity's close against its target, with no group-versus-sub distinction. OneWorld adds the per-sub-versus-group choice and the dependency where the slowest sub caps the group close. The interpretation discipline (decompose to the bottleneck sub) only applies to OneWorld; single-sub accounts read the rate directly.

**How does this differ from the live close-status card?**
This card is the trailing scorecard over 12 periods; [Accounting Period Close Status](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/accounting-period-close-status) is the live state of the current period, and [Accounting Period Close Past Deadline](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/accounting-period-close-past-deadline) is the acute alert when the current period has already missed its target. Read them together: the live cards tell you about today's close, this card tells you whether today's close fits a healthy long-run pattern or a worrying one.

**Why does my rate not improve immediately after we fix the process?**
Because the window is a rolling 12 periods, so an old late close keeps dragging the rate until it rolls off the back of the window. A process fix shows up gradually as on-time closes accumulate and late ones age out. This is intentional: a discipline metric should require sustained good behaviour to recover, not reward a single good month. Watch the rate trend upward over several periods as confirmation the fix held.

**Does multi-currency or FX affect this card?**
No. This is a process-timing metric with no currency or value dimension. FX, multi-currency, and revaluation timing are irrelevant to whether a period locked on or before its target date. The only indirect link is that complex multi-currency consolidations can take longer to close, which would show up as later lock timestamps, but the card measures the timestamp, not the cause.

**B2B versus DTC, does the business model change the reading?**
The metric itself is model-agnostic, but the close difficulty is not. B2B businesses with heavy AR, terms-based invoicing, and inventory typically have more to reconcile at close than a lean DTC operation, so a B2B close legitimately takes longer and the target should reflect that. The card judges you against your own target, so set a target that matches your operational reality rather than a generic "five-day close" that does not fit a complex B2B book.

**What is a realistic target on-time rate?**
A well-run finance function should sit at or above 90% (no more than one late close per year), which is why the alert is set there. World-class teams run at 100% with a comfortable buffer. Below 75% the close process is effectively unreliable and downstream reporting cannot be planned around it. The right interpretation is directional: a rate that is stable and high is healthy, a rate that is falling is the signal to investigate, regardless of the absolute level.

**NetSuite versus other ERPs on close discipline?**
NetSuite's period-management and locking model is robust and gives a clean audit trail of lock timestamps, which is what this card relies on. Oracle ERP Cloud and SAP S/4HANA both have sophisticated financial-close-management modules (Oracle's Financial Consolidation and Close, SAP's Group Reporting and Close cockpit) that track close tasks natively, sometimes with built-in on-time reporting. NetSuite leaves the on-time scoring to the user, which is the gap Vortex IQ fills, and the advantage is that the same card sits alongside the upstream data-quality cards that explain why a close slipped, rather than living in a separate close-management silo.
