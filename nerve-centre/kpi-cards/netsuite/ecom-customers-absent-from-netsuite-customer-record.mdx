> Ecom buyers with no matching NetSuite Customer record. Without a customer entity, the Cash Sale or Invoice cannot post under the correct entity and revenue recognition rules cannot apply.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> A count of customers who have bought on your commerce platform but have no corresponding Customer record in NetSuite. The customer record is the anchor for everything downstream: without it, a Cash Sale or Invoice for that buyer cannot post under the correct entity, the revenue cannot be attributed to a customer, AR cannot be tracked, and ASC 606 revenue-recognition rules have nothing to attach to. An ecom buyer with no NetSuite customer is a transaction your books cannot fully account for. A rising count means your customer-sync is breaking, and every absent customer is a downstream posting waiting to fail.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | The number of distinct commerce-platform customers, with at least one order, who have no matching NetSuite Customer record. The headline counts those with active orders; the full list is available for remediation. |
| **Match basis** | A commerce customer is matched to a NetSuite customer by email, account code, or a mapped customer ID. Absence means no match was found by any configured key, not merely a name spelling difference. |
| **Active-order qualifier** | The alert focuses on absent customers who have placed orders, because those are the ones whose transactions cannot post correctly. A registered storefront account that never bought is lower priority. |
| **Currency** | n/a; this is a count. The downstream revenue at risk is the value of these customers' orders in reporting currency. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. On OneWorld the absent customer cannot be assigned to a subsidiary, which is itself part of the problem, the transaction has no entity to post under. |
| **Time window** | `7D` (customers seen ordering in the last 7 days without a NetSuite record) |
| **Alert trigger** | `greater than 10 with active orders absent`, driven by the customer-sync sentiment key |
| **Roles** | owner, finance, marketing, operations |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

Calculated automatically by comparing your commerce customer list against your NetSuite Customer records. The card takes every customer who has placed an order on the storefront in the window, looks for a matching NetSuite Customer by the configured identity keys, and counts those for which no match exists. The result is a single number plus a list of the absent customers and the orders attached to them, so the team can create the missing records or fix the sync that should have created them.

The match is only as reliable as the identity keys that link the two systems. The card looks for an email match, an account-code match, or a mapped customer ID, in that order of preference, and reports a customer as absent only when none of them resolves. A customer that exists in NetSuite under a slightly different email is a near-miss that better identity hygiene would catch; a customer that genuinely has no record is a true absence. The alert fires above ten absent-with-orders because beyond that level the customer-sync is failing systematically rather than dropping the occasional edge case. See the At a glance summary above for what the metric tracks and the worked example below for a typical reading.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A UK lifestyle brand on NetSuite (single subsidiary, GBP) selling through Shopify Plus DTC and a small B2B portal. Annual revenue ~$24M (reported in GBP, here shown in local currency). New customer records are created in NetSuite by a scheduled customer-sync script that runs hourly. Window: 7 days ending 22 Jun 26.

**Headline:** 47 ecom customers with active orders have no NetSuite Customer record. Orders attached to them: 61 orders, £38,400 of revenue with no entity to post under.

| Cause cluster | Absent customers | Orders | Order value | Root cause |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Guest checkout, no account | 28 | 31 | £18,200 | Buyer never created a storefront account |
| Customer-sync script stalled | 12 | 22 | £14,100 | Hourly sync last ran 26 hours ago |
| Email mismatch (near-duplicate) | 5 | 6 | £4,300 | Ordered under personal email, billed under company |
| New B2B account, manual setup pending | 2 | 2 | £1,800 | Awaiting credit approval before record creation |
| **Total (this card)** | **47** | **61** | **£38,400** | |

Five things to notice:

1. **£38,400 of revenue this week has no customer entity to post against, which means none of it can flow cleanly into the books.** Each of these 61 orders, when it reaches NetSuite, needs a Customer record to become a Cash Sale or Invoice. Without one, the posting either fails outright (the script throws because the entity is missing) or, worse, gets booked against a generic catch-all customer that destroys all customer-level reporting. On every other dashboard these are normal orders; only the cross-system match reveals that they are orphaned at the entity level. The count rising past the alert line is the signal that the customer-sync, the most foundational link between storefront and ledger, has started to break.

2. **The clusters demand completely different responses, which is why the breakdown matters more than the headline.** The 28 guest-checkout customers are arguably working as intended, the buyer chose not to create an account, and the fix is a policy decision about how to represent guest orders in NetSuite (a per-order customer, a guest umbrella customer per channel, or auto-creating a record from the order's billing details). The 12 customers behind the stalled sync are a genuine break that needs immediate attention. Treating all 47 as one problem would either over-engineer the guest case or under-react to the sync failure. Reading the clusters separately turns a scary number into four right-sized actions.

3. **The stalled-sync cluster is the dangerous one and it is hiding £14,100 of real B2B-style revenue.** The hourly customer-sync script last ran 26 hours ago, so for over a day no new customer records have been created. The 12 customers and 22 orders in this cluster are buyers who would normally have records by now. The root cause is almost certainly the same family of failure the [SuiteScript Execution Errors (24h)](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/suitescript-execution-errors-24h) card watches: the sync script threw and stopped, or a credential expired, so the customer pipeline silently stalled. Until it is restarted, this cluster grows every hour, and so does the count of orders that cannot post.

4. **The downstream consequence is precisely the billing gap the next card measures.** A customer with no NetSuite record cannot have a Cash Sale or Invoice created for them, so their orders become the unmatched, unbilled orders that the [Ecom Orders Missing Matching NetSuite Cash Sale / Invoice](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ecom-orders-missing-matching-netsuite-cash-sale-invoice) card surfaces. This card is the upstream cause; that card is the downstream effect. Fixing the absent customers here removes a large share of the unbilled orders there. The two cards together trace the leak from its origin (no customer entity) to its financial consequence (no revenue document), which is far more actionable than seeing either symptom in isolation.

5. **Marketing and operations care about this as much as finance, which is why four roles own it.** An absent customer record is not only an accounting problem. Marketing cannot segment, retarget, or measure lifetime value on a customer NetSuite does not know exists, so the absent population is invisible to every customer-analytics view downstream of the ERP. Operations cannot apply account-level fulfilment rules, credit terms, or service history. The £38,400 is the finance framing, but the relationship and analytics cost compounds over time: a customer who buys repeatedly while remaining absent is a high-value relationship the business is flying blind on. The card's value is that it catches the foundational data gap before it metastasises across every downstream system.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

NetSuite holds the Customer records; the buyers live on the commerce platform. The NetSuite side of this card reconciles against the customer list and the customer-creation audit trail. The closest native views to triangulate against:

> **Lists → Relationships → Customers** (the master customer list; the population a commerce buyer is matched against)
> **Reports → Customers/Receivables → Customer List** (an exportable list to diff against a commerce customer export)
> **Saved search**: a Customer saved search by date created, to confirm whether new records have stopped appearing (a flat line is the signature of a stalled sync)
> **Customization → Scripting → Script Execution Log** (if a customer-sync script is the creator, its errors and last-run time live here)
> **Setup → Company → System Notes** (to see who or what created recent customer records and when, useful when a sync is suspected of having stalled)

The headline difference between this card and any NetSuite report is that NetSuite cannot, on its own, know about a customer who is not in NetSuite. The absence is invisible from inside the ERP by definition; it only becomes visible when the commerce customer list is held up against the NetSuite list, which is exactly the cross-system comparison the card performs continuously.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a manual customer diff:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Match key choice** | Either | The card matches on email, account code, or mapped ID; a manual diff on name alone will find more false absences (same person, different spelling) or miss true ones (different person, same name). |
| **Guest checkout policy** | Either | Whether guest orders should create a customer record is a policy choice. If your policy is to represent guests under an umbrella customer, the card can be configured to treat them as matched rather than absent. |
| **Sync latency** | Card may overstate | A customer created in the storefront minutes ago may not yet have synced to NetSuite. The 7-day window reduces this noise, but very recent buyers can briefly read as absent before the next sync run. |
| **Near-duplicate records** | Either | A buyer who exists in NetSuite under a different email reads as absent on email match but is really a near-duplicate. Better identity hygiene moves these from absent to matched. |
| **Inactive customers** | Either | A customer record that exists but is set inactive may or may not count as present depending on the workspace rule; the default treats an inactive record as not a valid posting target. |
| **B2B contact vs company** | Either | In B2B, the person who checks out (a contact) may differ from the billed entity (the company customer). The card needs the contact-to-company mapping to match these correctly. |

**Cross-connector reconciliation, the killer cross-channel finding:**

This card is a pure cross-connector metric: it exists only because the commerce customer list and the NetSuite customer list can be compared. Neither system can produce it alone.

| Card | Expected relationship | What the comparison reveals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [shopify.customers](/nerve-centre/shopify/customers) | Identity diff | The Shopify customer population diffed against NetSuite customers; the unmatched buyers are this card's headline. |
| [bigcommerce.customers](/nerve-centre/bigcommerce/customers) | Identity diff | Same for BigCommerce; multi-storefront brands accumulate absences fastest because each storefront is a separate identity source. |
| [adobe_commerce.customers](/nerve-centre/adobe_commerce/customers) | Identity diff | Adobe Commerce B2B company accounts add a contact-versus-company matching layer that is a frequent source of false absence. |
| [shopify.orders](/nerve-centre/shopify/orders) | Revenue-at-risk measure | The value of orders attached to absent customers is the revenue that has no entity to post under. |
| [Ecom Orders Missing Matching NetSuite Cash Sale / Invoice](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ecom-orders-missing-matching-netsuite-cash-sale-invoice) | Causal | Absent customers are a leading cause of unbilled orders; a rise here predicts a rise there. |
| [google_ads.cost](/nerve-centre/google_ads/cost) | Attribution-blind frame | You are paying to acquire customers your ERP cannot see, so acquisition cost and lifetime value cannot be reconciled for the absent cohort. |

The killer cross-channel finding is that a brand can be paying to acquire customers, recognising their orders on the storefront, and shipping their goods, while a measurable slice of those customers do not exist in the system of record at all. On this brand, 47 buyers and £38,400 of weekly revenue were anchored to nothing in NetSuite. The storefront reports them as customers; the ad platforms report them as conversions; NetSuite has never heard of them. Only Vortex IQ, holding the commerce customer list against the NetSuite customer list, can say "these 47 buyers are spending money you cannot attribute, on orders you cannot post, under entities that do not exist". That single comparison closes the gap between marketing's customer count, the storefront's order count, and finance's customer ledger, three numbers that should reconcile and, on a broken sync, silently do not. No connector alone can surface it; the join is the product.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Are guest-checkout buyers always counted as absent?**
By default yes, because a guest order genuinely has no customer account to match. Whether that is a problem depends on your policy. Many brands choose to auto-create a NetSuite customer from the order's billing details, or to post all guest orders under a per-channel umbrella customer. Once that policy is configured, the card treats guests as matched and stops counting them, leaving only the true sync failures. Decide the policy first, then read the card against it.

**Why can't the order just post without a customer record?**
Because NetSuite's transaction model requires an entity. A Cash Sale or Invoice must reference a Customer, and that customer must belong to a subsidiary on OneWorld. Without the record there is nothing to post the revenue against, no AR to track, and nothing for revenue-recognition rules to attach to. Some integrations work around this by posting to a generic catch-all customer, but that destroys all customer-level reporting and is a worse outcome than the absence itself.

**The count jumped suddenly. What happened?**
Almost certainly a stalled customer-sync. Check when new NetSuite customer records last appeared (a flat creation line is the signature) and check the [SuiteScript Execution Errors (24h)](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/suitescript-execution-errors-24h) card for a failing sync script. A sudden jump is rarely many independent absences; it is usually one broken pipeline dropping every new customer until it is restarted.

**How does the email-match work for customers who use different emails?**
The card matches on email first, then account code, then a mapped customer ID. A buyer who orders under a personal email but is billed under a company record in NetSuite will not match on email and may read as absent. This is a near-duplicate, not a true absence, and the fix is identity hygiene: map the personal email to the company record, or maintain a contact-to-company link. The card cannot match what the data does not connect.

**We are on OneWorld. How does subsidiary assignment factor in?**
An absent customer has no subsidiary, which is itself a blocker: even if you create the record, you must assign it to the correct subsidiary before transactions can post. The related [OneWorld Customers Missing Subsidiary](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/oneworld-customers-missing-subsidiary) card catches customers who exist but lack a subsidiary. Together the two cards cover the full "cannot post under the correct entity" failure: this one for no record, that one for a record with no entity.

**Does FX matter for this card?**
Not for the count, which is currency-independent. FX matters only for the revenue-at-risk figure attached to the absent customers' orders, which is translated to reporting currency at the dashboard cadence. The decision to fix an absence is driven by the count and the cause, not by the FX-translated value.

**B2B versus DTC, does the card read differently?**
Yes. DTC absences are dominated by guest checkout and are often a policy question. B2B absences are more serious and more structural: a B2B buyer is usually a company on terms, so an absent B2B customer means on-account orders with no entity to post against and no AR being tracked, which is a direct revenue and credit-control gap. B2B also introduces the contact-versus-company matching layer, where the person checking out differs from the billed entity, a frequent source of false absence that needs the contact-to-company mapping to resolve.

**Could a customer exist but still be counted as absent?**
Yes, in two cases. First, a record under a different identity key that the configured match cannot resolve (the near-duplicate case). Second, a record set inactive, which the default rule treats as not a valid posting target. Both are configuration and data-hygiene issues rather than true absences, and both are worth resolving because they pollute the count and obscure the genuine sync failures underneath.

**Does this affect revenue recognition?**
Directly. ASC 606 revenue recognition operates on revenue attributed to a customer through a posted Cash Sale or Invoice. If the customer record does not exist, the document cannot be created, the revenue cannot be attributed, and recognition rules have nothing to apply to. An absent customer is therefore not just a data gap, it is a revenue-recognition gap: the revenue is real, the buyer paid, but the books cannot recognise it correctly until the entity exists.

**How does this relate to the orders-missing-invoice card?**
It is the upstream cause. A customer with no record cannot have a Cash Sale or Invoice, so their orders necessarily become unbilled and appear on the [Ecom Orders Missing Matching NetSuite Cash Sale / Invoice](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ecom-orders-missing-matching-netsuite-cash-sale-invoice) card. Fixing absences here clears a large share of the unbilled orders there. Read this card as the root-cause view and that card as the financial-consequence view.

**NetSuite vs other ERPs on customer-sync gaps?**
Every ERP that integrates with a separate storefront has this gap, because the storefront and the ERP each maintain their own customer identity and the two must be reconciled. SAP S/4HANA uses business partner master data and Oracle ERP Cloud uses trading-community party records; in both, a storefront customer must be matched or created before transactions can post. The failure mode is identical regardless of ERP, and Vortex IQ catches it the same way everywhere: by holding the commerce customer list against the ERP customer list rather than trusting either system to know about the other.
