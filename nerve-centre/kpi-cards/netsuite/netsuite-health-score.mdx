## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> A composite 0-100 health gauge for your whole NetSuite account. It blends four independent signals: how error-free your integrations are, how fast your saved searches and SuiteScript jobs run, how clean your AR aging looks, and how tightly your ERP inventory matches commerce availability. A reading below 70 means at least one of the four sub-scores is dragging the account, and the gauge tells you where to look first.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | A weighted blend of four sub-scores, each scaled 0-100, then combined into one headline gauge. Default weighting is roughly equal across the four pillars, configurable per workspace. |
| **The four pillars** | Integration-error-free (SuiteScript and import error rate), saved-search-fast (query and scheduled-job latency), AR-aging-low (share of receivables past terms), inventory-variance-low (ERP vs commerce on-hand divergence). |
| **Currency** | n/a. The headline is a unitless index; the AR pillar reads underlying balances in reporting currency. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. Can be read consolidated or per subsidiary on OneWorld. |
| **Time window** | `RT/7D` (real-time pillars blended with a 7-day trailing view for the rate-based pillars) |
| **Alert trigger** | Headline gauge less than 70 |
| **Roles** | owner, finance, operations |
| **Chart type** | Gauge (0-100), colour-banded into red, amber and green zones with the four pillar sub-scores listed beneath |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

Calculated automatically from your NetSuite data. The card samples four independent health signals, normalises each onto a 0-100 scale so they are directly comparable, and then combines them into one headline index. Each pillar measures a different failure mode: integration health catches broken automation, saved-search speed catches a degrading account that is about to time out, AR aging catches collections drift, and inventory variance catches the system-of-record falling out of step with what the storefront is selling.

Because the four pillars are independent, the headline can fall for very different reasons, and the card always shows the pillar breakdown so the drop is diagnosable rather than mysterious. See the At a glance summary above for what each pillar tracks and the worked example below for a typical reading. The headline alert fires when the blended score crosses below 70, but the action is always driven by the lowest pillar, not the headline itself.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A mid-market home-and-garden brand on NetSuite OneWorld. Two subsidiaries: a US parent (USD) selling DTC through Shopify and a UK sub (GBP) selling through BigCommerce and a wholesale channel. Annual revenue ~$45M. Reading taken 12 Jun 26, blended over the trailing 7 days. Reporting currency USD.

| Pillar | Weight | Raw signal | Pillar sub-score | Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Integration-error-free | 25% | 4 SuiteScript save failures in 7 days, 1 stuck CSV import | 62 | 15.5 |
| Saved-search-fast | 25% | 2 scheduled searches over the latency budget, 1 timeout | 71 | 17.8 |
| AR-aging-low | 25% | 22% of AR past terms, 2 invoices over 60 days | 78 | 19.5 |
| Inventory-variance-low | 25% | 6.4% aggregate ERP-vs-commerce variance | 58 | 14.5 |
| **Headline NetSuite Health Score** | **100%** | | | **67** |

Five things to notice:

1. **The headline of 67 is below the 70 alert threshold, but the headline number is not the story - the pillar breakdown is.** A board member who only reads "67, amber" learns nothing actionable. The operator who reads the breakdown sees immediately that inventory-variance (58) and integration-error-free (62) are the two anchors dragging the account, while AR aging (78) is broadly healthy. The whole point of a composite gauge is that it compresses four monitoring surfaces into one glance, but it must always decompose on demand, otherwise it is a vanity metric. The card lists the four sub-scores directly beneath the gauge so the diagnosis takes the same two seconds as the headline read. Treat the headline as the smoke alarm and the pillar list as the floor plan that tells you which room is on fire.

2. **Inventory-variance at 58 is the worst pillar, and it points straight at a sibling card for the detail.** A 6.4% aggregate ERP-vs-commerce variance is above the 5% line that the [ERP-vs-Ecom Inventory Variance %](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/erp-vs-ecom-inventory-variance) hero card alerts on, which means oversell risk is live on this account right now. The health-score gauge is deliberately shallow: it tells you the pillar is red, then hands you off to the dedicated variance card, which in turn rolls up from the SKU-level [SKUs with NetSuite-vs-Ecom Inventory Drift](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/inventory-sync-drift) detail table. This is the intended drill path. The composite exists for the executive; the variance card exists for the operations lead; the drift table exists for the person who fixes the individual SKU sync.

3. **Integration-error-free at 62 is the pillar most likely to be silently catastrophic.** Four SuiteScript save failures and a stuck CSV import sound minor, but a failing integration is not a degraded experience, it is missing data. If the failures are commerce orders not landing as Sales Orders, then revenue is being recognised late or not at all, and three other cards (open Sales Orders, invoiced revenue, the commerce-orders-without-NetSuite-SO reconciliation) will all be quietly wrong downstream. A slow saved search annoys a user; a broken integration corrupts the books. For that reason many finance teams weight the integration pillar higher than the default 25% once they understand that this pillar is the only one of the four that can make every other NetSuite number untrustworthy.

4. **AR-aging at 78 is the healthy pillar, and it is worth understanding why it does not need to be 100.** A perfect AR score would mean zero receivables past terms, which almost never happens in a real business with net-30 and net-60 customers. A score in the high 70s with 22% of AR past terms is normal for a business with a meaningful B2B book, because B2B customers routinely pay a few days late and that is priced into the relationship. The pillar is designed to flag deterioration, not to demand perfection, so the band that maps 22%-past-terms to a 78 is calibrated against what a healthy distributor actually looks like, not against an unachievable zero. If this pillar fell to the 50s, the drill path is the AR aging family of cards, starting with [A/R Aging Detail](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/a-r-aging-detail) and [AR > 60 Days](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ar-aging-60-days).

5. **On OneWorld, the consolidated 67 can hide a much worse single subsidiary.** If the US parent is sitting at a healthy 82 and the UK sub is at 51, the consolidated blend lands around 67, and the headline looks like a uniformly mediocre account when in reality one sub is fine and the other is in trouble. This is the classic averaging trap of any composite metric on a multi-entity group. The fix is to read the gauge per subsidiary using the dashboard filter, then act on the worst sub first. The card supports both consolidated and per-sub reads precisely so the group view does not anaesthetise you to a single failing entity. The most useful operating pattern is a consolidated gauge on the executive dashboard plus a small-multiple of per-sub gauges on the operations dashboard.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

NetSuite does not expose a single "health score". This card is a Vortex IQ composite, so there is no native report that returns 67. Instead you reconcile each pillar against its own native surface, and the gauge is correct if all four pillars reconcile. The closest native views to triangulate each pillar against:

> **Customization → Scripting → Script Execution Log** and **Setup → Import/Export → View CSV Import Status** (the integration-error pillar; failed saves and stuck imports live here)
> **Customization → Saved Searches → All Saved Searches** with execution timing, plus **Setup → Company → Scheduled Script Status** (the saved-search-fast pillar)
> **Reports → Customers/Receivables → A/R Aging Summary** (the AR-aging pillar; the share past terms)
> **Reports → Inventory → Inventory Activity Detail** cross-referenced with the commerce platform's available-to-sell export (the inventory-variance pillar)
> **Saved search**: a transaction saved search filtered to imbalanced transactions, which should always return zero on a healthy account (a direct input to the integration pillar)

Because the composite blends real-time and trailing-7-day signals, an exact tie-out against a single point-in-time NetSuite report is not expected. Reconcile the direction and the pillar ranking, not the second decimal place.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a manual pillar-by-pillar calculation:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pillar weighting** | Either | Default is roughly equal across four pillars; a workspace that weights integration health higher will read differently from one using equal weights. The headline is a policy choice, so two correctly-configured workspaces can score the same account differently. |
| **Normalisation bands** | Either | Each pillar maps a raw signal (e.g. 22% AR past terms) onto a 0-100 sub-score via calibrated bands. Tuning the bands shifts the sub-score without any change in the underlying business. |
| **Time-window blend** | Either | Real-time pillars (variance, imbalances) and trailing-7-day pillars (error rate, search latency) are blended; a spike that has just cleared still influences the trailing pillars for a few days. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Either | Consolidated blend versus per-sub read can differ sharply when one subsidiary is much healthier than another. |
| **Saved-search latency baseline** | Either | The latency budget is relative to your account's own historical baseline; a naturally heavy account and a light account are scored against their own norms, not an absolute millisecond figure. |
| **AR currency translation** | Small | The AR pillar reads balances in reporting currency; FX revaluation moves the underlying balances slightly even when no collections changed. |
| **Sampling cadence** | Small | The card samples on a schedule; a failure that occurred and self-cleared between samples may not register, which is intentional noise suppression. |

**Cross-connector reconciliation:**

The health score is inherently cross-connector on two of its four pillars. The inventory-variance pillar only exists because Vortex IQ holds both the NetSuite on-hand and the commerce available-to-sell in the same graph, and the integration pillar partly measures whether commerce orders are successfully becoming NetSuite Sales Orders. No native NetSuite report can produce either pillar, because NetSuite cannot see the storefront side of the join.

| Card | Expected relationship | What the comparison reveals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ERP-vs-Ecom Inventory Variance %](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/erp-vs-ecom-inventory-variance) | Drives a pillar | The aggregate variance gauge is the inventory pillar; if it is red, the health score is dragged regardless of how clean the other three pillars are. |
| [Commerce Orders Without a NetSuite SO](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/commerce-orders-without-netsuite-so) | Drives a pillar | Orders captured on the storefront but missing in NetSuite are an integration failure that NetSuite alone cannot detect, because the missing record is invisible to NetSuite by definition. |
| [Transaction Imbalances](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/transaction-imbalances) | Drives a pillar | A non-zero imbalance count is a direct, unambiguous integration-health failure and should always pull the integration pillar down hard. |

The killer cross-channel framing is that the NetSuite Health Score is the only number that catches the two most expensive silent failures in a commerce-plus-ERP stack at once: orders that never reached the books, and stock the storefront thinks it has but the ERP says is gone. Both are invisible inside NetSuite alone and invisible inside the commerce platform alone. Vortex IQ holds both systems in one graph, so the gauge can fall the moment either side drifts, and the executive sees a single amber number days before the month-end close would have surfaced it manually.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Why is the headline a single number when the four pillars measure totally different things?**
Because the executive audience for this card wants a two-second read, not a four-panel dashboard. The composite exists to answer "is my NetSuite account broadly healthy right now, yes or no", and to do that it has to collapse four surfaces into one. The honest tension is that any composite hides detail, which is why this card never shows the headline alone: the four pillar sub-scores are always listed beneath the gauge, and the action is always driven by the lowest pillar. Read the headline to decide whether to care; read the pillars to decide what to do.

**What does a score of exactly 70 mean, and why is the alert there?**
70 is the default boundary between "broadly fine" and "at least one pillar is dragging hard enough to warrant attention". It is not a magic number from an accounting standard; it is a calibrated operating threshold that maps to roughly one pillar being clearly unhealthy or two pillars being mildly degraded at once. The threshold is configurable per workspace. Teams that run a very tight ship often raise it to 80 so the alert fires earlier; teams in a turnaround sometimes lower it to 60 so the gauge is not permanently red while they fix structural issues.

**Can I change the weighting of the four pillars?**
Yes, and many finance teams do. The default is roughly equal weight, but the most common adjustment is to over-weight the integration-error-free pillar, because a broken integration is the only failure mode that can make every other NetSuite number untrustworthy. A retailer in a heavy growth phase might over-weight inventory-variance because oversell is their biggest near-term risk. The weighting is a policy choice that should be revisited when the business changes shape, for example after a replatform or a new subsidiary acquisition.

**OneWorld and subsidiaries, how does the score behave across entities?**
The gauge can be read consolidated or per subsidiary using the dashboard filter. The important caution is that a consolidated blend can mask a single failing entity: a healthy parent and a struggling sub average out to a mediocre-looking middle. On any OneWorld account, read the per-sub gauges at least weekly and act on the worst sub first. The consolidated number is for the board; the per-sub numbers are for operations.

**FX and multi-currency, does it move the score?**
Only through the AR-aging pillar, and only slightly. The AR pillar reads receivable balances in reporting currency, so NetSuite's FX revaluation can nudge the share-past-terms calculation as balances translate, even when no customer actually paid late or early. The other three pillars are currency-agnostic: integration errors, search latency and inventory unit variance do not care about exchange rates. If your score moves on a day with a large FX swing and nothing operational changed, check whether the AR pillar is the only one that shifted.

**B2B versus DTC, do I read the score differently?**
Yes, mostly on the AR pillar. A B2B-heavy book runs structurally higher receivables-past-terms because net-30 and net-60 customers pay late by habit, so the AR pillar is calibrated to treat a moderate past-terms share as healthy rather than alarming. A pure DTC business collects at the point of sale and should have an almost perfect AR pillar, so any AR drift on a DTC account is a much louder signal. The inventory-variance and integration pillars read the same for both models.

**Does period close affect the score?**
Indirectly. During a close, journal-entry and adjustment volume rises and saved-search load often spikes, which can briefly nudge the saved-search-fast and integration pillars. A short dip in the gauge during close week is usually a workload artefact, not a real deterioration. If the dip persists after close completes, it is real. Read the gauge in the context of where you are in the accounting calendar.

**What kinds of integration failures feed the integration pillar?**
SuiteScript save failures, stuck or partially-failed CSV imports, and any transaction that NetSuite rejected because it was malformed (the clearest example being an imbalanced transaction, where debits do not equal credits). The most expensive class is a commerce order that never became a NetSuite Sales Order, because that is missing revenue rather than a degraded process. The pillar weights hard failures (rejected or missing records) more heavily than soft ones (a retry that eventually succeeded).

**Does the saved-search pillar penalise me just for having a big account?**
No. The latency pillar is scored against your own account's historical baseline, not an absolute millisecond target. A large, naturally heavy NetSuite account and a small light one are each measured against their own norm, so the pillar flags degradation relative to how your account usually performs, not how it compares to a smaller customer. A sudden slowdown against your own baseline is the signal; raw size is not.

**Can the score be green while something is genuinely wrong?**
Yes, if the problem lives outside the four pillars. The composite only measures integration health, search speed, AR aging and inventory variance. It does not measure, for example, margin erosion, a pricing error, or a fraud pattern. A green health score means the plumbing of your NetSuite account is sound; it does not certify that every business decision feeding that plumbing was correct. Treat the gauge as an infrastructure-health read, not a business-performance read.

**How is this different from NetSuite's own system status or SuiteApp health tools?**
NetSuite's native status pages tell you whether the NetSuite service itself is up, and SuiteApp monitoring tells you about specific bundles. Neither blends your AR discipline and your ERP-vs-commerce inventory variance into the same number, because those are business-data signals, not platform-uptime signals. This card is about the health of your data and your integrations, not the uptime of the NetSuite servers. The two are complementary: NetSuite can be perfectly up while your account's data health is amber.

**NetSuite versus other ERPs, is a health score like this portable?**
The concept is portable but the pillars are NetSuite-shaped. The integration pillar is built around SuiteScript and CSV import behaviour; on SAP S/4HANA or Oracle ERP Cloud the equivalent would be IDoc or integration-framework error rates. The inventory-variance and AR pillars translate cleanly to any ERP because they are business concepts, not platform features. If you run a mixed-ERP estate, the score is comparable in spirit across entities but the integration pillar is not strictly apples-to-apples between a NetSuite sub and an SAP sub.

**Why does the gauge sometimes recover slowly after I fix the underlying issue?**
Two of the four pillars are trailing-7-day rate-based signals (integration error rate and saved-search latency), so a fix you make today still has yesterday's failures inside its window for a few more days. This trailing behaviour is deliberate: it prevents a single good hour from flipping the gauge green prematurely and gives a more honest read of whether the fix actually held. The two real-time pillars (inventory variance and imbalances) recover immediately. If you fixed an integration this morning and the gauge is still amber this afternoon, that is the trailing window doing its job, not a sign the fix failed.
