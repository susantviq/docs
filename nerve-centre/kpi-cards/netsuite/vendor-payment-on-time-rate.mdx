> Share of vendor bills paid on or before their due date. An AP-discipline and vendor-relationship signal.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> The percentage of vendor bills paid on or before their due date over the period. It is the accounts-payable counterpart to your receivables metrics: where DSO measures how well you collect, this measures how well you pay. A high rate means tight AP discipline, protected early-payment discounts, and vendors who keep you in good standing. A falling rate is an early signal of either a cash crunch, an approval bottleneck, or a broken bill-entry process, and any of those three quietly erodes your supplier relationships.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | `bills paid on or before due date ÷ total bills paid in the period`, expressed as a percentage. A bill counts as on-time when its payment date is on or before the due date derived from its terms. |
| **Due date basis** | The bill's due date as NetSuite calculates it from the vendor's payment terms (for example net 30, net 60, 2/10 net 30). Early-payment-discount terms are still measured against the net due date unless the workspace is set to measure against the discount date. |
| **Denominator** | Bills paid in the period, not bills outstanding. A bill that is overdue but unpaid does not pull this rate down until it is paid; track those separately on the AP aging cards. |
| **Currency** | n/a; this is a ratio. The underlying bills can be multi-currency and the rate is currency-independent. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. On OneWorld, computed per paying subsidiary, then consolidated. |
| **Time window** | `30D vsP` (30-day rate compared against the prior 30-day period) |
| **Alert trigger** | `less than 90%`, driven by the AP-discipline sentiment key |
| **Roles** | owner, finance |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

Calculated automatically from your NetSuite data. The card looks at every vendor bill paid in the period, compares each bill's payment date against its due date, and divides the count paid on or before due by the total count paid. The result is a single percentage shown on a gauge, with the prior period as the comparison point so you can see whether discipline is improving or slipping.

The metric is intentionally count-based rather than dollar-weighted at the headline, because a missed payment damages a vendor relationship regardless of the bill's size, and AP discipline is about consistency. A dollar-weighted view is available as a secondary read for cash-flow analysis, but the gauge tracks the simpler "what share of our commitments did we honour on time" question. The alert fires below 90% because that is the level at which on-time payment has stopped being the norm and missed dates are frequent enough to start costing discounts and goodwill. See the At a glance summary above for what the metric tracks and the worked example below for a typical reading.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A US outdoor equipment brand on NetSuite OneWorld selling through Shopify Plus and wholesale. Two subsidiaries: a US parent (USD) and a Canadian sub (CAD). Annual revenue ~$55M, annual vendor spend ~$31M across about 140 active vendors. Period: 30 days ending 22 Jun 26, compared against the prior 30 days. Reporting currency USD.

| Measure | Prior 30d | This 30d | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bills paid | 420 | 446 | +26 |
| Paid on or before due | 399 | 383 | -16 |
| **On-time rate (this card)** | **95.0%** | **85.9%** | **-9.1 pts** |
| Bills paid late | 21 | 63 | +42 |
| Early-payment discounts captured | $14,200 | $6,800 | -$7,400 |

Five things to notice:

1. **The rate dropped from 95% to 85.9% and crossed under the 90% alert line, which is the whole reason the card earns its place.** A nine-point fall in a single period is not random drift; it means something in the pay process changed. The dollar value of the bills is almost beside the point at the headline, because the damage from paying late is relational and procedural before it is financial. The gauge makes the slip visible the moment it happens rather than at quarter-end when a vendor calls to complain, and that head start is the difference between a quiet internal fix and an awkward supplier conversation.

2. **The most immediate measurable cost is the lost early-payment discounts.** Captured discounts halved from $14,200 to $6,800, a $7,400 swing in one month, roughly $89,000 annualised if the slip persists. Many vendor terms offer 2/10 net 30, meaning a 2% discount for paying within ten days. Each late payment that misses the discount window is a 2% premium paid for no reason. On a brand with $31M of annual vendor spend, capturing even half of the available 2/10 discounts is worth several hundred thousand dollars a year, and the on-time rate is the leading indicator that those discounts are being left on the table.

3. **The cause matters because the fixes are completely different.** A falling on-time rate has three classic causes and the investigation has to separate them. If it is a cash crunch, payments are being deliberately delayed and the fix is a treasury conversation, not an AP one. If it is an approval bottleneck (bills sitting in a pending-approval queue past their due date), the fix is to chase or re-route approvals, and the [Open Transactions Pending Approval / Error](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/open-transactions-pending-approval-error) card will show the backlog. If it is a bill-entry lag (bills arriving but being keyed in days after receipt, so the due date is already close or past on entry), the fix is process. On this account the pending-approval card showed a backlog spike in the same period, pointing to the second cause: an approver was on leave and bills queued behind them.

4. **Late payment damages vendor relationships in ways that do not show on any financial report until it is too late.** Vendors quietly de-prioritise late payers: longer lead times, smaller credit lines, less flexibility on rush orders, and eventually a switch to cash-on-delivery or prepayment terms that hammer your own working capital. None of that appears in the GL. The first hard sign is often a vendor tightening your terms, by which point the relationship damage is already done. The on-time rate is the only forward-looking metric that catches the behaviour while it is still reversible, which is why it sits with the owner and finance roles rather than buried in an AP clerk's worklist.

5. **Read the rate against the prior period and against your terms mix, not against a flat 100% ideal.** A 100% on-time rate is not actually the goal; a business with strong cash management may deliberately pay net-60 bills on day 60 and net-30 bills on day 30 to optimise its own cash position, and that is 100% on-time by definition. The signal is the direction and the cause. A stable 96-98% with full discount capture is excellent; a 90% that is stable and intentional can be fine; an 85.9% that just fell nine points is the alert. Pair the gauge with the discount-capture figure and the pending-approval backlog to turn the headline into an action.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

NetSuite holds every vendor bill, its terms, its due date, and its payment date, so this card reconciles against the AP reports and a bill-level saved search. The closest native views to triangulate against:

> **Reports → Vendors/Payables → A/P Aging** (the outstanding side; bills not yet paid that risk going late)
> **Reports → Vendors/Payables → A/P Register** (the transaction-level bill and payment history that drives on-time versus late)
> **Reports → Vendors/Payables → Bills Paid** (the denominator, every bill paid in the period)
> **Saved search**: a Vendor Bill saved search with a formula comparing payment date to due date, flagged on-time or late, summarised as a percentage; this is the manual equivalent of the card
> **Reports → Vendors/Payables → Vendor Payment Discounts** (the early-payment discounts captured and missed, the financial consequence)

The headline difference between this card and a manual AP report is that the card computes the on-time percentage live and compares it against the prior period automatically, whereas the native reports give you the raw bill and payment data and leave the on-time calculation to a saved search or a spreadsheet you build and maintain.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a manual AP calculation:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Due date basis** | Either | The card measures against the net due date by default; a calculation that measures against the discount date (the 2/10 window) will read lower because the discount date is earlier and harder to hit. |
| **Partial payments** | Either | A bill paid in two instalments, one on time and one late, can be counted as on-time, late, or split depending on the rule. The card uses the final-settlement date by default; a manual count may treat each payment line separately. |
| **Credit memos applied** | Small | A bill settled partly by applying a vendor credit rather than a cash payment can shift the effective settlement date; the card uses the date the bill reached zero balance. |
| **Terms changed after entry** | Either | If a vendor's terms were edited after a bill was entered, the due date NetSuite stores may differ from the terms in force when the bill was raised. The card uses the stored due date. |
| **Denominator scope** | Either | The card counts bills paid in the period; a manual report that counts bills due in the period (paid or not) uses a different denominator and will not match. |
| **Subsidiary aggregation** | Either | On OneWorld the card consolidates per-subsidiary rates; a single account-wide saved search weights all bills equally regardless of paying subsidiary. |

**Cross-connector reconciliation:**

This card is primarily a NetSuite-internal AP metric and does not have a direct commerce-platform counterpart the way the revenue cards do, because vendors and bills live entirely in the ERP. Its cross-channel value is contextual rather than a direct join: a falling on-time rate read alongside [Cash Collected](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/cash-collected) and the commerce revenue trend ([shopify.revenue](/nerve-centre/shopify/revenue), [bigcommerce.revenue](/nerve-centre/bigcommerce/revenue)) tells you whether the AP slip is a cash problem (revenue and collections also down) or a pure process problem (revenue healthy, payments still late). That distinction is the first fork in any investigation, and only the combined ERP-plus-commerce view answers it.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Is 100% on-time actually the goal?**
Not necessarily. A business with strong cash management may deliberately hold net-60 bills until day 60 to optimise its own working capital, and that is 100% on-time. The goal is intentional, consistent payment that honours your terms and captures available discounts, not paying everything the moment it arrives. The metric flags slippage from your own norm, not deviation from an unrealistic pay-immediately ideal.

**Does this measure against the discount date or the net due date?**
Against the net due date by default. If your priority is capturing 2/10-style early-payment discounts, you can set the workspace to measure against the discount date instead, which makes the rate stricter and more directly tied to discount capture. Most accounts run the net-due-date basis for the headline and watch the separate discount-capture figure for the early-payment story.

**A bill is overdue but we have not paid it yet. Does it lower this rate?**
No. The denominator is bills paid, not bills outstanding. An unpaid overdue bill does not pull this rate down until it is actually paid late. To watch the unpaid overdue exposure, use the [AP Aging (60+ days)](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ap-aging-60-days) card, which is the unpaid counterpart to this paid-bills metric.

**How are partial payments handled?**
By default the card uses the date the bill reaches a zero balance (final settlement) to judge on-time versus late. A bill paid in two instalments where the first was on time and the second was late counts as late, because the vendor was not made whole by the due date. The workspace can switch to a per-payment-line basis if your reporting convention differs.

**We are on OneWorld. How does the rate work across subsidiaries?**
It is computed per paying subsidiary and then consolidated. Each subsidiary may have its own AP team, its own approval chain, and its own cash position, so a consolidated dip can hide one healthy sub and one struggling sub. Use the per-subsidiary view to locate where the discipline slipped before assuming it is account-wide.

**How does FX affect multi-currency vendor bills?**
The on-time test is date-based, so FX does not change whether a bill was paid on time. FX does affect how much you paid: a bill in a foreign currency settles at the exchange rate on the payment date, so delaying a payment can change the dollar cost as well as the timeliness. The rate itself is currency-independent; the cash impact of a late payment is not.

**B2B vs DTC, does that change this card?**
This card is about your vendors, not your customers, so the DTC-versus-B2B distinction on the sales side does not apply directly. What does matter is your vendor mix: a brand that buys from a few large strategic suppliers reads this card differently from one with a long tail of small vendors. A single late payment to a sole-source strategic supplier is far more dangerous than several late payments to interchangeable commodity vendors, so weight your reading by vendor criticality, not just by count.

**Can an approval bottleneck show up here?**
Yes, and it is the single most common cause of a sudden drop. When bills sit in a pending-approval queue past their due date, they get paid late through no fault of the AP team. The fix is to clear or re-route the approval queue, and the [Open Transactions Pending Approval / Error](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/open-transactions-pending-approval-error) card shows the backlog directly. If both cards move together, the approval queue is your problem.

**Does this card touch revenue recognition?**
No. Vendor bills are accounts payable and expense or inventory transactions, not revenue. ASC 606 governs revenue timing on the sales side and has no bearing on when you pay your suppliers. This metric lives entirely on the AP and cash-management side of the ledger.

**Can SuiteScript or workflow automation affect the rate?**
Yes, in both directions. Approval-routing workflows and scheduled payment-run scripts can keep the rate high by paying bills automatically on their due date. A broken approval workflow or a failed scheduled payment run can also tank it by stranding bills, in which case the [SuiteScript Execution Errors (24h)](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/suitescript-execution-errors-24h) card may show the underlying failure. Automation is usually the friend of this metric, but only when it is working.

**NetSuite vs other ERPs on AP discipline?**
The concept is universal. SAP S/4HANA tracks payment behaviour through its AP aging and days-payable-outstanding analyses and supports automated payment runs with discount optimisation; Oracle ERP Cloud has payables-aging and supplier-payment dashboards. NetSuite holds all the underlying bill and payment data; what Vortex IQ adds is the live on-time percentage on a gauge with prior-period comparison and an alert, plus the ability to read it alongside the commerce revenue and collections picture to separate a cash cause from a process cause.

**Can Ask Viq break down which vendors we are paying late?**
Yes. Ask Viq can return the late-paid bills for the period grouped by vendor, so you can ask "which vendors did we pay late this month?" and see whether the slip is concentrated on one approval chain, one subsidiary, or one struggling vendor relationship, which is exactly the breakdown you need to act rather than just observe.
