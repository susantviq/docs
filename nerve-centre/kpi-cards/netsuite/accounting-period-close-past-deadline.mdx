> Accounting Period Close Past Deadline: an alert list of accounting periods whose target close date has passed without the period being locked.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> Every accounting period has a target date by which it should be closed and locked. When that date passes and the period is still open, the books for that period are still moving, which means every downstream number (revenue, margin, AR, cash) is provisional and could change. This card lists each period that is past its deadline so finance sees the risk before the board, the auditor, or a lender does.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | Each accounting period whose target close date has passed while the period remains open or only partially locked. One row per overdue period (and per subsidiary on OneWorld, since each sub locks independently). |
| **Tax treatment** | n/a directly, but an unclosed period means tax provisions and filings based on that period are not yet final. |
| **Currency** | Reporting currency for any value context shown per row. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. On OneWorld, each subsidiary period closes separately, so a single calendar period can be overdue for one sub and locked for another. |
| **Time window** | `RT` (real-time; the list reflects the current open/locked state of each period). |
| **Alert trigger** | A period is flagged when its close deadline is `more than 5 days past` without the period being locked. Tune to your close-calendar SLA. |
| **Roles** | owner, finance |
| **Chart type** | Alert list (one row per overdue period / subsidiary). |
| **Why it matters** | An overdue close means the books are still moving; every downstream financial number is provisional until the period locks. This is a Hero card because a slipping close is a leading indicator of finance-team or data problems. |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

The card compares each accounting period's target close date against the current date and the period's lock state. If the deadline has passed and the period is still open, or is only partially locked (for example AP locked but AR still open, or the period locked for one subsidiary but not others), the period appears on the list. The card surfaces how many days past deadline the period is, so the most overdue periods sort to the top. It draws on the close-calendar configuration in NetSuite, which defines target dates per period, and on the period's lock status, which NetSuite tracks per transaction-type and per subsidiary.

The logic is deliberately a list rather than a single number, because each overdue period is a distinct piece of work with its own owner and its own blockers. The close deadline itself is the workspace-configured SLA; the default alert fires once a period is more than five days past that target, which gives a normal close a few days of grace before it is treated as slipping. The card does not attempt to diagnose why a period is late; it surfaces the fact and the lateness so the controller can drill into the specific blockers (unreconciled accounts, pending approvals, failed integrations) that are holding the close open.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A multi-brand commerce group on NetSuite OneWorld. Four subsidiaries: a US parent (USD), a UK sub (GBP), an EU sub (EUR), and an APAC sub (AUD). Annual revenue roughly $120M. Snapshot taken 18 Jun 26, when the May close should have been complete. Reporting currency USD. These figures are illustrative.

| Period | Subsidiary | Target close date | Status | Days past deadline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| May 26 | US parent | 07 Jun 26 | Locked | 0 (closed on time) |
| May 26 | UK sub | 07 Jun 26 | Locked | 0 (closed on time) |
| May 26 | EU sub | 07 Jun 26 | AR still open | 11 |
| May 26 | APAC sub | 07 Jun 26 | Open | 11 |
| Apr 26 | APAC sub | 08 May 26 | AP still open | 41 |
| **Periods on this card** | | | **4 overdue rows** | |

Five things to notice:

1. **Two subsidiaries closed May on time and two did not, which is exactly why this card lists per subsidiary rather than per calendar period.** A consolidated "is May closed" flag would read as ambiguous or simply false, hiding the fact that half the group is in good shape. By breaking the list down per subsidiary, the card turns a vague group-level worry into two specific, assignable problems: the EU sub's AR is not reconciled, and the APAC sub has not started its close. The US and UK rows show as closed and drop off the alert. This is the difference between a dashboard that says "something is wrong" and one that says "these two teams need help with these two tasks".

2. **The APAC sub has both May and April overdue, and the April row at 41 days past deadline is the real emergency.** A single overdue period can be a one-off slip; two consecutive overdue periods for the same subsidiary is a pattern, and it almost always means that subsidiary's close is structurally broken, not just late. When April never closed, May was always going to be late too, because you cannot cleanly close a period while the prior one is still moving. The 41-day-old April row should be escalated immediately, because at that age the audit trail is degrading, accruals are stale, and any number reported to the board for APAC has been provisional for a month and a half. The card sorts by lateness precisely so this row floats to the top.

3. **A partially locked period is still an overdue period, and that nuance trips up a lot of teams.** The EU sub has locked some transaction types but left AR open, perhaps because a few customer payments are still being applied. From a casual glance the EU close "looks done", but because AR is open, revenue and receivables for May can still change, so May for the EU sub is not final. The card treats partial locks as overdue, which is the correct and conservative reading: until every transaction type that affects the financials is locked, the period's numbers are not safe to report. This prevents the common mistake of declaring a period closed when one stream is quietly still open.

4. **The cost of a slipping close compounds downstream, and that is why this is a Hero card.** Every other financial KPI for an open period is provisional. Revenue by subsidiary, gross margin, AR aging, cash collected, and intercompany balances are all subject to change while the period is open. If the board reviews May numbers on 18 Jun and two subs are still open, those numbers can move after the meeting, which erodes trust in the entire reporting stack. A late close also delays the consolidation, the management accounts, the lender covenant package, and the tax provision. The card is Hero-class because a slipping close is a leading indicator that something upstream (data integration, staffing, reconciliation) has gone wrong, and catching it early is far cheaper than discovering it at quarter-end.

5. **The five-day grace built into the alert keeps the card honest.** Closes routinely run a couple of days over target for benign reasons (a delayed bank feed, a late vendor bill, a public holiday in one region). Firing an alert on day one of overdue would train the team to ignore the card. By only flagging periods more than five days past deadline, the card distinguishes a normal close that ran slightly long from a close that is genuinely stuck. The threshold is tunable per workspace, because a fast-moving DTC business with a three-day close SLA wants a tighter grace window than a complex OneWorld group with a ten-day consolidation calendar. Calibrating the grace to your own close SLA is what makes the list trustworthy.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

Period close is native to NetSuite via the Manage Accounting Periods page and the period close checklist, so this card triangulates directly against the system's own close tooling:

> **Setup → Accounting → Manage Accounting Periods** (the master list of periods with their lock status per subsidiary and transaction type)
> **Period Close Checklist** (the step-by-step close workflow; an incomplete checklist is why a period stays open)
> **Reports → Financial → Period End Financials** (these reports are provisional until the period locks; running them is a quick test of whether numbers are still moving)
> **Saved search**: a search of accounting periods filtered to "closed is false" with the period end date and target close date, which is essentially the population the card filters by deadline
> **SuiteAnalytics workbook**: an Accounting Periods workbook joined to subsidiary lock status, sliced by days-past-target, gives the per-subsidiary overdue list the card displays

The headline difference is that NetSuite's Manage Accounting Periods page shows the lock state but does not, on its own, compute "how many days past the deadline" or alert you when that crosses a threshold. The card adds the deadline comparison and the alerting layer on top of NetSuite's raw lock status.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from NetSuite's period list:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Partial locks** | Card may show more | The card treats a period with any open transaction type as overdue; a glance at the period list might read it as mostly closed. |
| **Subsidiary breakout** | Card may show more rows | The card lists per subsidiary; a consolidated view shows one period that may be locked for some subs and open for others. |
| **Deadline definition** | Either | The card uses the workspace-configured target close date and grace; if NetSuite's close-calendar dates differ from the workspace setting, the overdue count can differ. |
| **Grace window** | Card may show fewer | The default flags only periods more than five days past target; a raw "is it closed" list would include periods that are late but within grace. |
| **Adjustment periods** | Either | Year-end adjustment periods have their own close timing and may appear or be excluded depending on configuration. |
| **Reopened periods** | Card may show more | A period reopened to post a late adjustment becomes open again; the card will re-flag it while a static report may still show the original close. |
| **Multi-book accounting** | Either | A period can be closed in the primary book but open in a secondary book; the card follows the configured book. |

There is no cross-connector reconciliation for this card. Period close is a NetSuite GL governance concept with no equivalent in commerce or ad platforms, so the only valid comparison is against NetSuite's own period-management tooling.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**What does "past deadline" actually mean here?**
Every accounting period has a target close date defined in your close calendar. "Past deadline" means that target date has passed and the period is still open or only partially locked. The card shows how many days overdue each period is, with the default alert firing once a period is more than five days past target.

**Why does a single calendar month appear as multiple rows?**
On OneWorld, each subsidiary closes its own period independently, so the same calendar month can be locked for some subsidiaries and open for others. The card lists one row per overdue period per subsidiary, which is what makes the problem assignable to a specific team rather than a vague group-level worry.

**What is a partial lock and why is it still flagged?**
NetSuite can lock some transaction types within a period (say AP) while leaving others open (say AR). The period "looks closed" at a glance but its financials can still change while any relevant stream is open. The card treats a partially locked period as overdue, because until everything that affects the numbers is locked, the period is not final.

**Why is this a Hero card?**
Because a slipping close is one of the most reliable leading indicators that something upstream is wrong (data integration, reconciliation, staffing) and because every downstream financial number for an open period is provisional. A late close delays consolidation, management accounts, lender packages, and tax provisions, so catching it early is high-value.

**How do I set the right deadline and grace window?**
The deadline comes from your close-calendar configuration and should reflect your real close SLA. The grace window (default five days) should match how much slack a normal close legitimately needs. A fast DTC business with a three-day close wants a tighter grace; a complex OneWorld group with a ten-day consolidation wants more. Calibrate so the card only fires on genuine slips, not routine overruns.

**Two consecutive overdue periods for the same sub, how worried should I be?**
Very. A single overdue period can be a one-off. Two in a row for the same subsidiary is a pattern that almost always means that subsidiary's close is structurally broken, because you cannot cleanly close a period while the prior one is still moving. Escalate the oldest period first; it is the root.

**Multi-currency and FX, do they affect this card?**
The overdue determination is about lock state and dates, not currency, so FX does not move it. FX matters only for any value context shown per row, which uses the reporting currency. An open period does mean the period's FX revaluation has not been finalised, which is another reason its numbers are provisional.

**B2B vs DTC, does the reading differ?**
The mechanism is identical, but B2B and multi-entity groups generally have longer, more complex closes (intercompany eliminations, more reconciliations, more subsidiaries) and so a longer legitimate close calendar. DTC single-entity businesses often close in a few days. Set the deadline and grace to your model so the card is meaningful.

**What usually causes a period to be stuck open?**
The most common blockers are unreconciled accounts (especially bank and clearing accounts), transactions pending approval, errored or unbalanced transactions, failed integration sync from commerce or banking feeds, and missing accruals. The card flags the overdue period; the sibling cards (open reconciliation items, pending approvals, transaction imbalances) help you find the specific blocker.

**Does a SuiteScript or integration failure show up here?**
Indirectly, and often. If a scheduled SuiteScript that posts daily sales summaries or applies payments fails silently, the affected accounts will not reconcile and the period cannot lock. The result shows up here as an overdue period. Pair with the SuiteScript error and saved-search cards to connect a technical failure to a delayed close.

**Can a period be reopened, and does that re-flag it?**
Yes. Finance sometimes reopens a closed period to post a late adjustment. While reopened, the period is open again and the card will re-flag it if it is past deadline. This is intentional: a reopened period's numbers are once again provisional until it relocks.

**How does this compare to other ERPs?**
Every serious ERP enforces period close, but the granularity differs. NetSuite locks per subsidiary and per transaction type, which is why the card lists at that grain. Oracle and SAP have their own period-control and posting-period mechanisms with comparable concepts; the card normalises NetSuite's model into a clear days-past-deadline list that reads the same regardless of how the underlying ERP names its lock states.

**Does the card help with year-end and adjustment periods?**
Yes, with a caveat. Year-end and adjustment periods have their own close timing and target dates, which can differ from regular monthly periods. As long as those dates are configured in the close calendar, the card flags them on the same basis. If adjustment periods are configured without a target date, they may not be evaluated for the deadline; confirm your close-calendar setup includes them.
