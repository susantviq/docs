> Total value of inventory with no sales or transfer movement in over 90 days. The leading edge of dead stock, while it is still cheap to fix.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> The dollar value of every inventory item that has not moved (no sale, no transfer, no consumption) in more than 90 days. This is the early-warning band that sits just ahead of true dead stock. Catch a SKU here and you can still markdown, bundle, or transfer it while it has resale value. Miss it and in another 90 days it becomes a write-down candidate. The card is the difference between a managed clearance and a forced disposal.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | The valuation of all inventory items whose last outbound movement (sale, fulfilment, transfer out, assembly consumption) is older than 90 days, summed at cost. Each SKU contributes `quantity on hand × unit cost`. |
| **Movement definition** | Outbound movement only. A purchase receipt or a positive adjustment does not reset the clock; only a sale, fulfilment, transfer, or build consumption does. Configurable per workspace if you want receipts to count. |
| **Threshold band** | 90 days is the default slow-moving line. Items past 180 days usually graduate to the [Dead Stock](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/dead-stock-value) cards; this card is deliberately the earlier, more actionable band. |
| **Currency** | Reporting currency, valued at cost. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. On OneWorld, slow-moving stock is computed per subsidiary location, then consolidated. |
| **Time window** | `RT` (live snapshot of current on-hand against last-movement date) |
| **Alert trigger** | `greater than 10% of inventory value`, driven by the slow-moving sentiment key |
| **Roles** | owner, finance, operations |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

Calculated automatically from your NetSuite data. The card scans every stocked item, finds the most recent outbound transaction date, and where that date is more than 90 days in the past it adds the item's current on-hand value (quantity times unit cost) to the headline. The result is a single dollar figure plus a per-SKU breakdown so operations can see exactly which lines are stalling.

The 90-day window is a rolling one, evaluated live rather than at period close, so a SKU that sells today drops out of the count immediately and a SKU that crosses the 90-day line today appears immediately. The alert compares the slow-moving total against total inventory value; once slow-moving stock exceeds roughly a tenth of the whole, the working-capital drag is large enough to warrant a clearance review. See the At a glance summary above for what the metric tracks and the worked example below for a typical reading.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A UK home and garden brand on NetSuite OneWorld selling through Shopify Plus DTC and a wholesale channel. Two subsidiaries: a UK parent (GBP) and an EU distribution sub (EUR). Annual revenue ~£42M. Snapshot 22 Jun 26. Reporting currency GBP. Total inventory value £9,800,000.

| Band | SKUs | On-hand value (GBP) | Share of inventory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Moving (last sale within 90d) | 2,140 | £8,330,000 | 85.0% |
| **Slow-moving (this card, over 90d no movement)** | **312** | **£1,176,000** | **12.0%** |
| of which 90-120 days | 168 | £540,000 | 5.5% |
| of which 121-180 days | 144 | £636,000 | 6.5% |
| Dead stock (over 180d, separate card) | 96 | £294,000 | 3.0% |

Five things to notice:

1. **At 12% of inventory value the card is over its 10% alert line, and that 12% is £1.18M of cash sitting still.** The instinct on a board is to treat slow-moving stock as a rounding error against the £8.3M that is selling, but that framing hides the cost. That £1.18M is cash the business borrowed or earned and then locked into goods nobody is buying. At a 24% annual carrying-cost rate (see [Inventory Carrying Cost](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ns-inventory-carrying-cost)) the slow-moving cohort alone burns roughly £282,000 a year just to hold. Surfacing the cohort weekly turns "we have some old stock" into a £282,000 line item the CFO can act on.

2. **The two sub-bands tell completely different stories and demand different actions.** The 90-120 day band (£540K) is still fresh enough to clear at a modest markdown, a 10-15% promotion will usually move it without destroying margin. The 121-180 day band (£636K) is closer to the dead-stock cliff; these lines need aggressive action now (deeper markdowns, bundling with fast sellers, or wholesale liquidation) because in under 60 days they cross into the write-down band where recovery is pennies on the pound. Splitting the headline into bands is what makes the card operational rather than merely descriptive. A single "slow-moving total" tells you there is a problem; the bands tell you how much runway you have to fix each part.

3. **Seasonality is the most common false alarm and the card lets you read past it.** A home and garden brand will always show a spike in slow-moving stock in mid-summer because winter SKUs (fire pits, insulated covers, snow tools) have not sold since spring and will not sell until autumn. That stock is not dead, it is dormant. The fix is to tag seasonal SKUs in the workspace so they are reported separately, or to read the card against the same date last year rather than against zero. On this account, stripping the 78 known-seasonal SKUs (£410K) out of the 121-180 band leaves £226K of genuinely-at-risk stock, which is the number operations should actually chase.

4. **Pair the cohort with the demand side before you discount.** Some of these 312 SKUs are slow-moving because they are mispriced, some because they lost search visibility, and some because they are genuinely past their moment. Before defaulting to markdown, cross-reference the cohort against open wholesale demand and against any active ad spend. A SKU that is slow-moving in DTC but has an open wholesale sales order against it (see [SOs Blocked on Inventory or Credit](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/sos-blocked-on-inventory-or-credit)) should be allocated, not discounted. A SKU that is slow-moving and still drawing paid clicks is a double waste and belongs on the [Dead Stock with Active Ad Spend](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/dead-stock-with-active-ad-spend) card. The slow-moving total is the entry point to that triage, not the conclusion.

5. **The earlier you catch a SKU, the more of its value you keep.** This is the entire reason the card uses a 90-day line rather than a 180-day one. A line cleared at 100 days might recover 80-90% of cost through a light markdown. The same line left until 250 days might recover 20-30% through a liquidator. The economic gap between acting at the slow-moving stage and acting at the dead-stock stage is, on this account, the difference between recovering roughly £1M of the £1.18M cohort and recovering roughly £350K of it. The card exists to move the decision forward in time, which is where almost all of the value is.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

NetSuite does not surface a single "slow-moving inventory" headline, but it has all the inputs across several reports and saved searches. The card consolidates them into one live number. The closest native views to triangulate against:

> **Reports → Inventory → Inventory Activity Detail** (the last-movement dates that drive the 90-day test)
> **Reports → Inventory → Inventory Valuation Summary** (the on-hand value of each item, the dollar input)
> **Reports → Inventory → Inventory Turnover** (the turn rate that slow-moving stock drags down)
> **Saved search**: an Item saved search with a formula on `Last Sale Date` or `Days Since Last Sale` greater than 90, with on-hand quantity and average cost columns, summed; this is essentially the manual equivalent of the card
> **Reports → Inventory → Stock Ledger** (audit-grade movement history when you need to confirm a specific SKU's last outbound date)

The headline difference between this card and a manual NetSuite saved search is cadence and definition. The saved search is usually run monthly and often counts "days since last sale", whereas this card runs live and counts "days since last outbound movement of any kind", so a transfer between locations resets the card's clock but not the typical saved search's clock.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a NetSuite saved search:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Movement definition** | Either | The card counts any outbound movement (sale, transfer, build consumption); many saved searches count only sales. A SKU transferred but not sold reads as moving here, slow in a sales-only search. |
| **Snapshot timing** | Either | The card is live; a monthly saved search is a point-in-time snapshot. A SKU that sold the day after the saved search ran will read differently. |
| **Cost basis** | Small | The card values at the item's current average or FIFO cost as NetSuite holds it; a manual export may use standard cost or last purchase cost, shifting the dollar total. |
| **Seasonal exclusions** | Either | If the workspace tags seasonal SKUs to exclude them, the card reads lower than a raw saved search that includes everything. |
| **Receipts resetting the clock** | Either | By default a purchase receipt does not reset the 90-day clock here; some saved searches use "last transaction date" which a receipt does reset, making them read lower. |
| **Multi-location aggregation** | Either | On OneWorld, the card aggregates per-location last-movement; a single consolidated saved search may mask a SKU that is fast in one location and dead in another. |

**Cross-connector reconciliation:**

This card is primarily a NetSuite-internal inventory metric, but its value rises when joined to the demand side. The slow-moving cohort intersected with [Dead Stock with Active Ad Spend](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/dead-stock-with-active-ad-spend) reveals SKUs that are both stalling in NetSuite and still consuming paid traffic in [google_ads.cost](/nerve-centre/google_ads/cost) or [facebook_ads.spend](/nerve-centre/facebook_ads/spend), which is the double-waste pattern. Intersected with [shopify.inventory_value](/nerve-centre/shopify/inventory_value) it shows whether the slow-moving stock is even visible on the storefront, sometimes a SKU is slow-moving precisely because a sync issue has hidden it from the web catalogue. The card itself is single-connector; the actionable findings come from the joins.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Why 90 days and not 180?**
Because 90 days is where the action still has economic value. A SKU caught at 90 days can usually be cleared with a light markdown and recover most of its cost; a SKU caught at 180 days is already a write-down candidate. The 180-day band is covered by the [Dead Stock](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/dead-stock-value) cards on purpose, so the two metrics do not overlap. Think of this card as the early-warning light and the dead-stock cards as the alarm.

**A purchase receipt arrived but the SKU still shows as slow-moving. Is that wrong?**
No, that is by design. The clock resets on outbound movement, not inbound. Receiving more of a SKU that nobody is buying does not make it less slow-moving, it makes it more. If your business genuinely wants receipts to reset the clock (rare, but valid for certain replenishment models), the workspace field map can switch the definition to "last transaction date".

**Seasonal stock always trips this card in the off-season. How do I stop the noise?**
Tag seasonal SKUs in the workspace so they report in a separate band, or read the card against the same date last year rather than against zero. A garden brand will always carry dormant winter stock through summer; that is dormant, not dead. The fix is configuration, not a higher threshold, because raising the threshold would also hide genuinely-stalling non-seasonal lines.

**Does this card respect OneWorld subsidiaries?**
Yes. Last-movement is evaluated per subsidiary location and the value is consolidated. A SKU can be fast-moving in the UK parent and slow-moving in the EU sub; the per-sub view shows this, the consolidated headline nets it. For operational decisions use the per-sub view, because a transfer between subs may be the right fix rather than a markdown.

**How does FX affect a multi-currency reading?**
The cohort is valued at cost in each subsidiary's local currency, then translated to reporting currency at the dashboard FX cadence. A weakening local currency lowers the translated value of a remote sub's slow-moving stock even if nothing moved, so for period-over-period comparison lock the FX cadence. The 90-day movement test itself is currency-independent because it is based on dates, not values.

**B2B vs DTC, do I read this differently?**
Yes. B2B inventory legitimately moves slower because it is often customer-allocated or held as safety stock for slow-replen accounts, so a higher slow-moving share can be normal. Before discounting a B2B slow-moving SKU, check for an open wholesale sales order against it; allocation, not markdown, may be the answer. DTC slow-moving stock is usually a cleaner signal that demand has faded and markdown is appropriate.

**We use bundles and kits. How are they counted?**
Kit and assembly items are counted on their component movement. If a component last moved more than 90 days ago it contributes to the cohort even if the assembled kit is theoretically sellable. Configure bundle handling in the workspace if you want the card to evaluate the assembled SKU's movement instead of the components'.

**Does revenue recognition (ASC 606) affect this card?**
No. This is an inventory-movement and valuation metric drawn from the Balance Sheet side, independent of how or when revenue is recognised. ASC 606 governs the timing of revenue, not the age of stock. The card reads the same regardless of whether a sale posts as a Cash Sale or an Invoice.

**Can SuiteScript customisations change what counts as a movement?**
They can. If a SuiteScript or workflow posts custom transaction types for movement (for example a bespoke consignment-out record), the workspace field map needs to know about them or the card will treat those SKUs as not having moved. Most standard NetSuite transaction types (Sales Order fulfilment, Transfer, Assembly Build) are recognised out of the box.

**How does this relate to the carrying-cost number?**
Directly. This card gives you the value of the slow-moving cohort; [Inventory Carrying Cost](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ns-inventory-carrying-cost) gives you the rate. Multiply the two and you get the annual cost of holding stock that is not selling. On most accounts that single multiplication is the most persuasive line in the clearance business case.

**NetSuite vs other ERPs on this metric?**
NetSuite exposes the raw movement data but leaves the slow-moving headline to a saved search you have to build and maintain. SAP S/4HANA has slow-moving and non-moving inventory analysis in its inventory cockpit but it requires configuration and often the controlling module. Oracle ERP Cloud has an aging and excess-inventory analysis under Inventory Management. For mid-market commerce the NetSuite plus Vortex IQ stack delivers the live cohort plus the cross-channel ad-spend join without the ERP-side configuration overhead.

**Does writing down a SKU remove it from this card?**
A write-down reduces the SKU's carried value but does not by itself change its last-movement date. If you write a SKU down to zero and it still has on-hand quantity, it can still appear in the cohort at zero or near-zero value. Disposing of the stock (a negative inventory adjustment out) is what removes it. The card is movement-and-value driven, so it reflects the physical reality, not just the accounting entry.
