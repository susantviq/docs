> Open Transactions (Pending Approval / Error): a live count of transactions stuck awaiting approval or sitting in a rejected/error state, where a spike signals a broken integration or approval-routing rule.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> How many transactions are currently jammed: waiting on an approval that has not happened, or rejected and erroring rather than posting cleanly. A small standing number is normal because some approvals are always in flight. A spike is the warning sign: it usually means a SuiteScript integration has started failing, an approval-routing rule broke, or an approver is absent and the queue is backing up. Because stuck transactions hold the books open and starve downstream numbers, this is a Hero card.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | Transactions currently in a pending-approval state or in a rejected/error state, counted live. One per transaction, blended across transaction types (sales orders, purchase orders, vendor bills, journal entries, and others depending on your approval workflows). |
| **States included** | Pending approval (awaiting an approver) and rejected/error (a transaction that failed to post, was rejected in the workflow, or errored during creation). Both are "not cleanly posted". |
| **States excluded** | Approved and posted transactions, and transactions still in a normal open lifecycle (for example a sales order pending fulfilment) that are not blocked on approval or in error. |
| **Tax treatment** | n/a, this is a count. The financial impact of the stuck transactions is felt downstream when they fail to post, not in this count itself. |
| **Currency** | Currency-agnostic. A stuck transaction in any currency adds one to the count. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects the dashboard filter. On OneWorld, each subsidiary can have its own approval workflows, so a routing break in one sub can spike the count without touching the others. |
| **Time window** | `RT` (real-time live snapshot; the count reflects what is stuck right now, refreshed at SuiteAnalytics cadence). |
| **Alert trigger** | Fires when the count exceeds `greater than 50`. Tune to your normal standing volume; a high-throughput account may set this higher, a small account lower. |
| **Roles** | owner, finance |
| **Chart type** | Single KPI count with alert threshold. |
| **Why it matters** | Stuck transactions do not post, so they do not appear in revenue, AP, AR, or the GL, which means the books are quietly understated until the jam clears. A spike is a leading indicator of an integration or workflow failure. Hero-class. |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

The card counts transactions that are currently sitting in a pending-approval state or in a rejected/error state, across the transaction types your account routes through approval workflows. It is a live count, not a period total, so it answers "how many are jammed right now" rather than "how many were jammed this month". The two states are grouped because they share a consequence: in both cases the transaction has not cleanly posted, so its value is missing from the GL and from every downstream financial figure until the jam is resolved.

The number is read against your normal standing volume. Some pending approvals are healthy: a purchase order waiting overnight for a manager's sign-off is the system working as designed. The signal is not the existence of a count but its movement. A sudden jump above the baseline almost always traces to a structural cause, a SuiteScript integration that started throwing errors and rejecting the records it tries to create, an approval-routing rule that was edited and now sends transactions to a non-existent approver, or a key approver who is out of office while the queue grows behind them. The card does not diagnose which of these it is; it surfaces the spike so the controller and the integration owner can investigate before the stuck transactions distort the close.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A mid-market consumer-electronics brand on NetSuite OneWorld. Three subsidiaries: a US parent (USD), a UK sub (GBP), and a DE sub (EUR). Annual revenue roughly $90M, with a busy purchase-order approval workflow, automated vendor-bill capture, and a nightly SuiteScript that creates sales orders from the commerce connector. Snapshot Tuesday 09 Jun 26, 08:30 EST, the morning after a deployment. Reporting context USD. These figures are illustrative.

| Transaction type | Pending approval | Rejected / error | Total stuck | Normal baseline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase orders | 22 | 1 | 23 | ~20 |
| Vendor bills | 9 | 3 | 12 | ~8 |
| Journal entries | 4 | 0 | 4 | ~3 |
| Sales orders (from nightly SuiteScript) | 0 | 71 | 71 | ~0 |
| **Total** | **35** | **75** | **110** | **~31** |

Five things to notice:

1. **The headline count of 110 is well over the alert threshold of 50, but the diagnosis lives in the breakdown, not the headline.** Three of the four transaction types are sitting roughly at their normal baseline: 23 purchase orders against a usual 20, 12 vendor bills against a usual 8, 4 journals against a usual 3. These are healthy queues doing their job. The entire spike comes from one line: 71 sales orders in a rejected/error state against a normal baseline of essentially zero. So the card is not telling you "approvals are slow everywhere", it is telling you "something that creates sales orders broke last night". Reading the breakdown turns a vague alarm into a precise pointer at the nightly SuiteScript that posts commerce orders into NetSuite.

2. **The timing, the morning after a deployment, is the single most useful clue, and it is why a Hero card earns its place.** A spike of errored sales orders that appears the morning after a code or configuration change is almost never a coincidence. The most common root cause is that a deployment changed a required field, an approval rule, or a saved-search the integration depends on, and now every record the SuiteScript tries to create fails validation and lands in error. Because the card is real-time, the controller sees 110 at 08:30 instead of discovering at month-end that a week of commerce orders never posted. That early catch is the entire value proposition of treating this as a Hero card: the cost of a silent integration failure compounds every day it runs unnoticed.

3. **Seventy-one missing sales orders means revenue, fulfilment, and AR are all quietly understated until the jam clears.** Each errored sales order is an order a customer placed that NetSuite does not yet know about. Revenue for those orders is not booked, the warehouse may not have a pick instruction, and no invoice will be raised until the SO posts. So while the SuiteScript is failing, the revenue cards read low, the open-sales-order pipeline reads short, and the cash forecast is missing those orders, all without any obvious error on those cards themselves. This is the insidious part: the downstream cards look merely "a bit soft", and only this card explains why. The fix is to repair the integration, reprocess the 71 failed records, and confirm they post.

4. **The pending-approval portion and the error portion are both stuck, but they fail for opposite reasons and need different owners.** The 35 pending-approval transactions are waiting on a human: an approver needs to act, or the routing needs to point at someone who exists. The 75 errored transactions are waiting on a fix: a field, a rule, or a script needs correcting before they can post at all. Grouping them in one count is right because both starve the GL, but acting on them is two different jobs. The controller chases the approvers; the integration owner or administrator chases the errors. On this snapshot the approval side is normal and the error side is the emergency, so the work goes to the integration owner first.

5. **The DE sub having its own approval workflow means a routing break can be sub-local, so always check whether a spike is concentrated in one subsidiary.** On OneWorld, approval workflows are frequently configured per subsidiary, with different approvers and different rules. If next week the spike were instead 60 pending-approval purchase orders all in the DE sub while the US and UK subs sat at baseline, the diagnosis would be completely different: not a broken script, but a DE approver who is on leave or a DE-specific routing rule that was edited. The card respects the dashboard filter, so slicing by subsidiary is the fastest way to tell a global failure (the script) from a local one (an approver or a sub-specific rule). Always ask "is this everywhere or in one sub" before you start fixing.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

Approval status and transaction errors are native to NetSuite, so this card triangulates against standard transaction views and the approval and script logs:

> **Transactions → Management → Open Transactions** or a transaction list filtered by status (the population of pending and errored transactions)
> **Saved search**: a Transaction saved search filtered to `status is Pending Approval` plus a filter for rejected/errored records, grouped by type and subsidiary, is essentially what the card counts
> **Customization → Scripting → Script Execution Log** or the relevant script's deployment log (where errored integration-created transactions leave their failure trail)
> **Setup → Approvals** and the approval workflow definitions (to see the routing rules that determine who must approve what, the source of pending-approval backlogs)
> **SuiteAnalytics workbook**: a Transactions workbook sliced by status and type gives the per-type, per-subsidiary breakdown the card shows

The headline difference is that NetSuite shows you the list of pending and errored transactions, but it does not, on its own, count them live against a baseline and alert you when they spike. The card adds the real-time count, the type and subsidiary breakdown, and the threshold alert on top of the raw transaction status.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a NetSuite transaction search:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Which statuses count as "stuck"** | Either | The card groups pending-approval and rejected/error; a saved search that includes or excludes a borderline status (for example "pending supervisor approval" versus "pending accounting approval") can differ. |
| **Transaction types in scope** | Either | The card blends the types your account routes through approval; a search scoped to only one type reads lower. |
| **Errored versus merely unapproved** | Either | A transaction that errored during creation may not always create a saved record at all; depending on how failures are captured, the script log count and the saved-record count can differ. |
| **Subsidiary filter** | Either | The card respects the dashboard subsidiary filter; a search over all subs reads higher. |
| **Refresh cadence** | Small | The card refreshes at SuiteAnalytics cadence (typically minutes), so a transaction approved seconds ago may still show for a short window. |
| **Workflow-state versus field-state** | Either | Approval status can live in a workflow state and/or a status field; if these diverge briefly, the count can differ from a search keyed on only one of them. |
| **Reprocessed records** | Card may show fewer | Once errored records are fixed and reposted, they drop off the card; a historical log still lists the original failures. |

This card has no cross-connector reconciliation in the sense of comparing dollar totals across platforms, because it is a NetSuite workflow-governance count with no direct equivalent in commerce or ad systems. There is, however, an important upstream dependency: when the errored portion of this card is made up of sales orders that should have been created from your storefront, those same orders show up unmatched on [Commerce Orders Without NetSuite SO](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/commerce-orders-without-netsuite-so). The two cards corroborate each other: a spike of errored sales orders here and a matching spike of unmatched commerce orders there is the same integration failure seen from the NetSuite side and the commerce side. That correlation is the fastest confirmation that the root cause is the order-sync integration and not something internal to NetSuite approvals.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**What is the difference between "pending approval" and "error" here, and why group them?**
Pending approval means the transaction is valid but waiting on a human to approve it. Error (or rejected) means the transaction could not post cleanly, because it failed validation, was rejected in the workflow, or errored during creation. They are grouped because both have the same consequence: the transaction is not posted, so its value is missing from the GL and every downstream number. They are separated in the breakdown because fixing them is different work, the approval queue needs an approver, the error queue needs a fix.

**What is a normal number and when should I worry?**
Normal depends on your throughput. A high-volume account always has dozens of approvals legitimately in flight. The signal is movement, not absolute level. The default alert fires above 50, but you should tune that to your own standing baseline so the card fires on genuine spikes, not routine queue depth. A doubling of the count overnight is more meaningful than the raw number.

**Why is this a Hero card?**
Because stuck transactions silently understate the books. Errored sales orders mean unbooked revenue; stuck vendor bills mean understated AP; jammed journals mean an incomplete GL. None of those produce an obvious error on the revenue or AP cards themselves; they just read soft. This card is the one place the jam is visible, and a spike is a reliable leading indicator of an integration or workflow failure, so catching it in real time is high-value.

**A spike appeared right after a deployment, is that related?**
Almost certainly. The most common cause of a sudden error spike is a change, a deployment, a workflow edit, a renamed field, a modified saved search, that breaks an integration the next time it runs. If the spike's timing lines up with a release, start there: check what changed and whether it touches a required field, an approval rule, or a script the integration depends on. Real-time visibility exists precisely so you connect the spike to the change before a week of records fail silently.

**Multi-currency and FX, do they affect this card?**
No. This is a count of transactions, not a value, so currency and FX do not move it. A stuck transaction in any currency adds one. Currency only becomes relevant when you quantify the financial impact of the jam, for example the total value of the errored sales orders, which you read from the value cards once you know how many are stuck.

**OneWorld and per-subsidiary workflows, how should I read a spike?**
Always check whether the spike is global or concentrated in one subsidiary. Approval workflows are often configured per sub, so a routing break or an absent approver in one sub can spike that sub's count while the others stay normal. A global spike across all subs points at a shared integration; a single-sub spike points at a sub-specific rule or approver. Slice by subsidiary first; it is the fastest way to localise the cause.

**Does this card count transactions that errored but did not create a record at all?**
It depends on how the failure is captured. Some failures create a saved record in a rejected/error state, which the card counts directly. Other failures (a SuiteScript that throws before the record saves) leave a trail in the script execution log but no saved transaction. For the second kind, pair this card with the SuiteScript error cards, which read the log rather than the transaction list, so together they cover both failure modes.

**B2B versus DTC, does the reading differ?**
The mechanism is identical, but B2B accounts tend to have heavier approval workflows (purchase orders, vendor bills, credit decisions) so a larger standing pending-approval queue is normal. DTC volume shows up mostly as auto-created sales orders, which usually do not require human approval, so for DTC-heavy accounts an error spike in sales orders is more likely an integration failure than an approval backlog. Tune your baseline to your transaction mix.

**Does this card block period close?**
Frequently, yes. A period cannot cleanly lock while transactions that belong to it are pending or errored, because their values are not yet in the GL. A persistent jam here is one of the most common reasons a close slips, which is why this card is a recommended sibling of the period-close cards. Clearing the queue is often a prerequisite step on the close checklist.

**How do I clear an errored backlog safely?**
Fix the root cause first, then reprocess. If the errors come from a broken integration, repair the field, rule, or script, then re-run the integration or re-submit the failed records so they post correctly. Do not simply force-post errored transactions without understanding why they failed, because the same defect may have produced subtly wrong data. Once fixed and reprocessed, the records drop off the card; confirm the count returns to baseline.

**How does this compare to approval and error handling in other ERPs?**
Every serious ERP has approval workflows and error states, but the naming and granularity differ. NetSuite exposes approval status per transaction and per subsidiary workflow, and surfaces script errors in the execution log. Oracle, SAP, and Microsoft Dynamics have comparable concepts under different names. The card normalises NetSuite's pending-and-error states into one count, so the reading is the same regardless of how the underlying ERP labels its workflow states; what differs is where you go to fix the root cause, which is always NetSuite-specific here.
