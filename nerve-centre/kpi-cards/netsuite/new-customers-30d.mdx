> New Customers (30d): the count of customer records created in NetSuite in the last 30 days, compared against the prior 30-day window, as an acquisition pulse.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> How many new customer records appeared in NetSuite over the trailing 30 days, and whether that is up or down on the previous 30 days. It is the simplest acquisition signal a finance or marketing lead can read at a glance: is the customer base growing, flat, or shrinking. It is informational, not an alert, so it frames a trend conversation rather than firing an alarm.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | New customer records created in NetSuite within the trailing 30-day window, with the prior 30-day window shown for comparison (30D vsP). One count per new customer entity, regardless of how many orders that customer has placed. |
| **What "new" means** | A customer entity whose creation date falls inside the window. It is the record being created, not the first order, that defines newness, which matters because a record can exist before its first sale (a B2B account set up during onboarding) or be created automatically at first checkout (DTC). |
| **Tax treatment** | n/a, this is a count of entities, not a financial value. |
| **Currency** | Currency-agnostic. A new customer transacting in USD, GBP, EUR, or AUD each adds one to the count. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects the dashboard filter. On OneWorld, a customer can be shared across subsidiaries or assigned to one; the count follows how customer records are scoped in your account. |
| **Time window** | `30D vsP` (trailing 30 days versus the prior 30 days). |
| **Alert trigger** | None. This card is informational, an acquisition pulse, not a threshold alarm. A sustained fall is a conversation, not an automated alert. |
| **Roles** | owner, finance, marketing |
| **Chart type** | Single KPI value with prior-period comparison. |
| **What it does not count** | Reactivated lapsed customers (the record already existed), duplicate-merged records, and records created purely for internal or test purposes where those are filtered. |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

The card counts customer records whose creation date falls within the trailing 30-day window, then counts the same for the prior 30-day window and shows the change between the two. It is a record-creation count, so the unit of measure is the customer entity itself, not orders, revenue, or sessions. Because it keys on when the record was created rather than when the customer first transacted, the figure reflects how customers enter your NetSuite account: created up front in B2B onboarding, or auto-created at first checkout in DTC flows synced from a commerce connector.

The comparison against the prior period is what gives the number meaning. A standalone count of "412 new customers" says little; "412 this period versus 366 last period, up 13%" tells you acquisition is accelerating. The card does not attempt to attribute the change to a cause, deduplicate aggressively, or judge customer quality. It is a clean top-of-funnel pulse that you read alongside revenue and active-customer cards to understand whether growth is coming from more customers, more spend per customer, or both.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A mixed B2B and DTC homewares brand on NetSuite OneWorld. Three subsidiaries: a US parent (USD), a UK sub (GBP), and an EU sub (EUR). Annual revenue roughly $45M, split about 60% DTC web store and 40% B2B wholesale and trade accounts. Trailing window 24 May 26 to 22 Jun 26, prior window 24 Apr 26 to 23 May 26. Reporting context USD. These figures are illustrative.

| Segment | New customers (this 30d) | New customers (prior 30d) | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DTC web store (auto-created at checkout) | 1,910 | 1,540 | +24% |
| B2B trade accounts (manually onboarded) | 38 | 51 | -25% |
| EU sub (mixed) | 286 | 240 | +19% |
| **Total** | **2,234** | **1,831** | **+22%** |

Five things to notice:

1. **The headline is up 22%, but the headline hides two opposite stories, which is the first thing to understand about an aggregate acquisition count.** DTC record creation jumped 24% while B2B trade onboarding fell 25%, and the large DTC volume simply drowns out the B2B decline in the blended number. If you read only the top line you would conclude acquisition is healthy across the board, when in fact the high-margin, high-lifetime-value B2B channel is contracting. This is why the card is most useful when you segment it: the blend tells you the business is growing, but the segments tell you which engine is doing the work and which is stalling. A marketing lead celebrating the 22% should be the same person asking why trade onboarding halved.

2. **DTC counts behave like a marketing-spend echo, and that is both their strength and their trap.** The 1,910 DTC records were almost all auto-created at first checkout, so the count moves with paid acquisition, promotions, and seasonality. A spike often means a successful campaign or a sale event, not durable base growth, because a one-time discount buyer creates a record but may never return. Read this count next to repeat-rate and active-customer signals before treating it as growth. A 24% rise driven by a 40%-off flash sale is a different business event from a 24% rise driven by organic search or a new product line, even though the card shows the same number.

3. **B2B counts are small, lumpy, and lag the pipeline, so a 25% drop on a base of 51 is 13 accounts and may be noise or may be a real signal.** B2B records are created during a sales-led onboarding process, often weeks after first contact, so this month's count reflects deals that closed in prior weeks. A drop can mean the sales team is busy servicing existing accounts, that a large onboarding batch slipped into the next period, or that top-of-funnel genuinely softened. Because the absolute numbers are small, never react to a single month; look at the three-month trend. The card gives you the pulse, but B2B acquisition needs the pipeline context that lives in the CRM and the sales team's own forecast.

4. **Record creation is not the same as first revenue, and on OneWorld this gap can be material.** A B2B account is frequently created during onboarding, set up with payment terms, tax registration, and a credit limit, days or weeks before its first order posts. So a customer can be "new this month" on this card while contributing zero revenue until next month, which is why this card can rise while the revenue cards stay flat, with no contradiction. The opposite also happens: a DTC checkout creates the record and the order simultaneously, so new-customer count and first-order revenue move together. Knowing which pattern dominates your business tells you how to read the lead-lag relationship between this card and the revenue cards.

5. **The EU sub's 19% rise is healthy, but on OneWorld you must check whether a customer counted in the EU sub is genuinely new or simply newly assigned a subsidiary.** In some OneWorld configurations a single customer entity is shared across subsidiaries; in others each sub has its own customer records. If your account shares customers, a customer who bought from the US parent last year and from the EU sub this month may or may not count as "new EU", depending on how the record and its subsidiary assignment are structured. Confirm your customer-sharing model before reading per-sub new-customer counts as clean acquisition, because a configuration quirk can make a reassigned customer look like a fresh one. The consolidated total is the safest read; per-sub counts need the configuration caveat.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

Customer creation is native to NetSuite, so this card triangulates directly against the customer list and standard saved searches:

> **Lists → Relationships → Customers** (the master customer list; filter or sort by Date Created to see the new cohort)
> **Reports → Customers/Receivables → Customer Register** (customer-level activity, useful to cross-check which new records have transacted)
> **Saved search**: a Customer saved search filtered to `Date Created within last 30 days`, grouped by subsidiary, is essentially the population this card counts
> **SuiteAnalytics workbook**: a Customers workbook sliced by creation date and subsidiary gives the per-sub new-customer breakdown the card can show
> **Setup → Company → Auto-Generated Numbers** (relevant if new customer records get an auto-assigned ID, useful for spotting test or placeholder records that should be excluded)

The headline difference is that the NetSuite customer list shows every customer with a creation date, but it does not, on its own, compute the trailing-30-day count or the prior-period comparison. The card adds the windowing and the change calculation on top of the raw record list.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a NetSuite saved search:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Definition of "new"** | Either | The card counts record creation date; a marketing view of "new customer" might mean first order date, which can fall in a different window. |
| **Duplicate and merged records** | Card may show fewer | If the card de-duplicates obvious duplicates or excludes records later merged away, a raw saved search counting every created record reads higher. |
| **Test and placeholder records** | Card may show fewer | Internal test customers, sample records, and placeholders may be filtered by the card but counted in a raw list. |
| **Subsidiary sharing** | Either | On OneWorld with shared customers, a reassigned customer can look new at the per-sub level; the consolidated count avoids this. |
| **Sub-customers and jobs** | Either | Parent customers with sub-customers or job records can inflate a raw count if every child record is counted; the card typically counts at the entity grain you configure. |
| **Inactive customers** | Either | A customer created and immediately marked inactive may be included or excluded depending on whether the saved search filters on active status. |
| **Sync lag from commerce** | Card may show fewer | If commerce customers have not yet synced into NetSuite, they are not yet counted; a commerce-side acquisition report would already show them. |
| **Time-zone of the window boundary** | Small | The exact cut-off of "last 30 days" can shift a handful of records across the boundary versus a saved search using a different time-zone reference. |

There is no cross-connector reconciliation that changes this card's number, but the commerce connectors are an important upstream dependency. If DTC customers are created in your storefront (Shopify, BigCommerce, Adobe Commerce) and synced into NetSuite, any lag or failure in that sync suppresses the new-customer count here until the records land. When this card looks unexpectedly flat against a strong storefront acquisition month, check [ecom Customers Absent From NetSuite](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ecom-customers-absent-from-netsuite) first; a sync gap, not a real acquisition slowdown, is a common cause.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Does "new" mean a new record or a new buyer?**
A new record. The card counts customer entities whose creation date falls in the window. In DTC, record creation and first purchase usually coincide because the record is auto-created at checkout. In B2B, a record is often created during onboarding, sometimes weeks before the first order, so a "new customer" here may not yet have transacted. If you want first-order-based acquisition, that is a different metric that keys on the first sales transaction, not record creation.

**Why is there no alert on this card?**
Because acquisition is a trend, not a single threshold. A one-month dip can be seasonality, a slipped B2B onboarding batch, or a sync lag, none of which warrant an automated alarm. The card is deliberately informational so it frames a conversation. If you want alerting on acquisition softness, build it as a trend rule (for example several consecutive declining periods) rather than a single-day threshold.

**How do reactivated lapsed customers count?**
They do not. A customer who lapsed and returned already has a record, so they do not create a new one and do not add to this count. That is correct for an acquisition pulse, but it means the card understates total "buying activity" growth. Pair with the active-customer card to see reactivations, and with churn signals to see the outflow side.

**Multi-currency and FX, do they affect this card?**
No. This is a count of entities, not a value, so currency and FX are irrelevant to the number. A new customer transacting in any currency adds exactly one. Currency only matters when you pivot from this card to the revenue cards to ask what those new customers are worth.

**OneWorld and shared customers, what should I watch for?**
The configuration of customer sharing. If customers are shared across subsidiaries, a customer reassigned to a new sub can appear as new at the per-sub level even though the entity is not new to the group. The consolidated count is the clean read. Confirm with your administrator whether your account uses shared customers or per-subsidiary customer records before trusting per-sub new-customer figures.

**B2B versus DTC, do I read this card differently?**
Yes, substantially. DTC counts are large, fast-moving, and track marketing spend and promotions, so a spike often means a campaign rather than durable growth. B2B counts are small, lumpy, and lag the sales pipeline, so single-month moves are often noise. Segment the card by channel and judge each on its own baseline; a 25% DTC swing and a 25% B2B swing are completely different magnitudes of real-world change.

**Does a sync failure from my storefront suppress this number?**
Yes. If DTC customers are created in your commerce platform and synced into NetSuite, any lag or failure in that sync means those customers are not yet counted here. A flat new-customer card against a strong storefront month is a classic symptom of a sync gap, not a real acquisition slowdown. Check the commerce-to-NetSuite customer sync and the absent-customers card before drawing conclusions.

**Do sub-customers, jobs, and contacts inflate the count?**
They can, depending on how your account models relationships. If parent customers have sub-customers or job records, a naive count of every created record can overstate genuine new acquisition. The card counts at the customer-entity grain you configure; contacts (people attached to a company record) are not customers and are not counted. Confirm your entity model so you know whether child records are in or out.

**Does period close affect this card?**
No. New-customer count is not a posted financial figure, so it is not gated by period lock status. A customer record created during an open period still counts. This is one of the few cards that is genuinely real-time-clean regardless of where you are in the close cycle, which is why it sits comfortably as an always-on acquisition pulse.

**Can a SuiteScript or workflow create records that distort the count?**
Yes, and it is worth checking. Some accounts run SuiteScripts or workflows that auto-create customer records for non-acquisition reasons (placeholder records for quotes, records created by a data-migration script, records spawned by an integration test). If such automation runs inside the window, it inflates the count without representing real new customers. If you see an unexplained spike, look for a script or import that fired, not a marketing win.

**How does this compare to acquisition reporting in other ERPs or in my marketing tools?**
Marketing tools usually count "new customers" as first-purchase or first-session, attributed to a channel, which is a different definition from record creation. Other ERPs (Oracle, SAP, Microsoft Dynamics) also key on entity creation but name and scope the customer record differently. This card normalises NetSuite's record-creation event into a simple windowed count, so treat it as the financial-system view of acquisition, and reconcile it deliberately against the marketing-system view, which will rarely match exactly because the definitions differ.

**Should I worry if new customers rise but revenue does not?**
Not necessarily, especially in B2B, where records are created during onboarding before the first order posts, so this card leads revenue by a period. In DTC, a rise driven by a deep-discount event creates records that may spend little and not return, so revenue lags or never materialises. The honest read is to pair this card with average order value and the revenue trend: if new customers rise and AOV holds, growth is real; if new customers rise while AOV falls sharply, you may be buying low-value buyers.
