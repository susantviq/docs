> Accrual Reversals (last close) for the selected period: reversing journal entries booked at prior period-end that automatically flip back in the new period.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> When the accounting team books an accrual at month-end (an expense that has been incurred but not yet invoiced, like the last few days of a utility bill or an unbilled freight charge), they usually flag it to auto-reverse on day one of the next period. This card counts how many of those reversing entries fired in the current window. A healthy close has a predictable, repeatable count; a sudden spike or collapse means the close process changed, an accrual was missed, or someone is manually patching the GL.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | The number of reversing journal entries that originated as period-end accruals in the prior close and reversed into the current period. Includes both auto-reversing JEs and manually reversed accrual JEs flagged as reversals. |
| **Tax treatment** | n/a. Accrual reversals are a GL timing mechanism, not a taxable event in themselves; the underlying expense or revenue is what carries tax treatment. |
| **Currency** | Reporting currency. Per-subsidiary accruals are translated to the consolidated reporting currency. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. On OneWorld, accruals are booked per subsidiary and each sub runs its own close, so the count aggregates across subs in scope. |
| **Time window** | `30D` (the period that follows the most recent close). |
| **Alert trigger** | `greater than 10` reversals in the window. The default is a sanity ceiling, not a hard rule; tune to your normal close volume. |
| **Roles** | owner, finance |
| **Chart type** | Single KPI number with period-over-period delta. |
| **Why it matters** | Reversals are the cleanest proxy for whether your accrual discipline is consistent close to close. Stable count = repeatable process. Volatile count = process drift. |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

The card reads journal entries in the current window that are tagged as reversals of accrual entries originated in the previous accounting period. In NetSuite, accruals are typically booked as journal entries with a reversal date set to the first day of the following period, so the system creates the offsetting entry automatically when the new period opens. The card counts those system-generated reversals plus any manually entered reversal JEs that are explicitly linked to a prior-period accrual. It deliberately does not count ordinary journal entries, recurring journals that are not accrual reversals, or intercompany eliminations, because those are different mechanisms with different review owners.

The logic is intentionally a count rather than a value, because the diagnostic signal lives in the cadence, not the dollars. A finance team that books roughly the same set of accruals every month (rent, utilities, unbilled freight, payroll accruals, bonus accruals, professional-fees accruals) should see a stable reversal count. The card does not attempt to judge whether each individual reversal is correct, only whether the population of reversals looks like a normal close. The dollar magnitude of any single reversal is available by drilling into the underlying JE in NetSuite; the card surfaces the count so that an anomaly is visible before anyone has to open the GL.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A mid-market homeware brand on NetSuite OneWorld. Three subsidiaries: a US parent (USD), a UK retail sub (GBP), and an EU distribution sub (EUR). Annual revenue roughly $55M. The window runs 01 May 26 to 30 May 26, immediately after the April close was locked. Reporting currency USD. These figures are illustrative.

| Accrual category | Prior-close accruals booked | Reversed this period | Net new accruals expected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unbilled freight / carrier | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| Utilities and rent true-ups | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Payroll and bonus accruals | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Professional fees (legal, audit) | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Marketing and agency accruals | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| **Total reversals (this card)** | **14** | **14** | **12** |

Five things to notice:

1. **A reversal count of 14 is above the alert ceiling of 10, but that does not automatically mean something is wrong.** On a OneWorld account, three subsidiaries each running their own close legitimately push the aggregate reversal count higher than a single-entity business would ever see. The right response to crossing the alert threshold is not to panic, it is to compare the count to the same period last month and last year. If this business books 13 to 15 reversals every single close, then 14 is the signal of a healthy, repeatable process and the alert threshold simply needs to be raised to match the entity structure. The alert exists to catch deviation from your own normal, not to enforce a universal number that ignores how many subsidiaries you consolidate.

2. **The marketing and agency line is the one to inspect, because reversals (3) exceed the net new accruals expected (1).** That gap is the most common real finding this card produces. It usually means that an agency invoice that was accrued at April month-end actually arrived and was booked in May, so the accrual correctly reversed and the real invoice took its place, which is exactly what should happen. The risk is the opposite case: the accrual reverses but nobody re-accrues for the still-unbilled portion, which understates expense in the new period and overstates it when the invoice finally lands. The card cannot tell these two cases apart on its own; it surfaces the count so the controller knows to check whether a fresh accrual was booked to replace the reversed one.

3. **A reversal count that suddenly drops to near zero is a louder alarm than a count that spikes.** A spike usually reflects more accruals being booked, which is conservative and rarely dangerous. A collapse means the period-end accruals from the prior close were never set to auto-reverse, so the original accrual is now sitting in the GL as a permanent entry, double-counting the expense when the real invoice arrives. This is one of the classic close errors that surfaces months later during the audit as an unexplained over-accrual. Because the card watches the count every period, a drop from a steady 14 to 2 is visible the week it happens rather than at year-end, which is the entire point of running detection on the close process rather than reviewing it once a quarter.

4. **Manual reversals mixed in with auto-reversals are a process-quality tell.** In a disciplined close, almost every accrual is booked with a reversal date so NetSuite handles the flip automatically and no human touches it again. When the count includes a rising share of manually entered reversal JEs, it usually means someone is patching accruals that were booked without a reversal date, which is slower, error-prone, and not repeatable. Pairing this card with the volume of manual journal entries (see siblings) lets a controller see whether the close is becoming more hands-on over time, which is the early signal that the team is under-resourced or that a new staff accountant has not been trained on the standard accrual workflow.

5. **On a OneWorld consolidation, an uneven reversal count across subsidiaries points at a single sub with a weak close.** If the US parent and the UK sub each contribute a stable handful of reversals every month but the EU sub swings between zero and eight, the EU close is the one to fix. Different subsidiaries often have different local accounting staff, different shared-service arrangements, and different review rigour, so the consolidated count can look fine while one sub quietly carries all the volatility. Drilling the card by subsidiary turns a consolidated number that looks acceptable into a specific, assignable action: retrain or resource the close for the subsidiary that is generating the noise.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

NetSuite books accruals as journal entries with a reversal date, and the system creates the offsetting entry when the next period opens. The native views that triangulate against this card:

> **Transactions → Financial → Make Journal Entries** (filter on the reversal flag to see entries that will auto-reverse, and their reversal dates)
> **Reports → Financial → General Ledger** (the reversing entries appear in the new period as offsetting lines to the prior-period accrual)
> **Saved search**: a Transaction saved search on type Journal, filtered to entries whose Reversal Date falls in the current period, grouped by subsidiary and account; this is essentially the population the card counts
> **SuiteAnalytics workbook**: a Journal Entries workbook with a "Reversing Entry" or "Created From Reversal" dimension, sliced by period, gives the per-subsidiary breakdown the card aggregates
> **Reports → Financial → Trial Balance** (compare prior-period-end accrual balances to current-period-open balances; the difference should be the reversals)

The headline difference between this card and a manual GL review is cadence and scope. The card counts every close, across every subsidiary in the filter, the week it happens; a manual review usually happens once during the close checklist and is rarely revisited until the audit.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a NetSuite saved search:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Manual vs auto reversals** | Card may be higher | The card counts both system-generated auto-reversals and manually entered reversal JEs linked to a prior accrual; a saved search filtered only on the auto-reversal flag will miss the manual ones. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Either | The card respects the dashboard filter; a saved search run at the parent without subsidiary scoping can include or exclude subs differently. |
| **Period boundary** | Either | If an accrual was set to reverse on a date that falls just outside the 30D window, the card and a calendar-period search will disagree by a day or two of straddling entries. |
| **Recurring journals** | Card may be lower | Recurring journals that are not accrual reversals are excluded by the card but may appear in a broad journal search. |
| **Intercompany eliminations** | Card excludes | Consolidation elimination entries are not accrual reversals and are deliberately filtered out; a raw GL count includes them. |
| **Reversal booked early** | Either | If finance reverses an accrual manually before the auto-reversal date, the count can shift between periods depending on when the manual reversal was posted. |
| **Multi-book accounting** | Either | Accounts running NetSuite Multi-Book may have accruals that reverse in one book but not another; the card follows the primary book unless configured otherwise. |

There is no cross-connector reconciliation for this card. Accrual reversals are a pure NetSuite GL mechanism with no equivalent in commerce or ad platforms, so the only legitimate comparison is against NetSuite's own GL and saved searches.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**What exactly is an accrual reversal?**
An accrual is a journal entry that records an expense or revenue in the period it was economically incurred, even though the invoice or cash has not arrived yet. At period-end you accrue, for example, the last four days of a monthly utility bill. To avoid double-counting when the real invoice lands next month, the accrual is set to reverse on day one of the new period. The reversal is that automatic flip-back. This card counts how many of those flips happened in the current window.

**Why count reversals instead of the accrual dollars?**
Because the diagnostic value is in consistency, not magnitude. A team that books the same family of accruals every month should see a stable count. A swing in the count is the signal that the close process changed, regardless of whether the dollars went up or down. The dollar values of any individual accrual are one drill-down away in NetSuite; the count is the early-warning layer.

**Is a high count bad?**
Not on its own. More subsidiaries, more careful accrual discipline, and a conservative accounting policy all legitimately raise the count. The alert default of greater than 10 is a generic ceiling; the real signal is deviation from your own normal. Compare to the same period in prior months before treating a high count as a problem.

**Is a low count good?**
Usually the opposite. A count that collapses to near zero often means accruals from the prior close were never set to auto-reverse, so the original accrual is now stuck in the GL and will double-count the expense when the real invoice arrives. A sudden drop deserves more scrutiny than a spike.

**How does OneWorld change the reading?**
Each subsidiary runs its own close and books its own accruals, so the consolidated count is the sum across all subs in scope. A single-entity business might see 3 to 5 reversals a month; a five-subsidiary OneWorld group can legitimately see 20 or more. Always set the alert threshold relative to your entity structure, and drill by subsidiary to find which sub is generating any volatility.

**Multi-currency and FX, do they affect the count?**
The count itself is currency-agnostic; a reversal is a reversal regardless of currency. FX only matters if you also look at the reversed dollar value, where per-subsidiary accruals are translated to the reporting currency. The card surfaces the count, so FX revaluation timing does not move it.

**B2B vs DTC, does the card read differently?**
The mechanism is identical, but B2B operations tend to carry more accruals (unbilled freight on large shipments, volume rebates accrued to customers, supplier rebates accrued from vendors, longer professional-services engagements), so B2B-heavy businesses naturally show higher counts. DTC operations usually have a leaner, more repetitive accrual set. Calibrate the expected count to your business model.

**What about manual reversals my team enters by hand?**
The card counts manually entered reversal JEs as long as they are linked to a prior-period accrual. A rising share of manual reversals is itself a finding: it usually means accruals are being booked without a reversal date and then patched by hand, which is slower and more error-prone. Pair with the manual journal entry card to watch that trend.

**Does the timing of the close affect the count?**
Yes. If the prior period closes late, the reversals can land in a cluster once the period finally locks, sometimes spilling across the window boundary. A late close therefore often produces an erratic reversal count. Pair with the close-deadline and on-time-rate cards to separate process drift from timing artefacts.

**Can a reversal be wrong?**
Yes, in two main ways. An accrual can reverse without a fresh accrual being booked to replace the still-unbilled portion (understating current expense), or an accrual can fail to reverse at all (over-accruing). The card flags the count anomaly; the controller drills into the specific JEs to determine which case applies. The card is the detection layer, not the diagnosis.

**Does SuiteScript get involved?**
Sometimes. Many teams use scheduled SuiteScript or workflow automation to book recurring accruals and set reversal dates programmatically. If that script fails silently, accruals may be booked without reversal dates, which shows up here as a falling reversal count in the following period. Pair with the SuiteScript error cards to connect a script failure to a downstream accrual anomaly.

**How does this differ in other ERPs?**
Most accrual-capable ERPs (Oracle ERP Cloud, SAP, Microsoft Dynamics) support auto-reversing journals, but each names and flags them differently. NetSuite's reversal-date model is straightforward to read because the reversal is a system-generated offsetting entry tied to the original. The card is purpose-built for NetSuite's mechanism; the same concept exists elsewhere but the field that identifies a reversal differs by platform.

**Does period close need to be complete for this card to be accurate?**
Largely yes. The reversals only fire when the new period opens, which follows the prior period being locked. If a close is still in progress, the reversal population may be incomplete and the count will rise as the close finalises. Read this card alongside the period close status card so you know whether the count is final or still settling.
