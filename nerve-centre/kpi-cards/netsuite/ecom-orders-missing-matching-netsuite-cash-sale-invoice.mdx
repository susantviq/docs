> Ecom orders the merchant fulfilled but NetSuite never billed for. The billing-document gap: no Cash Sale, no Invoice, no recognised revenue. A common cause is a SuiteScript order-import script failing silently.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> A count of commerce orders that have been fulfilled but for which NetSuite holds no matching billing document, no Cash Sale and no Invoice. This is distinct from an order simply lacking a sales order: here the order may even have a sales order, but the document that actually recognises the revenue and creates the AR was never produced. The customer paid, the goods shipped, and the GL shows nothing. The most common cause is a SuiteScript order-import or billing script that fails silently, creating the sales order but never converting it to the Cash Sale or Invoice that books the money. Each unmatched order is recognised revenue that is missing from your books.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | Commerce orders, older than the grace window and fulfilled, with no matching NetSuite Cash Sale and no matching Invoice. The match is on the order identity carried through to the billing document. |
| **Billing document scope** | Both posting transaction types that recognise ecom revenue: Cash Sale (paid-at-checkout DTC) and Invoice (on-account or terms-based B2B). An order with neither is unmatched. |
| **Relationship to the SO card** | The [Commerce Orders Without NetSuite SO](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/commerce-orders-without-netsuite-so) card catches orders with no sales order at all; this card catches orders that may have a sales order but were never billed. The billing-document variant. |
| **Grace window** | Orders younger than 7 days are excluded, since the order-to-bill flow legitimately takes time. After 7 days an unbilled fulfilled order is a failure, not a delay. |
| **Currency** | Per-order in transaction currency; the unbilled-revenue total in reporting currency. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. On OneWorld an order must bill under the correct subsidiary; an order that cannot resolve a subsidiary often cannot bill. |
| **Time window** | `7D` (orders fulfilled in the window that remain unbilled past the grace period) |
| **Alert trigger** | `greater than 0 unmatched after 7d`, driven by the billing-gap sentiment key |
| **Roles** | owner, finance, operations |

## Calculation

Calculated automatically by joining your fulfilled commerce orders to your NetSuite billing documents. The card takes every commerce order that has been fulfilled and is older than the 7-day grace window, then looks for a matching posting transaction in NetSuite, a Cash Sale or an Invoice, that carries the order's identity. Where no matching billing document exists, the order is counted as unbilled, and the card reports both the count and the revenue those orders represent.

The distinction from the sales-order card is deliberate and important. An order can have a sales order (so it looks tracked) and still never be billed, because the step that converts a fulfilled sales order into a Cash Sale or Invoice is a separate action, often automated by a SuiteScript or workflow that can fail on its own. This card watches that final, revenue-recognising step specifically. The alert fires on any unmatched order past the grace window, because a fulfilled order with no billing document is, by definition, shipped goods the business has not booked the money for. See the At a glance summary above for what the metric tracks and the worked example below for a typical reading.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A US apparel brand on NetSuite OneWorld selling through Shopify Plus DTC and a B2B wholesale portal on BigCommerce. Two subsidiaries: a US parent (USD) and a UK sub (GBP). Annual revenue ~$46M. Orders import to NetSuite as sales orders via one script; a second scheduled script converts fulfilled sales orders into Cash Sales (DTC) or Invoices (B2B). Window: 7 days ending 22 Jun 26. Reporting currency USD.

**Headline:** 73 fulfilled orders are unbilled past the 7-day grace window. Unbilled revenue: $94,500. Of these, 73 have a sales order but no Cash Sale or Invoice.

| Cause cluster | Orders | Unbilled revenue | Has SO? | Root cause |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Billing-conversion script stalled | 58 | $71,200 | Yes | Scheduled bill-creation script erroring since 17 Jun 26 |
| Order on credit hold, never released | 9 | $12,800 | Yes | B2B accounts held; SO created, invoice blocked |
| Subsidiary unresolved | 4 | $6,900 | Yes | UK-sub orders could not resolve entity to invoice |
| Refunded after fulfilment | 2 | $3,600 | Yes | Genuinely should not be billed (correctly excluded on review) |
| **Total (this card)** | **73** | **$94,500** | | |

Five things to notice:

1. **$94,500 of fulfilled orders shipped this week with no billing document, which means the revenue is not recognised and the AR does not exist.** This is the crucial difference from an unmapped order: these orders are not invisible, most of them have a sales order, so they look tracked on a casual glance. What they lack is the Cash Sale or Invoice that actually books the money. The goods are gone, the customer has them, and the GL is silent. On the revenue trend, the cash-collected card, and the AR aging, this money simply does not appear. The only way to see it is to compare fulfilled orders against billing documents, which is exactly what the card does, and the comparison reveals revenue that has physically left the warehouse but never reached the ledger.

2. **The dominant cluster is a silently failing billing script, the canonical cause.** 58 of the 73 orders trace to the scheduled script that converts fulfilled sales orders into Cash Sales and Invoices, which has been erroring since 17 Jun 26. The sales-order import script is healthy, so orders are arriving and getting sales orders, which is why nothing looks wrong upstream. But the billing-conversion step is throwing, so no money is being booked. This is the precise pattern the card spec names: a SuiteScript order-import or billing script failing silently. It will show on the [SuiteScript Execution Errors (24h)](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/suitescript-execution-errors-24h) card as a cluster of identical errors, and the two cards together pinpoint both the symptom (unbilled orders) and the cause (the failing conversion script).

3. **The credit-hold cluster is a correct block being mistaken for a failure, and reading it right matters.** 9 orders are unbilled because the B2B accounts behind them are on credit hold, so the sales order was created but the invoice was deliberately not. These are not a billing failure; they are the credit control working as intended, refusing to extend more billed credit to held accounts. Crucially, they tie back to the [AR Aging on Customers with Active Ecom Orders](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ar-aging-on-customers-with-active-ecom-orders) card, because they are exactly the overdue customers who should not be getting new billed orders. Here the hold prevented the invoice, which is the right outcome, but the fact that the order was fulfilled at all means the storefront accepted it before the hold caught up. The card surfaces the tension; the fix is to stop the fulfilment, not to force the invoice.

4. **The subsidiary-unresolved cluster is a OneWorld-specific posting blocker.** 4 UK-sub orders could not be billed because the order could not resolve to a subsidiary, so NetSuite had no entity to post the Cash Sale or Invoice under. This often pairs with the customer-record gaps the [Ecom Customers Absent from NetSuite Customer Record](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ecom-customers-absent-from-netsuite-customer-record) and [OneWorld Customers Missing Subsidiary](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/oneworld-customers-missing-subsidiary) cards track: no resolvable entity means no valid posting target, so the billing document cannot be created even though everything else is in place. The fix is upstream, assign the customer and subsidiary correctly, after which these orders bill normally.

5. **Two of the 73 are correctly excluded on review, which is why the card supports triage rather than blind alarm.** Two orders were refunded after fulfilment, so they genuinely should not be billed, and a quick review confirms they belong off the list. This is the discipline the card demands: a fulfilled-but-unbilled order is usually a failure, but a small minority are legitimate (post-fulfilment refunds, sample or warranty shipments, intentional no-charge orders). Reviewing the list rather than auto-billing everything on it prevents booking revenue on orders that should not carry any. After excluding the 2 refunds, the actionable unbilled revenue is $90,900 across 71 orders, which is the number that goes to the finance team.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

NetSuite holds the billing documents; the fulfilled orders live on the commerce platform. The NetSuite side of this card reconciles against the sales transaction reports and the order-to-bill pipeline. The closest native views to triangulate against:

> **Reports → Sales → Sales by Customer** (the recognised revenue; orders missing from here are the unbilled ones)
> **Transactions → Sales → Enter Cash Sales** and **Create Invoices** (the document types this card looks for; a saved search over both reveals what was actually billed)
> **Reports → Sales → Open Sales Orders** (fulfilled sales orders still open and unbilled are the prime candidates for this card)
> **Saved search**: a Sales Order saved search filtered to fully fulfilled but with no linked Cash Sale or Invoice, which is the NetSuite-only approximation of this card before the commerce join confirms the order shipped to the customer
> **Customization → Scripting → Script Execution Log** (where a failing billing-conversion script logs its errors and last-run time)

The headline difference between this card and a NetSuite open-sales-order report is that NetSuite can show you a fulfilled sales order with no invoice, but it cannot confirm from inside the ERP that the customer actually received and paid for the goods on the storefront. The commerce join supplies that confirmation, which is what turns "an open sales order" into "shipped goods we never billed for".

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a NetSuite sales report:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Document-type scope** | Either | The card looks for both Cash Sale and Invoice; a report that checks only Invoices will miss the DTC Cash Sale orders and overstate the gap. |
| **Grace window** | Either | The card excludes orders younger than 7 days; a same-day NetSuite snapshot will include legitimately in-flight orders that the card does not. |
| **Fulfilment definition** | Either | The card keys on the commerce order being fulfilled; a NetSuite report keyed on sales-order status may differ if fulfilment status is not synced consistently between systems. |
| **Refunds after fulfilment** | Card may overstate | An order refunded after shipping should not be billed; if the refund is not reflected, the card can list it as unbilled when it is correctly so. Review excludes these. |
| **Credit-held orders** | Card may overstate | Orders deliberately not invoiced because the account is on hold are correctly unbilled; the card lists them but they are a credit decision, not a billing failure. |
| **Subsidiary or customer unresolved** | Either | An order with no resolvable entity cannot be billed and appears here, but the root cause is upstream identity, not the billing step itself. |

**Cross-connector reconciliation, the killer cross-channel finding:**

This card exists only at the intersection of the commerce fulfilment stream and the NetSuite billing ledger. Neither system can produce it alone: the commerce platform knows the order shipped, NetSuite knows what was billed, and only the join knows the order shipped but was never billed.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**How is this different from Commerce Orders Without NetSuite SO?**
That card catches orders with no sales order at all, an order that never made it into NetSuite as a transaction. This card catches orders that may well have a sales order but were never converted into the Cash Sale or Invoice that recognises the revenue. The difference is the stage of the pipeline that broke: the import step versus the billing step. An order can pass the first card (it has a sales order) and still fail this one (it was never billed), which is exactly the silent-failure case, because the order looks tracked while the money is missing.

**Why both Cash Sale and Invoice?**
Because ecom revenue posts as one or the other depending on how the customer paid. A DTC customer who paid at checkout produces a Cash Sale (the payment and the revenue post together). A B2B customer on terms produces an Invoice (revenue and AR post, payment comes later). An order is only unbilled if it has neither. A report that checks only for Invoices will wrongly flag every paid DTC order as unbilled, which is why the card checks both document types.

**Why a 7-day grace window?**
Because the order-to-bill flow legitimately takes time, especially for B2B orders that bill on fulfilment rather than at checkout, and for orders awaiting partial-shipment completion. Within 7 days an unbilled order may simply be in flight. After 7 days it is a failure, not a delay. The window is configurable; tighten it for fast-billing DTC-only operations, loosen it for B2B with long fulfilment cycles.

**The count jumped. What is the first thing to check?**
The billing-conversion script. The most common cause of a sudden rise is the scheduled or workflow-driven step that turns fulfilled sales orders into Cash Sales and Invoices failing silently. Check the [SuiteScript Execution Errors (24h)](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/suitescript-execution-errors-24h) card for a cluster of identical errors and the script's last successful run. A flat line in billing-document creation dated to the error spike confirms it.

**Some unbilled orders are correct, like refunds. How do I avoid booking them?**
Review the list before acting. A small minority of unbilled orders should stay unbilled: orders refunded after fulfilment, warranty or sample shipments, intentional no-charge orders. The card lists them because they are fulfilled and unbilled, but billing them would be wrong. The discipline is to triage the list, exclude the legitimate cases, and bill the rest. Auto-billing everything on the card without review risks recognising revenue on orders that should carry none.

**Credit-held orders show up here. Is that a billing failure?**
No, it is credit control working. An order from a customer on credit hold may be fulfilled but deliberately not invoiced, which is the correct outcome for an account that should not be extended more billed credit. These orders tie back to the [AR Aging on Customers with Active Ecom Orders](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ar-aging-on-customers-with-active-ecom-orders) card. The real question they raise is why the order was fulfilled at all if the account was held; the fix is upstream at fulfilment, not forcing the invoice.

**We are on OneWorld. How does subsidiary resolution affect billing?**
A Cash Sale or Invoice must post under a subsidiary, so an order whose customer or subsidiary cannot be resolved cannot be billed and appears here. This often co-occurs with the customer-record gaps the [Ecom Customers Absent from NetSuite Customer Record](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ecom-customers-absent-from-netsuite-customer-record) and [OneWorld Customers Missing Subsidiary](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/oneworld-customers-missing-subsidiary) cards track. The fix is upstream identity assignment; once the entity resolves, the billing document posts normally.

**How does FX affect this card?**
The count is currency-independent. The unbilled-revenue total is translated to reporting currency at the dashboard cadence, so a multi-currency brand's headline dollar figure moves with FX even though the underlying orders do not. The decision to bill is driven by the order list and cause, not by the FX-translated value.

**Does this card touch revenue recognition directly?**
Yes, more directly than almost any other card. The Cash Sale or Invoice is the document that recognises revenue under ASC 606. An order with no billing document has, by definition, unrecognised revenue: the performance obligation was satisfied (the goods shipped) but no document exists to recognise the revenue against. This card is therefore a direct measure of recognised-but-unbooked revenue, which is precisely the gap auditors and revenue teams care about most at period close.

**Will this card catch a problem before period close?**
That is its main purpose. Discovered during the close, unbilled fulfilled orders force a scramble to create dozens of documents and can delay the close itself. Discovered live, within days of the script breaking, the fix is small and the back-fill is manageable. Running this card continuously turns a period-end fire drill into a routine daily worklist, which is the difference between a clean close and a late one.

**NetSuite vs other ERPs on the order-to-bill gap?**
Every ERP integrated with a storefront has an order-to-bill conversion step that can fail independently of the order-import step, so the gap is universal. SAP S/4HANA bills through its sales and distribution billing run, and a failed or incomplete billing run leaves delivered orders unbilled; Oracle ERP Cloud bills through order management and receivables, with the same exposure if the autoinvoice process stalls. The failure mode is identical, and Vortex IQ catches it the same way regardless of ERP: by matching fulfilled commerce orders against the ERP's billing documents rather than trusting the conversion step to have run.
