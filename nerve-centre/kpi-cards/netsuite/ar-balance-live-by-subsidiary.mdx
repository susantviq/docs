> Live total open receivables, broken out per OneWorld subsidiary.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> The total open accounts receivable across the business right now, decomposed by OneWorld subsidiary. It answers the executive question "how much money are we owed, and by which part of the group?" without waiting for a month-end report. This is a live balance, not a period flow, so it moves every time an invoice is issued or a payment is applied.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | Sum of open balances on all approved AR transactions (invoices net of payments, credit memos, and deposits), grouped by subsidiary. |
| **Currency** | Reporting currency for the consolidated total; each subsidiary line is also available in its local functional currency. |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Per-subsidiary breakout is the whole point of the card. Respects dashboard filter; defaults to all subs. |
| **Time window** | `RT` (real-time live balance, not a period aggregate) |
| **Alert trigger** | None at headline; informational. Some workspaces add a per-sub absolute-balance ceiling. |
| **Roles** | owner, finance |
| **Balance type** | Open balance (amount remaining), not invoiced total. |
| **Excludes** | Fully settled invoices; draft and unapproved transactions; inter-co AR eliminated at consolidation. |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

The card sums the amount remaining on every open AR transaction and groups the result by the owning subsidiary. Invoices add to the balance; applied customer payments, credit memos, and deposits reduce it. Each subsidiary's balance is computed in its functional currency and then translated to the reporting currency for the consolidated headline, while the per-sub lines can be viewed either in local currency or in the common reporting currency depending on the dashboard setting. Because this is a live balance rather than a period flow, there is no "since" date: it reflects the state of the receivables ledger at the moment the card refreshes.

On a OneWorld account, inter-company receivables that net out at consolidation are eliminated from the consolidated total, so the sum of the per-sub lines viewed gross can exceed the consolidated headline by the inter-co amount. The card is informational by design; it is a scoreboard of where the group's uncollected cash is sitting, not an alerting gauge. To judge whether any single subsidiary's balance is healthy or worrying, pair it with the aging and DSO cards described below, which add the time dimension this live snapshot deliberately omits.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A mid-market outdoor-equipment group on NetSuite OneWorld, selling DTC through Shopify and wholesale into specialist retailers across three regions. Four subsidiaries: a US parent (USD), a UK sub (GBP), a German EU sub (EUR), and a small Australian sub (AUD). Group revenue roughly $95M. Reporting currency USD. The card is read live on 22 Jun 26.

| Subsidiary | Local currency | Local balance | Reporting (USD) | Share of group AR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US parent | USD | $6,400,000 | $6,400,000 | 52.0% |
| UK sub | GBP | £2,050,000 | $2,624,000 | 21.3% |
| EU (DE) sub | EUR | EUR 2,380,000 | $2,594,000 | 21.1% |
| AU sub | AUD | A$1,070,000 | $690,000 | 5.6% |
| **Consolidated open AR** | | | **$12,308,000** | **100%** |
| (Inter-co AR eliminated) | | | ($430,000) | |
| Gross sum of sub lines | | | $12,738,000 | |

Five things to notice:

1. **The consolidated $12.3M is the number an owner wants, but the per-sub split is where the decisions live.** A single headline AR figure tells you the group is owed a lot of money; it does not tell you whether that is a US wholesale concentration, a UK collections lag, or healthy growth in Germany. The breakout reframes the question from "how much are we owed" to "by whom, and is it where we expected it to be?" The US parent carrying 52% of group AR is unsurprising given it is the largest entity, but if the AU sub at 5.6% of AR is generating 12% of group revenue, that mismatch is interesting: either AU is collecting faster than the group (good) or it is under-invoicing relative to its sales (worth a look).

2. **This is a live balance, so it moves intraday and should not be reconciled against a period-flow card.** People new to the card sometimes try to tie it to invoiced revenue or cash collected over a window and are confused when the numbers do not match. They never will, because this is a stock not a flow: AR balance equals opening balance plus invoices issued minus cash applied minus credit memos, accumulated over all time, not over a 30-day window. The right mental model is a fuel gauge, not a trip meter. To understand why the balance is what it is, pair it with [Invoiced Revenue](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/invoiced-revenue) (what flowed in) and [Cash Collected](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/cash-collected) (what flowed out).

3. **The $430K inter-company elimination explains why the gross sum of sub lines exceeds the consolidated total.** When the US parent sells to the UK sub on an inter-co invoice, the UK sub owes the US parent and the US parent shows a receivable; at consolidation that receivable and its matching payable cancel because the group cannot owe money to itself. The card's consolidated headline uses post-elimination values, so it reads $12.308M, while naively adding the four sub lines gives $12.738M. This is not an error; it is correct consolidation accounting. Anyone reconciling the per-sub lines against the consolidated total must remember to back out inter-co AR, or they will chase a phantom $430K gap.

4. **FX translation means the consolidated number can move even when no customer pays or is invoiced.** The UK and EU subs together carry over $5.2M of the group's AR in non-USD currencies. A 3% strengthening of the dollar against sterling and the euro would shrink the reported consolidated balance by roughly $150K overnight, purely from translation, with no operational change whatsoever. For an owner watching the live number, this matters: a dip in the consolidated AR balance is not automatically good news (faster collection) and could simply be FX. Reading each sub in local currency alongside the consolidated USD view separates the operational move from the translation move, which is why the card exposes both.

5. **The balance alone says nothing about quality; pair it with aging to know if the money is collectible.** A $12.3M AR balance that is 90% current and 10% aged is a healthy growing business; the same $12.3M that is 40% aged past 60 days is a slow-motion cash crisis. This card deliberately omits the time dimension to stay a clean live scoreboard, which means it must be read next to [AR Aging 60+ Days](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ar-aging-60-days) and [Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/days-sales-outstanding). The pattern that should worry an owner is a rising AR balance with a simultaneously rising aged share, because that means receivables are growing and aging at the same time, which is the textbook signature of a collections function that cannot keep up with sales growth.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

This card maps directly onto NetSuite's receivables ledger, so it reconciles cleanly against the standard balance views. The card is a live open-AR balance grouped by subsidiary, which is exactly what the consolidated Balance Sheet AR line plus a per-sub breakout shows. The closest native views to triangulate against:

> **Reports → Financial → Balance Sheet** (the Accounts Receivable line is the consolidated total; run it consolidated for the group headline)
> **Reports → Financial → Balance Sheet by Subsidiary** (the per-sub breakout that matches the card's lines, before inter-co elimination)
> **Reports → Customers/Receivables → A/R Aging Summary** (the total open AR should tie to the card; the report also shows the time decomposition)
> **Reports → Customers/Receivables → Customer Balance** (customer-level open balances; sum to the sub or group total)
> **Saved search**: a Transaction saved search on open AR transactions, summing amount remaining grouped by subsidiary, in local and reporting currency
> **Reports → Financial → Consolidated Balance Sheet** (confirms the inter-co elimination that separates the gross sub-line sum from the consolidated headline)

The cleanest tie is between the card's consolidated headline and the consolidated Balance Sheet AR line, taken as-of the same instant. Because the card is live and the Balance Sheet is run as-of a chosen date, run the report as-of "now" to compare, and remember that any posting between the two refreshes will create a small drift.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from the native Balance Sheet:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **As-of timing** | Either | The card is live; the Balance Sheet is run as-of a date. Postings between refreshes shift the comparison. Run the report as-of now to minimise drift. |
| **Inter-co elimination** | Card consolidated reads lower than gross sub sum | The consolidated headline removes inter-co AR; naively summing per-sub lines double-counts it. Use the Consolidated Balance Sheet, not the per-sub one, to tie the headline. |
| **FX translation rate** | Small | Per-sub balances translated at the card's FX cadence; the Balance Sheet may use a different period-end or current rate. |
| **Unapplied payments and deposits** | Either | Cash received but not applied may show differently depending on whether deposits are netted into AR. |
| **Credit memo treatment** | Either | Open credit memos may or may not be netted against the AR balance depending on configuration. |
| **Non-AR account inclusion** | Either | Some saved searches include statistical or non-trade receivable accounts that the Balance Sheet AR line excludes. |
| **Draft and pending-approval invoices** | Card reads lower | The card counts approved transactions only; a saved search that includes drafts will read higher. |
| **Posting period vs live** | Either | A Balance Sheet run for a closed period reflects that period end; the live card reflects all activity since, including the current open period. |

The headline difference should be near zero when the card's consolidated total is compared against the Consolidated Balance Sheet AR line as-of the same moment. A persistent gap almost always traces to inter-co elimination (comparing against the per-sub Balance Sheet by mistake) or to unapplied payments.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Why is there no alert on this card?**
Because a raw AR balance has no universally "bad" level. A $12M balance is healthy for a $95M group and alarming for a $20M one, and a rising balance can mean either growing sales (good) or slowing collections (bad). The card is deliberately informational; the alerting lives on the cards that add context, namely [AR Aging 60+ Days](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ar-aging-60-days) and [DSO Increase Alert](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/dso-increase-alert). Some workspaces do add a per-sub absolute-balance ceiling for a single dominant customer concentration, but that is a workspace choice rather than a default.

**Why does the consolidated total not equal the sum of the subsidiary lines?**
Inter-company elimination. When one sub owes another, that receivable cancels at the group level because the group cannot owe itself. The consolidated headline uses post-elimination values, so it is lower than the gross sum of the per-sub lines by the inter-co AR amount. This is correct consolidation accounting, not a bug. Use the [Intercompany Balance](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ns-intercompany-balance) card to see the eliminated amount directly.

**How does multi-currency affect the live number?**
Each sub's balance is computed in its functional currency and translated to the reporting currency for the consolidated headline. A currency move changes the reported consolidated total without any customer paying or being invoiced. To separate operational moves from translation moves, read each sub in its local currency alongside the consolidated USD view. For period-over-period comparison of the consolidated number, lock the FX cadence so translation noise does not masquerade as a collections change.

**Is this a flow or a balance?**
A balance, a stock, a snapshot. It is the cumulative amount owed at this instant, not the amount invoiced or collected over a window. Do not try to reconcile it against [Invoiced Revenue](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/invoiced-revenue) or [Cash Collected](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/cash-collected), which are flows. The relationship is opening balance plus invoices minus collections minus credit memos equals current balance, accumulated over all time.

**B2B versus DTC, how does the mix show up?**
DTC sales are paid at checkout and generate almost no AR, so a sub that is mostly DTC will show a small AR balance relative to its revenue. A sub that is mostly B2B-on-terms will show a large AR balance relative to its revenue. Comparing each sub's AR share against its revenue share (using [Revenue by Subsidiary](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ns-revenue-by-subsidiary)) reveals the channel mix and flags any sub whose AR is disproportionate to its sales.

**Single-subsidiary versus OneWorld behaviour?**
On a single-sub account the card collapses to a single live AR balance in the functional currency, with no per-sub breakout, no consolidation, and no inter-co elimination. The reconciliation against the Balance Sheet AR line is then exact as-of the same instant. OneWorld adds the per-sub breakout (the card's headline feature), the FX translation layer, and the inter-co elimination, all of which are described above.

**Does period close change the live balance?**
The live balance is independent of close; it reflects all posted activity regardless of which period is open. However, adjusting entries and late cash application posted during close will move the balance, so a reading taken mid-close can differ from one taken after the period is locked. For a board number that ties to the audited Balance Sheet, take the reading after close; for an operational scoreboard, the live number is the right one.

**Why does my AR balance keep rising even though collections are fine?**
Usually because sales are growing. AR balance scales roughly with revenue at constant terms and collection speed, so a growing business will naturally carry a growing AR balance. The balance rising is only a problem when it rises faster than revenue, which shows up as rising DSO or a rising aged share. Read this card next to [DSO](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/days-sales-outstanding) to tell growth-driven AR from collection-driven AR.

**Does it include customer deposits and prepayments?**
Configurable. Customer deposits are a liability, not a receivable, so most configurations net them against the customer's open invoices or exclude them from the AR balance entirely. Check whether your workspace nets deposits, and match that to whatever your native Balance Sheet does, because a mismatch here is a common source of a small persistent gap.

**Can a single large customer distort the per-sub line?**
Yes, and that is often the most useful thing the card reveals. If one wholesale account makes up 40% of a sub's AR, that sub's balance is really a bet on one customer's solvency. The card surfaces the sub-level concentration; drill into [Customer Balance](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ns-top-customers-revenue) detail and [High-Value Overdue Invoices](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/high-value-overdue-invoices) to see whether the concentration is current or aging.

**NetSuite versus other ERPs on this?**
NetSuite's OneWorld consolidation, with its automatic inter-co elimination and multi-currency translation, is one of its genuine strengths, and this card leans on it directly. Oracle ERP Cloud and SAP S/4HANA both produce consolidated AR with sub-level breakouts through their financial-consolidation modules, though the setup is heavier. The value Vortex IQ adds is the live, always-on, per-sub scoreboard with the one-click joins to aging, DSO, and revenue, rather than a month-end consolidation report you have to commission and run.
