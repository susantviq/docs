> Count of customer records flagged on credit hold (manual ON or automatic AUTO from credit-limit rules).

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> The number of customer records currently flagged on credit hold, counting both manual holds set explicitly by a person and automatic holds triggered by NetSuite's credit-limit rules. A customer on credit hold cannot have new orders approved without finance intervention, so this count is a direct measure of how much of your customer base is currently blocked from buying. A rising count means sales are being throttled by credit risk, intentionally or not.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | Distinct customer records where the credit-hold status is ON (manual) or AUTO (set by credit-limit logic). |
| **Currency** | n/a (a count of records, not a value). |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. OneWorld accounts count holds across subs; a customer shared across subs may be held in one and not another. |
| **Time window** | `RT` (real-time count of currently held customers) |
| **Alert trigger** | Greater than 5 customers on hold |
| **Roles** | owner, finance, operations |
| **Hold types** | Manual (ON, set by a user) and Automatic (AUTO, set by credit-limit rule). The card sums both. |
| **Excludes** | Customers with hold status OFF; inactive customer records; prospects with no transaction history in some configurations. |

## Calculation

The card counts the distinct customer records whose credit-hold status is currently ON or AUTO. ON is a manual flag a person has set, typically by finance after a payment dispute, a bounced payment, or a risk decision. AUTO is set by NetSuite's credit-limit logic, which can automatically place a customer on hold when their open balance breaches their assigned credit limit. The card sums both into a single live count, because from the order book's perspective the effect is identical: orders for that customer will be blocked pending review.

The count is a real-time snapshot, not a period flow, so it rises the instant a customer is placed on hold and falls the instant a hold is cleared. On a OneWorld account the same legal customer can exist as separate records under different subsidiaries, so a buyer might be held in one sub and free to transact in another; the card counts records, and the dashboard subsidiary filter scopes which records are in view. The card does not distinguish why a customer is held; pair it with the credit-utilisation and aging cards below to understand whether the holds are driven by genuine overdue balances or by limits set too tight for a growing account.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A specialty food and beverage distributor on NetSuite OneWorld, selling DTC subscriptions through Shopify and wholesale into restaurants and independent grocers. Two subsidiaries: a US parent (USD) and a UK sub (GBP). Group revenue roughly $48M, heavily B2B on net-30 terms. The card is read live on 22 Jun 26.

| Customer | Sub | Hold type | Open balance | Credit limit | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harbor Grocers | US | AUTO | $84,200 | $75,000 | Balance over limit |
| Summit Cafe Group | US | AUTO | $61,500 | $60,000 | Balance over limit |
| Riverside Foods | US | ON | $38,000 | $90,000 | Disputed invoice |
| Oakwood Markets | US | AUTO | $52,300 | $50,000 | Balance over limit |
| Pennine Provisions | UK | AUTO | £41,000 | £40,000 | Balance over limit |
| Dale & Sons | UK | ON | £12,000 | £60,000 | Bounced payment |
| Calder Catering | UK | AUTO | £33,500 | £30,000 | Balance over limit |
| **Total on hold (this card)** | | **7** | | | |

Five things to notice:

1. **At 7 the card is over the alert of 5, and the split between AUTO and ON tells you whether this is a limits problem or a behaviour problem.** Five of the seven holds are AUTO, triggered purely because the customer's open balance crept over their credit limit, and only two are manual ON holds tied to an actual incident (a dispute and a bounced payment). That split is the first diagnostic. A book dominated by AUTO holds usually means credit limits have not kept pace with account growth: good customers buying more are tripping limits set when they were smaller. A book dominated by ON holds means finance is reacting to genuine payment trouble. The card surfaces the count; the AUTO-versus-ON mix points at the cause.

2. **Every customer on hold is a customer whose next order will not ship without a manual override, so this count is a direct drag on revenue.** Harbor Grocers at $84K of balance against a $75K limit is over by only $9K, yet the AUTO hold blocks their entire next order regardless of size. If that customer tries to place a $20K weekly order while held, it stalls in the approval queue until someone in finance either raises the limit, takes a payment, or overrides the hold. Multiply that across seven accounts and the card is quietly describing a revenue chokepoint: orders that the customer wants to place and the business wants to fulfil, blocked by a credit flag. The operational urgency is real because food-and-beverage wholesale runs on frequent reorders, so a hold that lingers a few days can cost several order cycles.

3. **The AUTO holds clustered just over their limits are the easy wins; the ON holds need a judgement call.** Four of the five AUTO holds (Harbor, Summit, Oakwood, Pennine, Calder) are only marginally over limit, the kind of breach that a single applied payment or a modest limit increase clears immediately. These are quick, low-risk releases for a finance team to action this morning. The two ON holds are different: Riverside is held over a disputed invoice (resolve the dispute, then release), and Dale & Sons is held after a bounced payment (confirm the replacement payment cleared, then release). The card lets operations triage by hold type rather than treating all seven as one undifferentiated queue, which is the difference between clearing five holds before lunch and letting all seven sit because they look equally daunting.

4. **A rising count on this card is the downstream consequence of trends visible earlier on the aging and utilisation cards.** Credit holds do not appear from nowhere; they are the end of a chain. First receivables age, which shows up on [AR Aging 60+ Days](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ar-aging-60-days). As balances grow without being paid down, [Customer Credit Utilisation](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/customer-credit-utilisation) climbs toward 100%. When it crosses the limit, NetSuite flips the customer to AUTO hold and this count rises. So a spike here is rarely the first signal; it is confirmation that an aging or utilisation trend has now reached the order book. The most valuable use of this card is as the trigger to look back up the chain, because by the time a customer is on hold the problem has been building for weeks.

5. **On a OneWorld account the same buyer can be on hold in one sub and trading freely in another, which is both a feature and a trap.** Harbor Grocers might be a US-sub record on hold while the same parent company buys through a separate UK-sub record that is well within its limit. That separation is correct if the two subs extend independent credit, but it can let a genuinely troubled customer keep buying through the unaffected sub while finance thinks they are blocked. The card counts records, so a customer held in one sub and free in another shows as one held record, not as "the customer is blocked everywhere". For a shared customer with real payment trouble, the right move is to review the credit relationship across all subs, not just the one that tripped the hold.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

Credit hold is a native customer-record field in NetSuite, so this card reconciles directly against the customer list filtered on hold status. The card counts records where the hold status is ON or AUTO. The closest native views to triangulate against:

> **Lists → Relationships → Customers** (filter the list view on the Credit Hold field; the count of ON plus AUTO records is the card)
> **Reports → Customers/Receivables → Customer Credit Hold** style saved search (a saved search filtered to hold status ON or AUTO, grouped by subsidiary)
> **Setup → Accounting → Accounting Preferences** (the customer-credit-limit handling and whether AUTO hold is enabled account-wide)
> **Customer record → Financial tab** (the per-customer Credit Hold setting: Auto, On, or Off, plus the credit limit and current balance)
> **Saved search**: a Customer saved search with a filter on credit hold equal to On or Auto, returning name, subsidiary, balance, and limit for the triage list

The single most common reconciliation question is whether AUTO holds are counted, because a customer set to AUTO is only effectively on hold when their balance has actually breached the limit. NetSuite's AUTO setting is a rule, not a state: the customer is on hold dynamically when over limit and off hold when under it. The card reflects the effective held state, so a customer set to AUTO but currently under limit should not be counted, and a discrepancy with a naive list filter on "Credit Hold = Auto" usually comes from that distinction.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a native customer-list count:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AUTO setting vs AUTO effective hold** | Either | A customer set to AUTO is only effectively held when over limit. A list filter on "Credit Hold = Auto" counts the setting; the card counts the effective held state. |
| **As-of timing** | Either | The card is live; balances move and limits change, flipping AUTO holds on and off between refreshes. A list run minutes apart can differ. |
| **Inactive customer records** | Card reads lower | The card excludes inactive records by default; a raw list view may include them. |
| **Per-sub records for one buyer** | Card may read higher | One legal customer with separate records per subsidiary counts as multiple records if held in more than one sub. |
| **Prospect and lead records** | Either | Depending on configuration, prospect-stage records with a hold flag may be included or excluded. |
| **Parent-child customer hierarchies** | Either | Holds set at a parent that cascade to children, or set independently per child, change the count depending on how the hierarchy is configured. |
| **Manual override in progress** | Small | A customer mid-release (hold being cleared) can read held on one side and free on the other for a brief window. |
| **Credit-limit currency** | Small | On OneWorld, the balance-versus-limit comparison that drives AUTO holds happens in the customer's currency; translation timing can momentarily flip a borderline customer. |

Reconciliation is usually exact once the AUTO-setting-versus-effective-hold distinction is understood and both sides exclude inactive records. A gap larger than a customer or two almost always traces to one side counting the AUTO setting and the other counting the effective state.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**What is the difference between ON and AUTO holds?**
ON is a manual hold a person set deliberately, usually finance responding to a specific incident like a disputed invoice, a bounced payment, or a risk decision. AUTO is a dynamic hold applied by NetSuite's credit-limit logic when a customer's open balance breaches their credit limit, and it clears automatically when the balance falls back under the limit. The card sums both because the order-book effect is identical, but the AUTO-versus-ON mix is the first thing to read: AUTO-heavy means limits may be too tight, ON-heavy means real payment incidents.

**Why is a customer on AUTO hold one moment and off the next?**
Because AUTO holds are dynamic. The customer is effectively held only while their balance exceeds their limit, so applying a payment, issuing a credit memo, or raising the limit can clear the hold instantly, and a new invoice can re-trip it. This is why the card is real-time and why a count taken minutes apart can differ. It also means AUTO holds often resolve themselves as customers pay down balances, without any manual action.

**How does OneWorld change the count?**
The same legal customer can have separate records under different subsidiaries, each with its own credit limit and hold status. The card counts records, so a buyer held in two subs counts as two, and a buyer held in one sub while trading freely in another counts as one. The dashboard subsidiary filter scopes which records are in view. For a customer with genuine payment trouble, review the credit relationship across all subs rather than treating a single-sub hold as the whole picture.

**Does multi-currency affect AUTO holds?**
Yes, at the margin. The balance-versus-limit comparison that triggers an AUTO hold happens in the customer's transaction currency, and on a OneWorld account FX translation timing can momentarily flip a borderline customer on or off hold as rates move. For the overwhelming majority of holds the breach is clear enough that FX is irrelevant, but for a customer sitting exactly at their limit, currency movement can be the deciding factor.

**B2B versus DTC, does this card apply to both?**
This is overwhelmingly a B2B card. DTC customers pay at checkout, so they carry no open balance and cannot trip a credit hold in the classic sense; a DTC-only book reads zero here. B2B is where credit holds live, because B2B sells on terms and NetSuite enforces credit limits against the open balance. A hybrid business reads this card as a measure of its wholesale arm's credit health, with the DTC side contributing nothing to the count.

**Single-subsidiary versus OneWorld behaviour?**
On a single-sub account the count is simply the held customer records in that one entity, with no cross-sub record splitting. OneWorld introduces the possibility of one buyer appearing as multiple records held in different subs, and the discipline of reviewing a troubled customer across all their sub records. The AUTO-versus-ON interpretation applies identically to both.

**Does period close affect the count?**
Not directly, since the count is a live status independent of the close calendar. Indirectly, the close process often involves applying outstanding cash and posting credit memos, which pay down balances and can clear AUTO holds, so the held count may dip during and just after close as receivables are tidied up. A count taken mid-close can therefore differ from one taken before reconciliation activity began.

**Can a customer be on hold without an overdue balance?**
Yes, in two ways. A manual ON hold can be set for any reason regardless of whether anything is overdue, for example a risk decision on a new account or a hold pending a contract dispute. And an AUTO hold can trip on a customer whose balance is entirely current but simply large enough to exceed the limit, which is common for a fast-growing account whose limit has not been raised. So a hold is not proof of late payment; read it alongside [AR Aging 60+ Days](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/ar-aging-60-days) to separate over-limit-but-current from genuinely overdue.

**Why might the card differ from a quick filter on my customer list?**
Almost always the AUTO distinction. A list filtered on "Credit Hold = Auto" counts every customer set to the AUTO rule, including those currently under their limit and therefore not actually held. The card counts the effective held state, so it excludes AUTO-set customers who are currently within limit. Match the definitions before concluding the numbers disagree.

**How do I bring the count down without just raising limits everywhere?**
Triage by hold type, as the worked example shows. Clear the AUTO holds that are marginally over limit with a payment application or a justified limit increase for genuinely growing accounts. Resolve the ON holds on their merits (settle the dispute, confirm the replacement payment). Blanket-raising limits to clear the count is the wrong move because it removes the credit control that the holds exist to provide; the goal is to clear holds that should not be there while keeping the ones that are protecting you.

**NetSuite versus other ERPs on credit hold?**
NetSuite's credit-hold model, with its distinct manual ON and dynamic AUTO states tied to the credit limit, is well suited to this card and reconciles cleanly. Oracle ERP Cloud and SAP S/4HANA both implement credit management through dedicated credit-control modules, often with more elaborate scoring and exposure rules but a similar core concept of blocking orders when exposure exceeds a limit. The value Vortex IQ adds is the live count sitting next to the upstream utilisation and aging cards and the downstream blocked-orders card, so the credit-hold count is read as one link in a visible chain rather than an isolated number on a customer record.
