> Count of commerce buyers with no matching NetSuite customer record.

## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> The number of buyers who exist in your commerce platform (Shopify, BigCommerce, Adobe Commerce, or a marketplace) but have no matching customer record in NetSuite. Every absent customer is a buyer whose orders, revenue, and cash may not be flowing cleanly into the financial system, which means the books and the storefront disagree about who your customers are. A rising count signals a sync gap between commerce and ERP that quietly corrupts revenue recognition, AR, and reporting.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | Distinct commerce-platform buyers (by email or external customer ID) with no matching NetSuite customer record. |
| **Currency** | n/a (a count of records, not a value). |
| **Subsidiary scope** | Respects dashboard filter. On OneWorld, the expected subsidiary for a commerce buyer is part of the match logic. |
| **Time window** | `7D` (buyers seen in commerce over the last 7 days with no NetSuite match) |
| **Alert trigger** | Greater than 10 absent customers |
| **Roles** | owner, finance, operations |
| **Match basis** | Email address and/or external customer ID, depending on the integration mapping. |
| **Excludes** | Guest checkouts intentionally not synced; archived NetSuite records in some configurations; test accounts. |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Calculation

The card compares the set of buyers active in your commerce platform over the recent window against the set of customer records in NetSuite, and counts the commerce buyers that have no match. Matching is typically done on email address, external customer ID, or both, depending on how the commerce-to-NetSuite integration is mapped. A buyer who placed an order on the storefront but whose corresponding customer record was never created or synced into NetSuite is counted as absent. The card is a count of records, not a financial value, but each absent record usually sits behind real orders and real revenue.

The window is recent (last 7 days) so the card focuses on the buyers most likely to have orders still in flight, where a missing record actively blocks order-to-cash. On a OneWorld account the match is more involved, because a commerce buyer should map not only to a NetSuite customer record but to the correct subsidiary for the channel and region they bought through; a buyer present in NetSuite but under the wrong subsidiary is a related but distinct problem covered by a sibling card. The worked example below shows how absent customers accumulate and why even a modest count is worth chasing.

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A homewares brand on NetSuite OneWorld, selling DTC through Shopify in the US and through a BigCommerce storefront in the UK, plus a small wholesale arm. Two subsidiaries: a US parent (USD) and a UK sub (GBP). Group revenue roughly $35M. The commerce platforms create or update buyers continuously; the integration is supposed to create matching NetSuite customer records nightly. The card is read on 22 Jun 26, covering buyers active in the last 7 days.

| Source | Buyers active (7d) | Matched in NetSuite | Absent | Likely cause |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shopify (US) | 2,140 | 2,131 | 9 | Sync failed on records with apostrophes in name |
| BigCommerce (UK) | 880 | 874 | 6 | New buyers since last successful nightly sync |
| Wholesale (manual) | 12 | 12 | 0 | Created directly in NetSuite |
| **Total absent (this card)** | | | **15** | |

Five things to notice:

1. **At 15 the card is over the alert of 10, and the source breakdown immediately localises the problem to two distinct sync failures.** Nine absent buyers on Shopify cluster around a data-formatting issue (names with apostrophes failing the record-creation step), and six on BigCommerce look like ordinary lag (new buyers since the last successful nightly run). Those are two different problems with two different fixes: the Shopify cluster needs an integration-mapping fix to handle the special characters, while the BigCommerce six will likely clear on the next successful sync. Without the source split, an operator sees "15 absent" as one undifferentiated alarm; with it, they see one bug to fix and one batch to let drain, which is the difference between a targeted fix and a panicked full re-sync.

2. **Every absent buyer is a break in order-to-cash, because their orders have nowhere to land in the financial system.** When a Shopify buyer with no NetSuite record places an order, the order either fails to create a NetSuite sales order, creates one against a fallback or generic customer (corrupting that customer's history), or sits in an error queue. None of those outcomes is benign: the first means revenue is invisible to finance, the second means revenue is misattributed, the third means the order is stuck. So a count of 15 absent customers is really a count of 15 broken order pipelines, and on a brand doing meaningful DTC volume those 15 buyers can represent a non-trivial slice of recent revenue that the books do not yet reflect.

3. **This card is the customer-side companion to the order-side gap, and they should be read together.** A buyer absent from NetSuite is the root cause; an order without a matching NetSuite sales order is the symptom. The [Commerce Orders Without NetSuite SO](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/commerce-orders-without-netsuite-so) card counts the downstream orders, while this card counts the upstream customers, and the two move together: fix the absent customers and the orphaned orders usually resolve, because the orders finally have a customer to attach to. Reading them as a pair turns "we have a sync problem somewhere" into "we have 15 missing customers causing N orphaned orders worth roughly X in revenue", which is the version finance can act on.

4. **A persistent non-zero count quietly corrupts every downstream financial number, which is why this is a hero card.** If buyers and their orders are not flowing into NetSuite cleanly, then invoiced revenue understates, AR is incomplete, customer-level reporting is wrong, and the [Revenue Gap vs Commerce](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/revenue-gap-vs-commerce) card will show storefront sales that the GL never received. The danger is that a small steady count looks harmless on any single day but compounds: 15 absent buyers a week, never fully cleared, becomes hundreds of customers and thousands of orphaned orders over a quarter, at which point the commerce-to-ERP reconciliation becomes a major project rather than a daily hygiene task. The hero status reflects that this is a foundational data-integrity gauge, not a peripheral nicety.

5. **On a OneWorld account, "absent" and "present but mis-subsidiaried" are different failures that this card deliberately separates.** A UK buyer who has no NetSuite record at all is counted here. A UK buyer who does have a NetSuite record but was created under the US subsidiary by mistake is not absent (they exist), so they are not on this card; they are on the [OneWorld Customers Missing Subsidiary](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/oneworld-customers-missing-subsidiary) card instead. Keeping the two distinct matters because the fixes differ: an absent customer needs to be created, while a mis-subsidiaried one needs to be reassigned, and reassignment has knock-on effects on intercompany flows and revenue rules that simple creation does not. The clean separation is what lets an operator route each problem to the right remedy.

## Reconciling against NetSuite

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in NetSuite:**

This card lives at the boundary between your commerce platform and NetSuite, so reconciliation means comparing the two customer lists. The card counts commerce buyers with no NetSuite match. The closest native views to triangulate against:

> **Lists → Relationships → Customers** (the full NetSuite customer list; the set the commerce buyers are matched against)
> **Customer record → external ID and email fields** (the match keys; confirm the integration writes the external ID the commerce side expects)
> **Setup → Integration → integration record / connector logs** (the sync job status and any record-creation failures, the usual source of absent customers)
> **Saved search**: a Customer saved search returning email and external ID, exported and compared against the commerce platform's customer export to find buyers present there but not here
> **Lists → Web Site → web store customer mappings** (where applicable, the storefront-to-customer mapping that should exist for every buyer)

On the commerce side, the comparison set comes from the relevant connector: Shopify customers, BigCommerce customers, or Adobe Commerce customers, filtered to those active in the recent window. The most common reconciliation question is the match key. If the integration matches on external ID but a buyer was created in NetSuite manually without that ID, the buyer exists but the card may count them as absent because the key does not line up. Confirm which key the match uses before concluding a buyer is genuinely missing rather than merely unmatched.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a manual list comparison:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Match key (email vs external ID)** | Either | If the integration matches on external ID and a record was created manually without it, the buyer reads as absent even though they exist. Match the key on both sides. |
| **Guest checkout handling** | Card may read higher or lower | Some stores do not sync guest checkouts to NetSuite by design; whether they count as absent depends on configuration. |
| **Sync lag within the window** | Card may read higher | Buyers created in commerce since the last successful sync are genuinely absent now but will match after the next run. |
| **Email normalisation** | Either | Case differences, plus-addressing, or trailing whitespace can cause a real match to read as absent if normalisation differs between systems. |
| **Archived or inactive NetSuite records** | Either | A matching record that has been archived may not be counted as a match depending on configuration. |
| **Merged duplicate customers** | Either | If NetSuite merged two duplicates, the surviving record may carry only one external ID, leaving the other commerce buyer unmatched. |
| **Marketplace buyers** | Either | Marketplace orders sometimes use a single aggregate customer rather than per-buyer records, changing what "absent" means for those channels. |
| **Test and bot accounts** | Card may read higher | Test orders and bot traffic create commerce buyers that were never meant to sync; filtering differs between the card and a raw export. |

Reconciliation is usually exact once the match key and the guest-checkout policy are agreed and both sides exclude test accounts. A gap larger than the expected sync lag almost always traces to a match-key mismatch or an email-normalisation difference.

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**What does "absent" actually mean here?**
It means a buyer who placed an order or was created in your commerce platform within the recent window but who has no matching customer record in NetSuite under the match key the integration uses. It is not a value, it is a count of customer records, but each absent buyer typically has real orders behind them, so the count is a proxy for broken order-to-cash pipelines. The fix is either to create the missing record, repair the failed sync, or correct the match key so an existing record lines up.

**How does the match between commerce and NetSuite work?**
Usually on email address, external customer ID, or both, depending on how the connector is mapped. The cleanest setups match on a stable external ID written by the integration; email-only matching is more fragile because emails change, get reused, or differ by case and plus-addressing. If buyers you know exist keep reading as absent, the match key is the first thing to check, because a buyer can be fully present in NetSuite yet unmatched if the key the integration looks for is not on the record.

**Does this card apply to guest checkouts?**
Configurable. Many stores deliberately do not sync guest checkouts as named customers, instead booking them against a generic web-customer record, in which case guests should not count as absent. Other stores create a real customer for every order including guests. The card respects your configuration, so confirm the guest-checkout policy before reading the count, because a store that books guests to a generic customer will legitimately show fewer absent records than one that expects a record per buyer.

**How does OneWorld affect the count?**
On OneWorld the match should place a commerce buyer not only into NetSuite but into the correct subsidiary for their channel and region. A buyer with no record at all counts here as absent. A buyer who exists but under no subsidiary or the wrong one is a different failure, counted on [OneWorld Customers Missing Subsidiary](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/netsuite/oneworld-customers-missing-subsidiary). Keeping the two separate matters because the remedies differ: create versus reassign, and reassignment carries intercompany and revenue-rule consequences that creation does not.

**Why is this a hero card if it is just a count?**
Because customer-sync integrity is foundational to every downstream financial number. If buyers are missing, their orders and revenue do not flow cleanly into NetSuite, so invoiced revenue understates, AR is incomplete, customer reporting is wrong, and the commerce-versus-GL revenue gap widens. A small steady count compounds into a major reconciliation backlog over a quarter. The hero status reflects that this is a leading data-integrity gauge whose neglect corrupts the books, not a peripheral metric.

**B2B versus DTC, does the card read differently?**
Yes. DTC commerce generates high volumes of buyers, so the absolute count here can be larger and a small percentage gap still produces double-digit absent records. B2B wholesale customers are usually created directly in NetSuite and synced more carefully or entered manually, so they rarely go absent; the wholesale arm in the worked example shows zero. A hybrid business should expect almost all absent records to come from the DTC channels, which is also where the order volume and revenue exposure concentrate.

**Single-subsidiary versus OneWorld behaviour?**
On a single-sub account the match is simply "does this commerce buyer have a NetSuite customer record", with no subsidiary dimension, so the card is cleaner and the related missing-subsidiary card does not apply. OneWorld adds the requirement that the buyer map to the correct subsidiary, which introduces the present-but-mis-subsidiaried failure mode handled by the sibling card. The core "absent entirely" count behaves the same on both.

**Does period close affect this card?**
Not directly, since the card reads the live customer sets in both systems regardless of the accounting calendar. Indirectly, the finance team often cleans up absent customers and orphaned orders as part of close preparation, so the count may dip during close as the order-to-cash backlog is tidied. A count taken before close prep will generally be higher than one taken after.

**Why does the count never quite reach zero?**
Because there is almost always some legitimate sync lag: buyers created in commerce since the last successful sync run are genuinely absent at the moment of reading but will match after the next run. A small floor driven by lag is normal and healthy. What matters is that the count drains back toward that floor after each sync, rather than ratcheting upward, which would indicate a failing sync rather than ordinary lag. The alert at greater than 10 is set above the expected lag floor for a typical store.

**Could the card miss a buyer who is actually absent?**
It can under-count if test or bot accounts are aggressively filtered, or if a match key is too permissive and matches a buyer to the wrong NetSuite record (a false match). The more common failure is over-counting due to a match-key or email-normalisation mismatch, which makes present buyers look absent. Both are configuration issues rather than card defects; tuning the match key and the test-account filter resolves them.

**NetSuite versus other ERPs on customer sync?**
The absent-customer problem is universal to any commerce-to-ERP integration, not specific to NetSuite; Oracle ERP Cloud and SAP S/4HANA face the same boundary issue when fed by a storefront. NetSuite's external-ID model and SuiteScript-based integration give a reasonably clean match key when configured well, but the failure modes (failed record creation, key mismatches, sync lag) are the same everywhere. The value Vortex IQ adds is surfacing the gap continuously, splitting it by source, and pairing it with the order-side and revenue-gap cards so the commerce-to-ERP boundary is monitored as a system rather than discovered at quarter-end.
