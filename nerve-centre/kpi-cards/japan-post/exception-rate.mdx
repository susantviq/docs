## At a glance

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}
> Share of Japan Post consignments that threw a delivery exception in the trailing 30 days, compared with the prior 30-day period. An exception is any tracking scan that diverts a parcel from the normal accept to delivered path: absence redelivery (fuzai), address incomplete, refused, customs held (EMS), damaged, or lost. This is the leading indicator that predicts a falling on-time rate and a rising claims queue 24 to 72 hours later.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it counts** | `COUNT(shipments WHERE has_exception_scan = true) / COUNT(shipments WHERE accepted = true)` over the rolling 30-day window, expressed as a percentage, with the prior 30 days shown alongside (`vsP`). Each consignment counts once even if it logged several exception scans. |
| **Data source** | Japan Post Tracking Service (追跡サービス) status history. The Vortex IQ connector ingests the per-item tracking event list and classifies each Japanese status string (for example 持ち戻り "returned to depot", ご不在 "addressee absent", 通関手続中 "in customs clearance") into a normalised exception taxonomy. Domestic Yu-Pack and international EMS items both feed this card. |
| **What counts as an exception** | Absence / fuzai redelivery attempts, address incomplete or unreadable, addressee refused, held at customs (EMS), damaged in transit, lost / untraceable, and detained for prohibited-item check. A successful redelivery after a fuzai notice still scores the consignment as an exception, because it cost a failed first attempt. |
| **Return-leg inclusion** | **Outbound only.** Return-to-sender consignments are tracked separately on [Returned to Sender](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/returned-to-sender); they are excluded from this denominator to avoid double counting a parcel that already failed outbound. |
| **Time window** | `30D vsP` (rolling 30 days against the prior 30 days). The 30-day window smooths the heavy weekday despatch cycle and the absence-redelivery tail, which can run 1 to 3 days behind the first attempt. |
| **Alert trigger** | `>3%` exception rate over the window. For a merchant shipping 6,000 parcels a month that is roughly 180 exception consignments, the point at which the redelivery and customer-service load becomes material. |
| **Roles** | owner, operations |
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:tldr */}

## Worked example

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}
A Tokyo-based DTC speciality-foods brand, around 6,200 outbound parcels per month, ships 80 percent domestic Yu-Pack (ゆうパック) and 20 percent EMS to overseas customers (mostly US, Taiwan and Singapore). Reading taken at 09:00 JST on 14 Apr 26 for the trailing 30 days (15 Mar 26 to 14 Apr 26), compared with the prior period (13 Feb 26 to 14 Mar 26).

| Exception type | Consignments (30D) | Share of accepted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Absence redelivery (fuzai) | 168 | 2.7% |
| Address incomplete / unreadable | 41 | 0.66% |
| Held at customs (EMS) | 33 | 0.53% |
| Refused by addressee | 12 | 0.19% |
| Damaged / lost | 7 | 0.11% |
| **All exceptions (this card)** | **261** | **4.2%** |

Accepted consignments in the period: 6,210. The card reads **4.2 percent**, up from **3.1 percent** in the prior period, so the alert at `>3%` is tripped and the period-over-period delta is +1.1 points. Five things to notice:

1. **Absence (fuzai) dominates, as it almost always does in Japan.** Japanese delivery culture means many households are out during the day, and the carrier leaves an absence notice (不在票) rather than a card-only attempt. 2.7 percent absence is normal-to-high; the lever is not the carrier, it is offering Yu-Pack delivery-time-slot selection and PUDO / convenience-store (コンビニ受取) or locker (はこぽす) pickup at checkout. Promoting time-slot selection typically halves the fuzai rate.
2. **The +1.1 point jump is the story, not the absolute level.** A single high reading is noise; a period-over-period rise is signal. Check the despatch calendar against the prior period for an obvious cause (a product launch that shipped to a lot of new, unverified addresses will spike address-incomplete exceptions).
3. **Customs holds are an EMS-only sub-population.** 33 customs holds on roughly 1,240 EMS items is a 2.7 percent EMS customs-hold rate. If that climbs, the cause is usually CN23 declaration quality, check [EMS CN23 Customs Document Generation Failure Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/ems-cn23-customs-document-generation-failure-rate); a failed auto-generation forces a manual CN23 that is more error-prone and gets held.
4. **Exception rate leads the delivery cards.** Today's 4.2 percent will show up as a dip on [On-Time Delivery Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/on-time-delivery-rate) and a rise on [Late Shipments](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/late-shipments) within 24 to 72 hours. Treat this card as the early-warning dial.
5. **Damaged / lost is small but expensive.** 7 consignments is rounding on the rate, but each one is a likely claim. Cross-check [Open Claims](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/open-claims) and [Claim Value (open)](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/claim-value-open); damaged perishable food is the worst case because the customer experience is poor even when the claim pays out.
{/* /HUMAN_AUTHORED:worked_example */}

## Reconciling against the source

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:reconcile */}
**Where to look in Japan Post's own tooling:**

The customer-facing record is the [Japan Post Tracking Service](https://trackings.post.japanpost.jp/services/srv/search/?locale=en) (追跡サービス), which shows the per-item status history that this card is built from. Business shippers using the Japan Post web-shipping tools (Yu-Pack Print / ゆうパックプリント and the EMS / international shipping portal) see batch despatch records there, but Japan Post does not publish a single merchant-level exception-rate report the way some Western carriers do. The like-for-like check is therefore to export your consignment list, pull the status history per item through the tracking service or the connector's ingested events, and count the consignments whose history contains an exception scan.

**Why our number may legitimately differ from a manual count off the tracking portal:**

| Reason | Direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scan timestamps are carrier-local (JST)** | Boundary days off | The Japan Post tracking history records each scan in Japan Standard Time. The card stores in UTC and presents in your workspace timezone. For a 30-day window the boundary-day shift averages out, but a single-day comparison against the portal can be off by one day. |
| **Tracking-event ingestion latency** | Ours can lag | Japan Post status updates are not real-time; depot and delivery scans batch through the tracking service with a typical 30-minute to several-hour delay, and rural depots can lag longer. A parcel that just threw a fuzai scan may not appear as an exception in our index for up to a few hours. |
| **Exception classification** | Either | We classify each Japanese status string into a normalised taxonomy. A manual count may include or exclude statuses differently (for example, whether "in customs clearance" alone counts as an exception, or only "held at customs"). Agree the definition before comparing. |
| **Redelivery counted once** | Ours lower per-scan | A parcel with three fuzai attempts logs three exception scans in the portal but counts as one exception consignment here. If you count scans rather than consignments off the portal, the portal number will be higher. |
| **Outbound only** | Ours lower | Return-to-sender legs are excluded from this denominator; the raw tracking export includes them. |

## Known limitations / merchant FAQs

{/* HUMAN_AUTHORED:faqs */}
**Why is my absence (fuzai) exception rate so high compared with carriers in other countries?**
This is structural to Japan, not a Japan Post failing. A large share of Japanese households are out during delivery hours, and the carrier leaves an absence notice (不在票) for redelivery rather than leaving the parcel. The fix is upstream of the carrier: offer Yu-Pack delivery-time-slot selection, convenience-store pickup (コンビニ受取), and locker (はこぽす) delivery at checkout. Merchants who promote time-slot selection routinely cut their fuzai exceptions by 40 to 60 percent.

**Does a successful redelivery still count as an exception?**
Yes. The consignment scores as an exception because it cost a failed first attempt, redelivery handling, and (usually) a customer interaction to arrange the second attempt. If you want the pure hard-failure view, read [Failed Deliveries](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/failed-deliveries); if you want the first-attempt success view, read [First-Attempt Delivery Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/first-attempt-delivery-rate).

**My EMS customs-hold exceptions spiked but domestic is flat. What happened?**
Customs holds are an EMS-only sub-population and are usually a declaration-quality problem, not a carrier problem. Check [EMS CN23 Customs Document Generation Failure Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/ems-cn23-customs-document-generation-failure-rate); when CN23 auto-generation fails, the manual fallback declaration is more error-prone and gets detained at the destination customs authority. Also check for destination-country rule changes (de minimis thresholds, prohibited-item lists) that can hold a whole corridor at once.

**Why does the rate look different from a count I made off the tracking portal?**
Three usual reasons. (1) The portal logs every scan, so a parcel with three fuzai attempts is three lines there but one exception consignment here. (2) Scan timestamps are JST on the portal and the card normalises to your workspace timezone, so boundary days differ. (3) Classification: agree which statuses count as exceptions before comparing, "in customs clearance" is normal transit for EMS and we do not treat it as an exception on its own.

**How quickly does this card react to a problem?**
Faster than the delivery cards but not instantly. Japan Post tracking events batch with a typical 30-minute to several-hour latency, and rural depots can lag longer, so allow a few hours before a real spike is fully reflected. Use this as the leading indicator and confirm against [On-Time Delivery Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/on-time-delivery-rate) once the delivery scans land.

**What is the playbook when the rate crosses 3 percent?**
In order: (1) break the rate down by exception type, if it is fuzai, the action is checkout (time slots, PUDO), not the carrier; (2) if it is address-incomplete, check recent order-data quality and any address-validation change; (3) if it is customs-held, check [EMS CN23 Customs Document Generation Failure Rate](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/ems-cn23-customs-document-generation-failure-rate) and destination-rule changes; (4) if it is damaged / lost, escalate to [Open Claims](/nerve-centre/kpi-cards/japan-post/open-claims) and review packaging for the affected SKUs; (5) check the despatch calendar for an Oshogatsu (New Year) or Obon peak that explains a seasonal rise.

**Does the New Year (Oshogatsu) peak distort this card?**
Yes, expect a seasonal rise. Late December through the first week of January is the heaviest Japan Post period (New Year nengajo and gift traffic), and both volume and exception rate climb. Annotate year-on-year comparisons across that window rather than re-baselining the threshold permanently. The same applies to a lesser degree around the mid-August Obon period.
