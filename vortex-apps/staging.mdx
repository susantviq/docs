title: "Create and manage staging environments for your store"
sidebarTitle: "Staging"

A staging environment is a copy of your live store where you can test theme changes, app installs, price-list updates, and catalogue edits against production-shaped data before any of it touches real customers. Neither Shopify nor BigCommerce ships this capability natively. StagingPro closes the gap for BigCommerce, Vortex Staging closes it for Shopify, and DryRunPro brings on-demand dryrun staging to Adobe Commerce Cloud.

<Warning>
  Never use a staging environment for live customer traffic. Staging stores are for testing only. Do not store business-critical or development-essential data on a sandbox; items may need to be cleared between test migrations.
</Warning>

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="StagingPro (BigCommerce)">
    ## StagingPro

    StagingPro provisions cloud-hosted staging, UAT, and integration environments linked to your production BigCommerce store. On top of those environments it gives you four operational surfaces: bulk content migration, selective content migration, Git-driven code deployment, and a full history and rollback trail.

    ### What gets copied

    A bulk migration copies your entire catalogue from production into the sandbox in one operation:

    - Products (variants, options, modifiers, images, pricing, inventory, metafields, locale data)
    - Categories, brands, and promotions
    - Customers and orders
    - Themes, pages, layouts, and blog posts
    - B2B Edition entities (companies, addresses, users, quotes, invoices)

    Selective migration lets you move a single product, a specific promotion, a category, or a page template between environments for surgical updates and last-minute content syncs.

    ### Setting up StagingPro

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Install from the BigCommerce App Marketplace">
        Open the StagingPro listing at [bigcommerce.com/apps/stagingpro](https://www.bigcommerce.com/apps/stagingpro/), click **Install**, and grant the requested permissions. Choose your subscription tier on the pricing page that appears — each tier provisions a different number of environments alongside production. Your licence activates immediately.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Plan your environments">
        Before linking anything, decide what environments you need. The recommended naming convention:

        | Name | Purpose |
        |---|---|
        | Production | Your live store — reserved, not editable |
        | Staging | Pre-deployment store for smoke tests |
        | UAT | User acceptance testing for client sign-off |
        | Integration1 / Integration2 | System integration tests for third-party connectors |

        Note the store hash ID for each store you plan to link — the alphanumeric token in `https://store-a1b2c3d4e5.mybigcommerce.com/`. You need Store Owner access on every store you link.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Link your sandbox environments to production">
        StagingPro is pre-installed on your production store after install. To link each sandbox:

        1. On the sandbox store, open StagingPro, enter the production store hash, and click **Save**.
        2. A connection request appears on the production store. Switch to production, find the request, and click **Approve**.
        3. Name the environment and choose one of three options: just connect it, clear the destination catalogue, or run a full copy from production.
        4. Repeat for each additional sandbox.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Connect your Git repository">
        StagingPro uses Git as the deploy pipeline for theme code. Connect GitHub or Bitbucket via the settings page. Allow 3–5 minutes after connecting for StagingPro to auto-generate branches per environment and team member.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Configure notifications">
        Set up at least one notification channel before your first migration. StagingPro supports email (add addresses in Settings → Status Notifications), Microsoft Teams (paste a workflow webhook URL), and Slack (paste an incoming webhook URL).
      </Step>
      <Step title="Run your first test migration">
        1. From the StagingPro home page, open **Bulk Content Migration**.
        2. Select **Production** as the source and a sandbox as the destination.
        3. Pick a small entity set — a handful of brands, categories, and products — to validate the pipeline end to end.
        4. Click **Start the Migration** and watch progress on the **History and Rollback** tab.
        5. Confirm the migrated entities appear in the sandbox and that your notification channels received the completion alert.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    ### Testing changes

    Once your sandbox has production-shaped data, make your changes there — update a theme, install an app, edit a price list, restructure a category. StagingPro's History and Rollback tab maintains a full audit trail of every migration and deployment so you can see exactly what changed and when.

    ### Promoting to production

    For theme changes, push your branch through your Git repository. StagingPro generates a preview URL for approval and then deploys to the live store. For data changes, use selective migration to move the tested records from staging into production.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Vortex Staging (Shopify)">
    ## Vortex Staging

    Vortex Staging gives your Shopify store a real staging environment that mirrors production. Shopify Plus customers get up to nine development stores but no built-in mechanism to clone production into them, no scheduled sync, and no environment-promotion workflow. Vortex Staging fills all three gaps — on every Shopify plan from Basic upward.

    ### What gets copied

    Vortex Staging covers 20-plus entity types in a bulk migration:

    - Products, collections, customers, orders
    - Discount codes, gift cards, pages, blogs, files
    - Themes, navigation menus, URL redirects, store policies
    - Locations, delivery profiles, markets, catalogs, price lists
    - Metaobject definitions and metaobjects

    Customer PII is automatically anonymised before writing to non-production environments, so your QA team can work against production-scale volumes without seeing real personal data.

    ### Setting up Vortex Staging

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Install from the Shopify App Store">
        Install Vortex Staging on **each** Shopify store you intend to link — production and every sandbox. The app is at [apps.shopify.com/vortexiq-staging-1](https://apps.shopify.com/vortexiq-staging-1). Approve the API scopes when Shopify prompts (read and write access to products, collections, customers, orders, themes, files, locations, markets, delivery profiles, metaobjects, and store policies).
      </Step>
      <Step title="Choose your subscription tier">
        Three tiers are available:

        | Tier | Connection slots |
        |---|---|
        | Standard | Production plus 2 environments |
        | Plus | Production plus 3 environments |
        | Premium | Production plus 5 environments |

        You can upgrade later from Vortex Staging settings if your environment needs grow.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Link sandbox environments to production">
        1. On the production store, open Vortex Staging and copy the **production hash ID** shown on the home dashboard.
        2. On the sandbox store, enter the production hash ID and click **Save**. An invitation request appears.
        3. Switch back to production, refresh, and click **Approve** on the invitation.
        4. Enter an environment name (for example, Staging) and click **OK**.
        5. Repeat for each additional sandbox.

        Alternatively, share your store IDs with the Vortex IQ Helpdesk and they can complete the linking within 2–3 working days.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Connect GitHub for theme deployments (optional)">
        If you plan to deploy themes through Vortex Staging, link a GitHub repository under **Settings → Connect Repository**. Vortex Staging auto-creates branches per environment and per developer. This step is optional if you only need data migration.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Configure notifications">
        Set up Email, Slack, or Microsoft Teams notifications so the right people are alerted when migrations or theme deployments complete or fail. All three channels are configured in the Notifications section of settings.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Run a first test migration">
        From the Vortex Staging home, open **Bulk Content Migration**, select production as the source and a sandbox as the destination, pick a small entity set, and start the migration. Verify the migrated records appear in the sandbox, then check your notification channel for the completion alert.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    ### Testing changes

    With production-shaped data in your sandbox, test theme changes, app installs, bulk product edits, and content updates exactly as they will behave on the live store. Migration history logs every request ID, source, destination, start time, end time, status, and per-entity progress with downloadable error logs.

    ### Promoting to production

    For themes, use Vortex Staging's Theme Deployments tab to approve, deploy, and roll back theme changes. For data, selective migration moves the tested products, themes, or collections to production. If a theme deployment goes wrong, rollback restores the previous version.

    <Info>
      Bulk migration creates records in an empty destination — it does not update existing records. Use selective migration when you want to update records that already exist on the destination store.
    </Info>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="DryRunPro (Adobe Commerce)">
    ## DryRunPro

    DryRunPro is the Adobe Commerce Cloud staging launcher inside Vortex Apps. One DryRunPro account holds many Adobe Commerce Cloud projects and gives every project a clean, isolated, on-demand dryrun staging surface that mirrors the production Magento topology. You spin a dryrun, push a branch into it, run smoke tests and a SWAT report, and merge with confidence.

    ### How dryrun environments work

    Adobe Commerce Cloud ships with three environment tiers per project: integration, staging, and production. That ceiling becomes a bottleneck the moment you have multiple feature branches queued for QA at the same time. DryRunPro removes the bottleneck by giving every project an on-demand dryrun fleet.

    A dryrun is a fully isolated clone of your production environment, spun up against any branch you point at it. Each dryrun runs SWAT reports, extension audits, and code audits. When you are done, tear it down with one click and the resources return to the pool.

    ### Multi-project architecture

    One DryRunPro tenancy manages many Adobe Commerce Cloud projects at once. The Projects tab shows each project with two columns of metadata: the Adobe Commerce Cloud column (environments, storage, users) and the DryRun Pro column (staging environments, packages, users). A team assignment on each project card lets you route groups of users to the right projects without per-user access grants on every project.

    ### Running a dryrun

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Create a dryrun environment">
        From the **Dryrun Environments** tab, click the launcher, select the project, and point it at the branch you want to test. The environment typically spins up within a few minutes.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Run tests and audits">
        The dryrun generates a public Fastly-fronted URL your whole team can hit. Run smoke tests, review the SWAT report, check extension audits, and run bin/sync against the canonical environment to catch any drift.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Promote or discard">
        If the dryrun is green, promote the branch to the Adobe Commerce Cloud staging or production environment. If the dryrun reveals a problem, discard it, fix the branch, and spin a new dryrun. There is no impact on the live store.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Download environment packages">
        Completed dryruns produce downloadable artefacts available on the Packages tab until the dryrun is torn down. These let your development team replicate the environment locally if needed.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Note>
      DryRunPro requires an Adobe Commerce Cloud subscription with super-admin credentials. It does not work against Magento Open Source or on-premise Adobe Commerce installations.
    </Note>

    ### Packages and lifecycle

    DryRunPro dryruns are ephemeral by design — they are torn down when their job is done. The staging packages (Docker snapshot and Warden package) that each completed dryrun produces remain available for download until the dryrun is deleted. These let your development team replicate the environment locally.
