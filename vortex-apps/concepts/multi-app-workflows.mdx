Most merchant problems are not solved by a single Vortex App. They are solved by two or three apps working together, each contributing one piece of the workflow. This page documents the most common multi-app patterns merchants converge on, with the operational sequence and the why-it-works rationale for each.

## Why combine apps?

Each Vortex App has a focused responsibility:

- Staging apps preview a change before it goes live.
- Backup apps preserve the pre-change state in case the change is wrong.
- CloudHub orchestrates multichannel inventory and orders.
- App Builder turns descriptions into running automation.

A real ecommerce operation needs more than one of these at once. You preview the change (staging), back up before deploy (backup), trigger the deploy from a description (App Builder), and run the multichannel side of the business (CloudHub). The patterns below are the most common combinations.

## Pattern 1, StagingPro plus RollbackPro for safe deploy and recover &#123;#staging-plus-rollback&#125;

**The scenario.** A merchant runs a BigCommerce storefront and wants to test a new theme version, an app install, or a config change before customers see it. They also want a safety net in case something goes wrong post-deploy that staging did not catch.

**The apps.** [StagingPro](/vortex-apps/staging-pro) plus [RollbackPro](/vortex-apps/rollback-pro).

**The flow.**

1. **Trigger a backup before the change.** RollbackPro is already running continuously. Confirm the latest snapshot is fresh by checking the RollbackPro dashboard.
2. **Spin up StagingPro.** Clone the live BigCommerce store into a staging copy.
3. **Apply the change on staging.** Install the new app, change the theme, or update the config on the staging environment.
4. **Test on staging.** Walk the customer journey, place a test order, watch the [Nerve Centre Checkout Conversion Failure](/vortex-mind/reports/checkout-conversion-failure) report on staging.
5. **Promote to production.** Once staging passes review, promote the change to live.
6. **Watch production for a defined window.** Set a 24-hour vigilance period. Watch the same Nerve Centre KPIs on the live store.
7. **If something goes wrong, roll back.** Click rollback in RollbackPro to the snapshot from step 1. The store reverts to the pre-change state in minutes.

**Why it works.** Staging catches most issues. Backup catches the rest. The combination handles the case where staging review missed a problem that only surfaces under production traffic, real customer behaviour, or edge data.

**Equivalent pattern on Shopify.** [Vortex Staging](/vortex-apps/vortex-staging) plus [Vortex Backup](/vortex-apps/vortex-backup). Same flow, different platform.

**Equivalent pattern on Adobe Commerce.** [DryRunPro](/vortex-apps/dryrunpro) handles both staging and snapshot rollback in one app.

## Pattern 2, CloudHub plus Vortex Backup for multichannel with safety &#123;#cloudhub-plus-backup&#125;

**The scenario.** A merchant operates Amazon, eBay, and a Shopify storefront. They use [CloudHub](/vortex-apps/cloudhub) to manage the master SKU and multichannel inventory. They also need a safety net for the Shopify storefront because CloudHub-driven inventory edits can cascade into the Shopify catalogue.

**The apps.** [CloudHub](/vortex-apps/cloudhub) plus [Vortex Backup](/vortex-apps/vortex-backup).

**The flow.**

1. **CloudHub is the source of truth** for the master SKU. Inventory levels, pricing, and listings are managed in CloudHub and pushed to each marketplace and to Shopify.
2. **Vortex Backup** runs continuously on the Shopify storefront, capturing every change CloudHub triggers as well as any direct edits.
3. **A bulk inventory edit in CloudHub** propagates to Shopify within minutes. Vortex Backup creates a snapshot before and after.
4. **If the bulk edit was wrong** (a pricing typo, a wrong SKU mapping, a regional inventory mismatch), the merchant rolls back the Shopify storefront in Vortex Backup to the pre-edit snapshot.
5. **Reconcile in CloudHub.** Fix the master SKU in CloudHub, push the corrected version, verify with Vortex Backup that the Shopify state matches the new master.

**Why it works.** CloudHub is the orchestration brain across many channels. It is high-leverage but also high-risk because one wrong edit propagates everywhere. Vortex Backup is the storefront-level undo button. The pair gives the merchant the orchestration leverage of CloudHub with a per-channel safety net.

**Equivalent pattern with BigCommerce.** [CloudHub](/vortex-apps/cloudhub) plus [RollbackPro](/vortex-apps/rollback-pro). Same logic, BigCommerce-side safety net.

## Pattern 3, App Builder plus Ask Viq for conversational automation &#123;#app-builder-plus-ask-viq&#125;

**The scenario.** A merchant wants their automation to be smart, not just rule-based. They want an app that can answer questions about its own output, explain why it did what it did, and adapt to questions the merchant asks at run time.

**The apps.** [App Builder](/vortex-apps/app-builder) plus [Ask Viq](/ask-viq/overview).

**The flow.**

1. **Build an App Builder app** that includes Ask Viq as a tool. The app's plan registers Ask Viq among its toolkit.
2. **Schedule the app** as a daily or event-driven workflow.
3. **At run time, the app asks Ask Viq questions** as part of its reasoning. Examples:
   - "What was last week's revenue compared to the week before?"
   - "Which SKU drove the highest conversion lift?"
   - "Are there any outlier patterns in the refund data?"
4. **Ask Viq returns natural-language answers** with the supporting data. The app uses the answers in its output (Slack message, email, ticket).
5. **The merchant can also ask Ask Viq about the app's runs** retroactively. "Why did the Tuesday summary include SKU X but not SKU Y?" Ask Viq reads the run history from [Vortex Memory](/vortex-memory/overview) and explains.

**Why it works.** App Builder gives the workflow shape. Ask Viq gives the workflow intelligence. The combination is conversational automation: the app does the work, but the merchant can talk to it before, during, and after.

**Worked example.** A merchant builds an App Builder app called "Daily revenue digest". The app's logic includes a step "ask Ask Viq for the top driver of yesterday's revenue movement". Ask Viq reads the order data, the GA4 sessions, the ad spend, and answers "yesterday's revenue was up 12% versus the prior day, driven primarily by a 22% lift in returning-customer orders, traceable to the email campaign that went out at 09:00." The app pastes that explanation into the daily Slack post. The merchant gets not just the number but the why.

## Pattern 4, DryRunPro plus Vortex Memory for change history with audit trail &#123;#dryrunpro-plus-memory&#125;

**The scenario.** An Adobe Commerce agency manages multiple enterprise Magento Cloud projects. They need a complete audit trail of every dryrun, every code change, every SWAT report, every promote-to-production decision, with the rationale for each one.

**The apps.** [DryRunPro](/vortex-apps/dryrunpro) plus [Vortex Memory](/vortex-memory/overview).

**The flow.**

1. **DryRunPro spins up a dryrun** for a feature branch. The dryrun runs SWAT reports, code audits, and bin-sync against the canonical environment.
2. **Vortex Memory archives** the dryrun configuration, the SWAT report output, the code audit findings, and the bin-sync diff. Each artefact is timestamped and tagged with the project, the branch, and the engineer who initiated the dryrun.
3. **The team reviews the dryrun** in DryRunPro. They post comments, link tickets, attach evidence.
4. **The promote-to-production decision** is logged to Vortex Memory with the rationale, the approver, and the timestamp.
5. **Months later, a regression appears.** The team queries Vortex Memory: "show me all dryruns for project X in the last six months, with their SWAT scores and promote decisions." Vortex Memory returns the timeline. The team can correlate the regression with a specific change.

**Why it works.** DryRunPro is the operational surface for staging Adobe Commerce changes. Vortex Memory is the long-term memory of every change ever staged. The combination gives the agency a SOC2-grade audit trail without manual logging discipline.

## Pattern 5, App Builder plus Vortex Mind for finding-driven automation

**The scenario.** A merchant wants to react automatically to specific [Vortex Mind](/vortex-mind/overview) findings. When the Daily Revenue Leakage report flags a payment failure cluster, when the Customer Recovery Opportunity report identifies a high-value at-risk cohort, when the Paid Traffic Waste report flags a wasteful campaign, the merchant wants an immediate response routed to the right team without manual triage.

**The apps.** [App Builder](/vortex-apps/app-builder) plus [Vortex Mind](/vortex-mind/overview).

**The flow.**

1. **Build an App Builder app** with a Vortex Mind report as the trigger. Subscribe to a specific report (Daily Revenue Leakage, Customer Recovery, Paid Traffic Waste, etc.) and set a severity threshold.
2. **When the report runs and a finding crosses the threshold**, the App Builder app fires.
3. **The app reads the finding's evidence** (affected SKUs, customer cohorts, financial impact, root cause hypothesis).
4. **The app routes to the right destination.** A payment-failure cluster routes to the engineering Slack channel and opens a Jira ticket. A customer recovery opportunity routes to the marketing team's Klaviyo programme. A paid traffic waste finding routes to the marketing lead with a draft pause recommendation.
5. **Approval gates** ensure write actions (paying recovery campaigns, pausing ad spend) get human sign-off where appropriate.

**Why it works.** Vortex Mind diagnoses the problem. App Builder routes it to the right responder. The combination automates the triage layer that most merchants currently handle by sending the report to a shared inbox and waiting for someone to read it.

## Pattern 6, App Builder plus CloudHub for multichannel ops automation

**The scenario.** A multichannel operator using [CloudHub](/vortex-apps/cloudhub) needs daily and event-driven automation around the master SKU and the multichannel state.

**The apps.** [App Builder](/vortex-apps/app-builder) plus [CloudHub](/vortex-apps/cloudhub).

**Common workflows.**

- "Every morning, list SKUs out of stock on Amazon but in stock on Shopify, and email the inventory manager." App Builder reads CloudHub state, joins on SKU, filters on the cross-channel discrepancy.
- "When an order has been Pending for more than 6 hours in CloudHub, post an alert to #ops with the order details." App Builder subscribes to CloudHub order state, fires when the threshold trips.
- "Every Friday, summarise the top 10 SKUs by buy-box win rate across Amazon and post to #merchandising." App Builder reads the CloudHub Compete module, ranks, posts.
- "When CloudHub repricing rules adjust a price by more than 15%, draft a notification for the merchandiser to review." App Builder watches the repricing log, fires on outlier moves, drafts and routes for approval.

**Why it works.** CloudHub holds the multichannel operational state. App Builder turns the state into automation, alerts, and reports tailored to the merchant's specific organisation.

## Pattern 7, the full safety stack for a single storefront

**The scenario.** A merchant on Shopify or BigCommerce wants the maximum operational safety and automation surface for their single storefront.

**The apps.**

- **BigCommerce stack:** StagingPro + RollbackPro + App Builder.
- **Shopify stack:** Vortex Staging + Vortex Backup + App Builder.

**The flow.**

1. **Continuous backup** running in the background (RollbackPro / Vortex Backup).
2. **Staging used for every deploy** (StagingPro / Vortex Staging).
3. **App Builder running:**
   - Daily KPI digest (revenue, orders, conversion, top SKUs).
   - Vortex Mind report subscriptions (Checkout Conversion Failure, Daily Revenue Leakage, Customer Recovery Opportunity).
   - Threshold alerts (refund rate spike, ad spend overrun, stockout alert).
   - Custom team workflows (weekly merchandising review, monthly finance digest).

This stack delivers staging-before-deploy safety, post-deploy rollback safety, and proactive alerting and automation across the storefront. For most single-storefront merchants, this is the steady-state Vortex IQ deployment.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to set up multi-app workflows manually?**
Most are wired up automatically as you authenticate apps. The pairings on this page are operational patterns, not configuration steps. Once you have RollbackPro and StagingPro on the same workspace, both are running and ready; the merchant just uses them in the sequence the pattern describes.

**Can App Builder coordinate workflows across multiple Vortex Apps in one app?**
Yes. A single App Builder app can call StagingPro to spin up a staging environment, run a battery of test queries on the staging environment, post the results to Slack, and call RollbackPro to verify a backup snapshot exists before promotion. The orchestration lives in one app.

**Is there a runbook library for these patterns?**
The patterns on this page are the highest-level versions. Each app's own deep-dive section (under [Vortex Apps](/vortex-apps/overview)) has app-specific runbooks. App Builder's [Recipes library](/vortex-apps/app-builder/recipes-and-faqs) has dozens of ready-to-fork starter templates.

**Can I build my own multi-app pattern that is not on this list?**
Yes. The patterns on this page are the most common, not the only valid ones. Anything you can describe in plain English, App Builder can compose, including bespoke combinations of staging, backup, and multichannel apps tailored to your business.

**Does running multiple apps cost more than running one?**
Most plans bundle the apps relevant to your platform together. App-by-app overage usually only applies to App Builder run volume on very high usage. See your plan documentation for specifics, or talk to your account manager.

**How do I know if I am missing a multi-app pattern that would help my business?**
The Vortex IQ onboarding flow surfaces recommended pairings based on the connectors you have authenticated. As your usage grows, new pattern suggestions appear in the workspace home as "you might want to add X". The patterns on this page are the bedrock; the suggestions evolve based on your specific operation.
